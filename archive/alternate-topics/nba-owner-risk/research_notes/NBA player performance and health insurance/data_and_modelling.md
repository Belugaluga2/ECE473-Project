## Key Question 1: Which free or cheap data sources cover NBA injuries and availability?

### Takeaway
A student team can build a usable injury and availability panel for free from three things: Pro Sports Transactions (injury list moves, long history, used by nearly every public-data paper), box scores from NBA.com or Basketball Reference (to infer games missed), and the official NBA injury report PDFs (status and reason, machine-readable from 2021-22 via the `nbainjuries` package). The public injury data is noisy on dates (only about 45% of injury dates validated in one audit) but good on games missed (about 84%), so price on games missed rather than on injury dates.

### Cited Findings

Official NBA injury report
- The NBA hosts per-season injury report pages on official.nba.com, and the underlying reports are timestamped PDFs on `ak-static.cms.nba.com/referee/injury/Injury-Report_YYYY-MM-DD_HHPM.pdf` (for example a 30 Nov 2025 11PM file and 2026 files with 15-minute stamps such as `_12_30PM`). Each PDF is a table of game date, time, matchup, team, player, status and reason. (search summary plus URL patterns) — [NBA Injury Report 2025-26](https://official.nba.com/nba-injury-report-2025-26/); [example PDF](https://ak-static.cms.nba.com/referee/injury/Injury-Report_2025-11-30_11PM.pdf)
- The `nbainjuries` Python package parses those official PDFs into JSON or a pandas DataFrame with fields Game Date, Game Time, Matchup, Team, Player Name, Current Status, Reason. Historical data in this format is available from the 2021-22 season onward; preseason is not covered and some dates (All-Star break, parts of the postseason) have gaps. Snapshots are hourly or every 15 minutes. — [nbainjuries on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/nbainjuries/)
- `nbainjuries` needs Python 3.10+ and Java 8+ on PATH (it uses tabula-py). Licence is MIT, but the package asks users to respect the NBA's Terms of Use. Latest version is 1.1.1, released 10 Feb 2026 (1.0.0 was Aug 2025). — [nbainjuries on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/nbainjuries/)
- The NBA requires teams to report injuries, illnesses, other medical conditions and rest for all games. (search summary) — [NBA Injury Report 2025-26](https://official.nba.com/nba-injury-report-2025-26/)
- Statsurge published free CSVs of all official injury reports from Oct 2021 to June 2024 (one combined file and one per season for 2021-22, 2022-23, 2023-24). The page now states the database "is no longer being maintained". Fields, source and licence are not stated on the page. — [Statsurge: Downloadable NBA Injury Datasets](https://statsurge.substack.com/p/downloadable-nba-injury-datasets)

Pro Sports Transactions (PST)
- An NBER working paper (Sept 2026) describes PST as, to the authors' knowledge, "the most complete public online database of NBA player injuries in existence", extending back to the start of professional basketball, and including both injury-onset and return-to-active-roster dates. — [Krishna, Lychagin and McLean, NBER WP 35803](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w35803/w35803.pdf)
- The PST site says the project began in August 2004 with compilation of basketball transactions (that is when compilation started, not the start of coverage). (search summary) — [Pro Sports Transactions](https://prosportstransactions.com/)
- Data-quality audit: Bullock et al. validated 180 random PST-derived records against NBA.net, ESPN and team sites. Positive predictive value was 44.7% for injury date, 84.1% for games missed and 95.6% for injury site. The authors note injury dates are often recorded as the first missed game. — [Bullock et al. 2021, OJSM](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8207289)
- The NBER authors cross-checked PST against NBA.com box-score player status and added Fox Sports injury records; of 6,570 unique time-loss injury events (2017-18 to 2022-23), 6,161 were found in PST. 903 records could not be matched to a game because the injury likely happened in practice or a lower league. — [NBER WP 35803](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w35803/w35803.pdf)
- Morikawa et al. used PST as the primary source and verified against The Sports Network, Rotowire and Basketball Reference game logs; they list "reliance on a public database that may differ from official team medical records" as a limitation. — [Morikawa et al. 2022, OJSM](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9445480)

Kaggle and packaged research datasets
- Kaggle `ghopkins/nba-injuries-2010-2018` is scraped from PST. Descriptions differ: one says it covers 2010-11 through 2019-20; row counts are reported as 17,408 in one write-up and 27,105 in a Baselight mirror. (search summary) — [Baselight mirror of the Kaggle dataset](https://baselight.app/u/kaggle/dataset/ghopkins_nba_injuries_2010_2018)
- A Wharton student paper built its whole pipeline from five Kaggle datasets: the Hopkins injuries set (described there as ten seasons, 2010 to 2020), NBA Traditional Stats (team box scores), NBA players (biometrics), NBA Game Details (player box scores, Lauga 2020) and NBA Player Stats and Salaries 2010-2025 (Ratin21, 2025). — [Wang, Using Injury-Risk Forecasting to Quantify Financial Impact in the NBA](https://wsb.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/WANG_Using-Injury-Risk-Forecasting-to-Quantify-Financial-Impact-in-the-NBA-1.pdf)
- No Kaggle dataset covering 1951 to 2023 was found. (search summary) — [Baselight mirror](https://baselight.app/u/kaggle/dataset/ghopkins_nba_injuries_2010_2018)
- An R data package `tylerferguson/NBAinjuries` (Ferguson is a co-author of Bullock et al.) exposes an `injuries` dataset, and the R package `josedv82/airball` has an `nba_injuries` function for the PST injury list. I saw only the index listings, not the contents. (search summary) — [NBAinjuries on rdrr.io](https://rdrr.io/github/tylerferguson/NBAinjuries/man/injuries.html); [airball nba_injuries](https://rdrr.io/github/josedv82/airball/man/nba_injuries.html)

Box scores and availability (NBA.com, `nba_api`, Basketball Reference)
- The NBER paper scraped NBA.com box scores for appearances and minutes for every official game 2017-18 to 2022-23, and used box-score player status (active, inactive, out due to injury) to validate injury durations: 1,126 players, 7,581 games, 251,037 player-game observations. All data were public (NBA.com, PST, FoxSports.com, ESPN.com). — [NBER WP 35803](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w35803/w35803.pdf)
- Bullock et al. scraped PST, basketball-reference.com and stats.nba.com in R for 2008-09 to 2018-19 (1,369 players, 302,018 player-games). — [Bullock et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8207289)
- stats.nba.com blocks cloud-hosted IPs: a `swar/nba_api` issue reports the NBA blocks cloud hosting providers (including Google Colab in some cases); community reports also describe Akamai bot protection and undocumented rate limits returning HTTP 429 or empty responses. These are community reports, not official statements, and none is dated 2026. (search summary) — [swar/nba_api issue #176](https://github.com/swar/nba_api/issues/176)
- Sports Reference (which runs Basketball Reference) blocks sessions sending more than 20 requests per minute (10 per minute for FBref and Stathead); a violating session is "in jail for up to a day". The page says third-party data agreements mean it cannot offer data downloads, so there is no API. — [Sports Reference bot traffic policy](https://www.sports-reference.com/bot-traffic.html)

Spotrac and other trackers
- Spotrac has an NBA injured-list tracker that reports cash paid to injured players by team, position and injury type, with season views (a 2024 URL exists). It counts days missed rather than games. A BetMGM article using it reported the Pacers lost $79,359,136 in cash to injuries in 2025-26. (search summary) — [Spotrac NBA Injured](https://spotrac.com/nba/injured/_/year/2024/view/reason); [BetMGM: Most Injured Teams 2025-26](https://sports.betmgm.com/en/blog/nba/most-injured-teams-in-nba-bm23/)
- Daily injury status pages (no history, no games-missed totals) exist at ESPN, CBS Sports and Action Network; Kalshi also now hosts an NBA injury-report page. (search summary) — [ESPN NBA injuries](https://www.espn.co.uk/nba/injuries); [Kalshi NBA injury report](https://kalshi.com/nba/injury-report)

Paid or proprietary context
- Teams hold far richer private data than any public source: in practice players wear GPS and accelerometer units, staff collect daily soreness, fatigue and sleep reports and use wearables, and each team has a medical and performance department often of ten or more people. — [NBER WP 35803](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w35803/w35803.pdf)
- The league's own injury surveillance analysis is run with IQVIA Injury Surveillance & Analytics (Dr Christina Mack); that data is not public. — [NBA.com: NBA sends data from load management study](https://www.nba.com/news/nba-sends-data-load-management-study)
- P3 (a private biomechanics firm) advertises an NBA non-contact knee injury model with AUC 0.75 and sensitivity above 0.6; this is a company claim, not peer-reviewed. (search summary) — [P3 injury risk modeling](https://p3.md/expertise/athlete-assessment/injury-risk-modeling)

### Inferences
- The most defensible free pipeline in late 2026 is the one the NBER paper used: PST for injury events, NBA.com or Basketball Reference box scores for games missed, cross-checked against each other. The official PDF archive only adds value from 2021-22 onward (status and reason, including "rest"), so it is a supplement, not the backbone.
- Because PST dates are unreliable (PPV about 45%) but games missed are reliable (PPV about 84%), an insurance payoff defined on games missed (or games played, which is exactly observable from box scores) is much easier to settle and model than one defined on injury type or date.
- Run `nba_api` from a laptop or home IP, cache everything locally, and throttle. Do not plan on running scrapers from Colab or cloud VMs.
- Basketball Reference scraping at under 20 requests per minute is workable for a season-level panel (a few thousand player-season pages takes hours, not days) but not for full game logs of every player unless cached once.
- Prefer scraping PST directly (or the Kaggle Hopkins set for a quick start) over Statsurge, which is frozen at June 2024.

### Gaps
- Pro Sports Transactions: I did not fetch its terms of use, robots rules or rate limits, so licence and scraping limits are unverified.
- In Street Clothes (Jeff Stotts) and Man-Games Lost: searches returned nothing on their current status, coverage or access in 2026. I found no reliable source saying whether either still publishes NBA data.
- Spotrac: I did not confirm which seasons the injury tracker covers, nor whether historical seasons need a premium account.
- Official injury report: I could not confirm whether the per-season pages on official.nba.com link to every historical PDF before 2021-22; the `nbainjuries` author says only that the current format has existed since 2021-22.
- `nba_api`: endpoint list, fields and current maintenance status were not verified this session (background knowledge: it wraps stats.nba.com endpoints such as player game logs and box scores, and is free and MIT-licensed, but I did not re-check this).
- Second Spectrum, Kinexon and Sportradar: not researched beyond the NBER description of team-side tracking; no pricing or access terms found.
- NBA.com Terms of Use wording on automated access was not read.

## Key Question 2: Which sources cover salaries, contracts and player value metrics, and which are public versus paywalled?

### Takeaway
Free historical value metrics that are confirmed available are the FiveThirtyEight RAPTOR archive on GitHub (1977 onward, frozen) and Neil Paine's consensus plus/minus sheet (1977 to 2023); EPM exists daily from 2001-02 but sits behind a subscription and paid API. I could not verify current access terms for DARKO, LEBRON, Spotrac or HoopsHype this session.

### Cited Findings
- FiveThirtyEight's `nba-raptor` folder on GitHub was still reachable on 11 Nov 2026 and holds four CSVs: `modern_RAPTOR_by_player.csv`, `modern_RAPTOR_by_team.csv` (seasons since 2014, tracking era), `historical_RAPTOR_by_player.csv`, `historical_RAPTOR_by_team.csv` (since the 1976 merger; full tracking from 2014, box plus regularised plus-minus blend 2001 to 2013, box-only 1977 to 2000). 23 columns include Basketball Reference player ID, season, possessions, minutes, RAPTOR offence, defence and total, WAR (total, regular season, playoffs) and PREDATOR. No licence is stated on the folder page. — [fivethirtyeight/data nba-raptor](https://github.com/fivethirtyeight/data/tree/master/nba-raptor)
- EPM (Dunks and Threes) is a statistical plus-minus prior fed into a RAPM calculation. Ratings and "Estimated Skills" exist for every day since 2001-02 (daily runs through 2025-26). The site has Subscribe and Login links and states "The same data is available through the API with an API subscription"; API version 1 remains available through 2026-27. Prices are not on the methodology page. — [Dunks and Threes: About EPM](https://dunksandthrees.com/about/epm)
- Neil Paine publishes "NBA Consensus Plus/Minus (1977-2023)" as a Google Sheet. It blends PER (10%), WS/48 (10%), BPM (20%), "Basketball on Paper" plus-minus (10%), on-court plus-minus (15%), on-off plus-minus (15%) and RAPTOR game score (20%), with a WAR figure. The page does not state licence or price. — [Neil Paine: NBA Consensus Plus/Minus Data](https://neilpaine.substack.com/p/nba-consensus-plusminus-data)
- A Kaggle dataset "NBA Player Stats and Salaries 2010-2025" (Ratin21, 2025) combines box-score stats and player salary and was used for salary-weighted injury cost. In that study 9,551 player-game observations lacked salary. — [Wang, Wharton](https://wsb.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/WANG_Using-Injury-Risk-Forecasting-to-Quantify-Financial-Impact-in-the-NBA-1.pdf)
- The NBER paper matched salaries to players and used within-season salary deciles as a control; a few established players lacked a matched salary in some seasons (for example LeBron James in 2017-18). — [NBER WP 35803](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w35803/w35803.pdf)
- Spotrac exposes per-player cash earned while injured, which implies per-player contract cash figures by season. (search summary) — [Spotrac NBA Injured](https://spotrac.com/nba/injured/_/year/2024/view/reason)
- Sports Reference states it cannot offer data downloads or an API because of third-party data agreements; scraping is limited to 20 requests per minute. — [Sports Reference bot traffic policy](https://www.sports-reference.com/bot-traffic.html)
- DARKO is described in a Wharton research paper as a box-score-based projection system that also produces plus-minus projections, and as the most predictive public all-in-one metric according to its creator (a self-claim). (search summary) — [Brill 2023, Wharton](https://wsb.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/Brill_2023_Q.pdf)
- BBall Index describes PIPM as luck-adjusted plus-minus combined with box-score value. (search summary) — [BBall Index: PIPM](https://www.bball-index.com/player-impact-plus-minus/)

### Inferences
- For a no-budget project the practical value-metric stack is: BPM, VORP and Win Shares scraped from Basketball Reference (current and historical), RAPTOR WAR from the GitHub archive for 1977 to roughly 2022-23 as a second opinion and for back-testing, and optionally Paine's consensus sheet. EPM is the best-regarded current metric but should be treated as paywalled.
- RAPTOR's `player_id` is the Basketball Reference ID, which makes joining RAPTOR to Basketball Reference salary, age and games-played tables straightforward.
- For pricing a "performance shortfall" contract, the settlement metric must be publicly reproducible. BPM or Win Shares (public formulae, public host) are safer settlement references than EPM, LEBRON or DARKO, whose values can be revised and whose access can change.

### Gaps
- FiveThirtyEight: I did not verify in this session when the site shut down or the last season in the RAPTOR files; the README's "latest" CSV links are hosted off-repo and may be dead.
- EPM: subscription and API prices, and whether any historical seasons are free, were not found.
- DARKO: whether the app and its downloadable CSV are still free in 2026 was not verified (search did not return the site).
- LEBRON (BBall Index): no source found this session on methodology, archive or paywall status.
- Spotrac and HoopsHype salary pages: coverage years and paywall status not verified. Basketball Reference contract pages likewise not checked.
- RAPM: no verified public multi-year RAPM download was located this session (an APBR thread on "26-year RAPM" appeared in search but was not read): [APBR thread](https://apbr.org/metrics/viewtopic.php?p=38998).

## Key Question 3: What are the empirical base rates for games missed and major injuries?

### Takeaway
Public-data studies put NBA time-loss injury incidence at roughly 15 to 19 per 1,000 player-games, with a very right-skewed severity: the median injury costs 2 to 3 games, the mean about 6, and about 15% cost 14 or more. Star absences have more than doubled since the 1990s (10.6 to 23.9 games per season), and Achilles ruptures and ACL tears carry return rates of about 72 to 89% with year-long absences and measurable production loss.

### Cited Findings

Incidence and severity
- 2008-09 to 2018-19 (1,369 players, 302,018 player-games, 5,375 injuries and illnesses): 17.80 injuries and illnesses per 1,000 athlete game-exposures; 15.60 for injuries alone. By site: ankle 2.57, knee 2.44, groin/hip/thigh 1.99, illness 1.85, trunk/back 1.55, concussion 0.31. 66% of players sustained at least one injury over the period. — [Bullock et al. 2021, OJSM](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8207289)
- Severity in the same study: median 3 games missed per injury (IQR 0 to 6). Slight (1 game) 33%, minor (2 to 3) 26%, moderate (4 to 13) 26%, severe (14 or more) 15%. Knee injuries had the highest severe rate (0.72 per 1,000). — [Bullock et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8207289)
- 2017-18 to 2022-23 (1,126 players, 7,581 games, 251,037 player-game observations): 6,570 unique time-loss injury events. Injury duration is heavily right-skewed: median 6 days and mean 18.8 days; in games, median 2 and mean 6.2; maximum 337 days. — [NBER WP 35803](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w35803/w35803.pdf)
- Same sample, decomposition of the 90,422 player-games not played: 44,734 coach decision (including 1,125 explicitly "rest"), 40,444 injury, 4,237 illness (of which 2,413 were COVID protocols), 1,007 other (personal, suspension). — [NBER WP 35803](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w35803/w35803.pdf)
- 1988-89 to 2004-05 trainer-reported data (1,094 players, 3,843 player-seasons): 19.1 injuries per 1,000 athlete exposures and 59,179 games missed. Lateral ankle sprains were most frequent (1,658; 13.2%); patellofemoral inflammation caused the most games missed (10,370; 17.5%). Player demographics were not correlated with injury rates. This definition includes injuries needing physician referral or emergency care even without a missed game. (search summary) — [Drakos et al. 2010, Sports Health](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3445097)
- A per-player-game injury rate of 0.03 (SD 0.17) appears in a merged Kaggle panel for 2010 to 2022 (about 141,000 rows before cleaning). The author notes that a model predicting "no injury" always would be about 98% accurate. — [Wang, Wharton](https://wsb.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/WANG_Using-Injury-Risk-Forecasting-to-Quantify-Financial-Impact-in-the-NBA-1.pdf)

By position, age, minutes, season timing
- By position (2008-19): guards 18.17, forwards 17.23, centres 18.16 per 1,000; no meaningful difference in incidence or severity. Age and minutes were not analysed as risk factors in that study. — [Bullock et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8207289)
- Monthly incidence rises through the season, from 5.40 (October) to 27.61 (April) per 1,000, with a dip in February (14.64). — [Bullock et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8207289)
- Season-level incidence ranged from 13.54 (2008-09) to 21.68 (lockout-shortened 2011-12), when severe injuries roughly doubled; other seasons were largely stable. — [Bullock et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8207289)
- Condensed 2020-21 season versus 2015-18 (4,346 injuries, 2,572 player-seasons, 136,457 game-exposures): injury proportion ratio 1.42 (95% CI 1.32 to 1.52) excluding COVID. Biggest rise in one-game injuries (1.53). Significant increases at ages 20 to 24 (1.26) and 25 to 29 (1.57), at 20.0 to 29.9 minutes per game (1.56) and 30+ minutes (1.67), and for point guards (1.67). Under-20s, 30+ year-olds and sub-20-minute players showed no significant change. — [Morikawa et al. 2022, OJSM](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9445480)
- The NBA's independent 10-year study identified previous injury history, previous surgery and age as injury risk factors. — [NBA.com load management study](https://www.nba.com/news/nba-sends-data-load-management-study)

Star players, load management and the 65-game rule
- Average games missed per season by players defined as stars: 10.6 in the 1990s versus 23.9 in the 2020s (57-page league-commissioned report by DiFiori, Mack and Herzog). — [NBA.com load management study](https://www.nba.com/news/nba-sends-data-load-management-study)
- The same study "did not see a lower injury rate when players rested"; the authors add that the report does not claim load management does not work. — [NBA.com load management study](https://www.nba.com/news/nba-sends-data-load-management-study)
- The NBER paper disputes the league's reading: after instrumenting, work causally raises injury risk, and injury risk is higher after a coach rests an available player than after an illness or personal absence, which the authors interpret as coaches resting players they privately know to be at risk. — [NBER WP 35803](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w35803/w35803.pdf)
- Since 2023-24 the Player Participation Policy fines teams for resting healthy stars; teams have paid $100,000 fines while citing injury risk. — [NBER WP 35803](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w35803/w35803.pdf)
- The 65-game rule (from 2023-24) requires 65 regular-season games for major award eligibility. In 2025-26 Anthony Edwards was ruled ineligible and the NBPA publicly called for the rule to be abolished or reformed (March 2026). (search summary) — [CBS Sports on Edwards](https://www.cbssports.com/nba/news/anthony-edwards-nba-65-game-rule-ineligible); [HoopsRumors on the NBPA statement](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2026/03/nbpa-calls-for-65-game-rule-to-be-abolished-or-reformed.html)
- Commentary reports that in 2024-25 three of the top ten scorers (Doncic, Durant, Maxey) fell short of 65 games, and that by mid-November 2025 recent All-Stars and All-NBA players had already missed over 200 games, nearly double the pace two years earlier. (search summary; outlet-to-claim mapping unverified) — [Deseret News](https://www.deseret.com/sports/2026/03/06/nba-65-game-rule-misses-mark/); [The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/2026/04/06/nba/nba-65-game-rule-mvp-awards-luka-doncic-cade-cunningham-injury)

Major injuries
- Achilles rupture, 1996-97 to 2016-17 (25 surgically treated players): 80% returned to play; mean recovery 311.0 ± 100.9 days; two seasons after injury VORP was 24.1% below pre-injury, with significant declines in BPM and offensive rating versus controls and no significant change in defensive rating (P = 0.38). (search summary) — [Chauhan et al. 2021, Phys Sportsmed](https://www.pubmed.gov/34010095/)
- Achilles rupture, 1970 to 2019 (47 players): 34 (72.3%) returned to the NBA after surgery; injured players' careers averaged 3.1 seasons versus 5.8 for matched controls; game utilisation and performance were below the player's own baseline at all time points, but versus controls only utilisation remained lower at three years. (search summary) — [PMC8436311](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8436311)
- Achilles (18 players, AAOS 2013 presentation, not peer-reviewed): 39% never returned and fewer than half played more than one further season. (search summary) — [LER Magazine](https://lermagazine.com/?p=16523)
- ACL reconstruction (58 NBA players): 86% returned to the NBA and 12% to FIBA or the D-League; performance fell from pre-injury but not significantly more than controls. (search summary) — [Harris et al. 2013, Sports Health](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3806178)
- ACL (26 athletes, OJSM 2021): 84% returned at a mean 372.5 days after surgery; PER in the first season back was 19.3% below pre-injury; the second season was not significantly different. Younger, less experienced players returned at higher rates. (search summary) — [PubMed 33738307](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33738307/)
- ACL (surgeon case series and review, 2017): 89% return to NBA play; PER fell from 12.5 pre-injury to 7.6 in the first season back and approximated pre-injury by season two. (search summary; URL mapping unverified) — [PMC4887878](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4887878)

### Inferences
- Rough per-season exposure from the NBER counts: 40,444 injury games over six seasons and 30 teams is about 225 injury man-games per team-season, or about 16% of all rostered player-game slots (40,444 / 251,037). COVID seasons are inside that window, so treat it as an upper-leaning estimate.
- From Drakos: 59,179 games missed over 3,843 player-seasons is about 15 games missed per player-season on average, on a broader trainer-reported base.
- The NBER event rate (6,570 events over 160,615 fielded player-games, about 41 per 1,000) is far higher than Bullock's 15.6 per 1,000. The gap likely reflects fuller capture (PST plus Fox Sports plus box-score status), inclusion of practice injuries, and the later era. A team should compute its own rate rather than import either number.
- Severity looks like a mixture: a large mass at 1 to 3 games (about 59%) and a heavy tail (15% at 14 or more, maximum near a full year). A single Poisson or normal model on games missed per season will fit poorly; a frequency model times a heavy-tailed severity model, or a hurdle model, matches these facts.
- For a contract on a star's games missed, the 1990s-to-2020s shift (10.6 to 23.9) means pre-2015 data understates current expected loss; training windows should weight recent seasons.
- A season-ending-injury rider can be sized from the major-injury studies: roughly 20 to 28% of Achilles cases and 11 to 16% of ACL cases never return to the NBA, absences average 10 to 12 months, and first-season-back production is about 20% lower.

### Gaps
- No source was found giving the full distribution of games missed per player-season (for example the share of player-seasons with 0, 1 to 10, 11 to 30, 30+ games missed), nor that distribution split by age, minutes load or prior-injury history. This must be computed from the team's own panel.
- No source was found on the annual frequency of season-ending injuries.
- No sources were found this session for NBA stress fractures (for example navicular, fifth metatarsal) or back surgery (lumbar discectomy) return-to-play rates.
- Annual counts of Achilles ruptures and ACL tears per season (incidence, rather than outcomes) were not found; the Achilles cohorts imply roughly one to two per season historically (25 in 21 seasons), but recent seasons were not checked.
- A search summary quoted league-wide totals of 7,625 games missed in 2024-25 and 13,674 "projected" for 2025-26 from a Substack; I could not verify the source or method and consider the numbers unreliable: [Above the Break](https://abovethebreak.substack.com/p/the-nba-is-in-the-red-zone).
- Quantified effect of the 65-game rule on games played by stars (before versus after 2023-24) was not found in any study; only commentary exists.

## Key Question 4: What does the literature say about predicting NBA injuries?

### Takeaway
Prior injury history and age are the only features that show up consistently; workload and schedule effects are real but small per game and badly confounded, because coaches rest the players they privately know to be at risk. Published discrimination ranges from about 0.73 to 0.84 AUC on public data, results above 0.9 are regarded as suspect, calibration is rarely reported, and next-game injury (base rate 2 to 4%) is predicted only modestly better than chance.

### Cited Findings

Predictive accuracy
- Lu et al. 2022 (case-control, NBA 1999 to 2019, lower-extremity muscle strains causing time loss, identified from injury-list transactions): XGBoost had the best internal-validation AUC, 0.840, and beat logistic regression; elastic net, random forest and a neural network were also tried. Calibration and decision-curve analysis were assessed but numbers were not in the abstract. (search summary) — [Lu et al. 2022, OJSM](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9340342)
- Important predictors in Lu et al.: number of previous lower-extremity injuries; age; recent ankle, hamstring or groin injury; recent concussion; three-point attempt rate; free-throw attempt rate. (search summary) — [Lu et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9340342)
- Cohan, Schuster and Fernandez 2021 (METIC, Journal of Sports Analytics): a deep-learning model on injury history and game activity logs; reported 93.4% accuracy and ROC AUC 0.80, while noting a constant "no injury" prediction would reach about 98% accuracy. Figures are as relayed in Wang's literature review; the journal page returned HTTP 403. — [Wang, Wharton](https://wsb.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/WANG_Using-Injury-Risk-Forecasting-to-Quantify-Financial-Impact-in-the-NBA-1.pdf); [JSA paper page](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3233/JSA-200529)
- Wang (Wharton, posted Dec 2025, not peer-reviewed): random forest on 16 workload and recency features from Kaggle data 2010 to 2022, five-fold cross-validation, next-game injury target. At a 2% probability threshold: 69% sensitivity and 62 to 63% specificity out of sample. Workload shifts and rest were the top features. — [Wang, Wharton](https://wsb.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/WANG_Using-Injury-Risk-Forecasting-to-Quantify-Financial-Impact-in-the-NBA-1.pdf)
- Weaknesses visible in Wang: the "AUC" is reported as about 8.087 (an un-normalised sum of true-positive rates, so not comparable to other papers); 16% of rows (22,631 of 140,879) had missing box-score features; mean imputation was used; the third most frequent "injury" type in the data was problematic to classify; illness was not modelled. — [Wang, Wharton](https://wsb.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/WANG_Using-Injury-Risk-Forecasting-to-Quantify-Financial-Impact-in-the-NBA-1.pdf)
- A University of Rochester student project reports logistic regression AUC of about 0.73 on 150 players (not peer-reviewed). (search summary) — [McClung, Rochester ECE408 report](https://hajim.rochester.edu/ece/sites/zduan/teaching/ece408/projects/2025/MaxMcClung_NBAInjuryRiskAndAvailabilityAnalysis_FinalReport.pdf)
- A 2025 scoping review of machine-learning injury prediction in sport (38 studies, reported as British Journal of Sports Medicine) found AUC the most common metric and noted three studies with AUC above 0.9 whose clinical relevance is questionable. (search summary; URL mapping unverified) — [University of Pretoria repository copy](https://repository.up.ac.za/bitstreams/b8f6cb2e-829b-4418-a8f9-f01c5a3901ce/download)
- The METIC authors caution that headline results such as 0.97 ROC AUC (Vallance et al. 2020, a football study) are misleading because generalisability is unknown. (search summary) — [JSA paper](https://content.iospress.com:443/articles/journal-of-sports-analytics/jsa200529)

Features and causal evidence
- NBER WP 35803 (Sept 2026; 2017-18 to 2022-23; 251,037 player-games; probit with control function, instrumenting minutes with exogenous teammate absences): naive models show a negative association between minutes and injury (the pattern "usually reported"), but the instrumented effect is positive and significant. One additional assigned minute raises a star's injury probability by roughly 0.1 percentage point; moving from the 25th to 75th percentile of assigned minutes (about 15 minutes) raises injury probability by 0.0182 for top-salary-decile players; a planned 48-minute game implies an injury probability of 0.075. — [NBER WP 35803](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w35803/w35803.pdf)
- Same paper: once current assigned minutes and a recent-absence proxy are included, there is no detectable linear or U-shaped relationship between injury and chronic workload over one to four weeks. In the naive model a one-hour increase in weekly chronic workload was associated with only a 0.00423 increase in injury probability. — [NBER WP 35803](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w35803/w35803.pdf)
- Same paper on method reliability: sports-medicine workload studies rest on realised exposure, which is truncated by the injury itself, and on workloads chosen by coaches who select on hidden risk; a reanalysis (Impellizzeri et al. 2021) showed the acute-to-chronic workload ratio's construction can manufacture associations; one review says "no studies have even tried to estimate causal effects properly". — [NBER WP 35803](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w35803/w35803.pdf)
- A cohort study of over 70,000 NBA games and 1,600 injury events is reported as finding injury odds up 2.87% per 96 minutes played and down 15.96% per day of rest. (search summary; likely Lewis 2018, URL mapping unverified) — [PMC6162549](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6162549/)
- Teramoto et al. (in-game injuries 2012 to 2015; 681 injuries, 280 players): back-to-backs alone were not associated with more in-game injuries, but road back-to-backs were, at about 3.5 times the rate. Reported via secondary summaries, no odds ratio seen. (search summary) — [WHOOP summary](https://www.whoop.com/us/en/thelocker/missing-games-to-rest-a-smart-move-in-the-nba/); [ESPN feature](https://www.espn.com/nba/story/_/id/14757066/nba-schedule-breaking-players)
- A study of 1,443 NBA players (2012 to 2015) found 54% of regular-season injuries occurred in away games versus 50% expected. (search summary; URL mapping unverified) — [PMC7925613](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7925613)
- Charest et al. 2021 studied distance and direction of travel on back-to-backs and found specific travel patterns worsen recovery and performance (as relayed by Wang). — [Wang, Wharton](https://wsb.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/WANG_Using-Injury-Risk-Forecasting-to-Quantify-Financial-Impact-in-the-NBA-1.pdf)
- Schedule compression matters at league level: lockout 2011-12 incidence 21.68 versus 13.54 to about 18 in other seasons, and 2020-21 injury ratio 1.42 versus 2015-18; teams averaged 15.2 back-to-backs (21% of games) in 2020-21 versus 13.3 (16%) in 2018. — [Bullock et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8207289); [Morikawa et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9445480)
- Body size and demographics: Drakos found player demographics (age, height, weight, experience) were not correlated with injury rates; Bullock found no meaningful position difference. The league study, by contrast, lists age, prior injury and prior surgery as risk factors. — [Drakos et al. 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3445097); [Bullock et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8207289); contradicted on age by [NBA.com load management study](https://www.nba.com/news/nba-sends-data-load-management-study)
- A systematic review (Chan et al. 2024, 11 studies) found training load correlated with injury risk (as relayed by Wang). — [Wang, Wharton](https://wsb.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/WANG_Using-Injury-Risk-Forecasting-to-Quantify-Financial-Impact-in-the-NBA-1.pdf)

### Inferences
- For season-level pricing (the contract horizon), the usable signal is mostly slow-moving: prior injury count and recency, age, and expected minutes. Game-level schedule features (rest days, road back-to-backs) matter for next-game hazard but largely average out across an 82-game season, since every team plays a similar number of back-to-backs.
- Published AUCs are not comparable with each other: Lu et al. used a case-control design on one injury class with internal validation only; METIC and Wang predict next-game injury at 2 to 3% prevalence where accuracy is meaningless. None of the papers I saw reports out-of-time validation (train on early seasons, test on later ones) with calibration. A student team that does a clean season-forward split with a Brier score and reliability plot would be doing better validation than most of the literature.
- Leakage risks specific to this data: PST dates are often the first missed game, so "minutes in last game" features can include the injury game itself; minutes fall on the injury night (truncation), which makes low workload look predictive; and rest or DNP flags encode the coach's private information. Random row-level cross-validation across player-games leaks player identity and season.
- The NBER result implies adverse selection for an insurer: teams and players know more about current injury risk than any public model can, and they can act on it (resting, minutes limits). A contract sold shortly before or during a season to the informed party would be mispriced by a public-data model.

### Gaps
- I did not read the full text of Lu et al., METIC or the scoping review, so calibration statistics (Brier score, calibration slope) are unreported here. I found no NBA paper whose abstract reports a Brier score.
- No peer-reviewed NBA study using Second Spectrum or wearable tracking workload for injury prediction was found in this session.
- No NBA-specific study quantifying height or weight as an injury risk factor with an effect size was found (beyond Drakos's null result).
- The Teramoto and Lewis papers were not fetched; effect sizes above come from secondary summaries and may be imprecise.
- arXiv 2112.14649 surfaced in the back-to-back search but was not read: [arXiv 2112.14649](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2112.14649).

## Key Question 5: What methods fit pricing, and how do public projection systems forecast games played and performance?

### Takeaway
No published paper prices an NBA injury contract with actuarial methods; the closest work is an expected-cost calculation (injury probability times salary) and a game-level probit hazard. The base-rate facts (rare events per game, heavy-tailed severity, strong player heterogeneity, league-wide shock seasons) point to a frequency-severity or hurdle model with player random effects, estimated on a player-season panel and priced by simulation.

### Cited Findings
- Expected-cost approach: Wang converts per-game injury probabilities into salary-weighted expected costs. Aggregate expected injury-related cost rose from $53.1M in 2012 to $471.1M in 2022; predicted and realised team-season costs correlated at r = 0.955; a rest-the-high-risk-player policy was estimated to save about $5.7M in 2021. — [Wang, Wharton](https://wsb.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/WANG_Using-Injury-Risk-Forecasting-to-Quantify-Financial-Impact-in-the-NBA-1.pdf)
- Game-level binary hazard: the NBER paper models Pr(time-loss injury in game g) by probit with salary-decile and season fixed effects, clustered errors and a control function for endogenous minutes; a linear probability version adds player fixed effects. — [NBER WP 35803](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w35803/w35803.pdf)
- Severity inputs for a frequency-severity model: games missed per injury has median 2 to 3, mean 6.2, 15% at 14 or more, maximum 337 days. — [NBER WP 35803](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w35803/w35803.pdf); [Bullock et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8207289)
- Hazard is not constant in time: incidence rises from 5.40 per 1,000 in October to 27.61 in April. — [Bullock et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8207289)
- Non-injury absences are as large as injury absences: 44,734 coach-decision missed games versus 40,444 injury games in 2017-23, plus 4,237 illness. — [NBER WP 35803](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w35803/w35803.pdf)
- EPM's "Estimated Skills" are per-stat predictive models with exponential-decay weighting of past games; they beat a player's season-to-date average, with minutes prediction improving about 14.5% and three-point percentage about 3.5%. EPM-based game predictions miss by an average of 11.0 points (versus 11.4 for an opponent-adjusted net rating baseline) in live 2024-25 to 2025-26 use, and 9.6 versus 9.9 in a 2005-06 to 2023-24 backtest. — [Dunks and Threes: About EPM](https://dunksandthrees.com/about/epm)
- RAPTOR ships a separate predictive variant (PREDATOR) alongside descriptive RAPTOR and WAR. — [fivethirtyeight/data nba-raptor](https://github.com/fivethirtyeight/data/tree/master/nba-raptor)
- Simple projection baselines used by practitioners: a 4-2-1 weighted average of the last three seasons (each season twice as important as the one before) plus an age adjustment, and empirical age curves built from year-over-year changes with larger gains at young ages and larger declines later. (search summary) — [NBA Insights Substack](https://nbainsights.substack.com/i/186624170/current-ability-ca); [APBR thread on RAPM ageing](https://apbr.org/metrics/viewtopic.php?p=38998)
- Age-curve estimation: imputation-based methods and models that account for individual player skill give lower RMSE and more accurate curve shapes, because naive curves suffer selection bias (only good players keep playing). (search summary) — [Schuckers et al., arXiv 2110.14017](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2110.14017)

### Inferences
- Recommended structure for a one-semester build, in rising order of effort:
  1. Empirical burn-rate pricing: bucket player-seasons by age band, prior-season games missed and minutes tier; price as the historical mean payoff in the bucket plus a loading. This is the actuarial baseline every fancier model must beat.
  2. Hurdle or zero-inflated negative binomial on games missed per player-season, with offset for team games, covariates (age, prior two seasons' games missed, minutes per game, position) and a player random effect. This handles the mass at low values and the over-dispersion.
  3. Frequency-severity: Poisson or negative binomial for the number of injury events per season (exposure = expected games or minutes) times a heavy-tailed severity (log-normal, or a discrete mixture with a "season-ending" component). Monte Carlo gives the full payoff distribution, which is needed for deductibles, caps and 65-game-style thresholds.
  4. Survival model (Cox or discrete-time logistic hazard by game) if the contract triggers on time to first major injury; month-of-season should enter the baseline hazard.
  5. Hierarchical Bayesian partial pooling (player within team within season) to get credible intervals for players with little history and to estimate the team and season variance components that drive portfolio correlation.
- "Games missed" in the settlement definition must separate injury from coach decision, rest, illness, suspension and trade or waiver, because non-injury absences are as numerous as injury absences. A payoff on "games not played" is exposed to moral hazard (rest, tanking); a payoff on "games listed out with an injury designation" depends on the official report, which is only cleanly machine-readable from 2021-22.
- Thresholds such as the 65-game line create digital payoffs; these need the simulated distribution rather than a mean forecast, and are sensitive to the heavy tail.
- Season-forward validation (train to season t, test on t+1) with Brier score, log loss and calibration by decile is the right evaluation; compare against the bucketed burn-rate baseline.

### Gaps
- No source was found that documents how public projection systems (CARMELO or RAPTOR projections, DARKO, EPM, fantasy projections) forecast games played, or their games-played projection error. FiveThirtyEight projection methodology articles surfaced but were not read: [2017-18](https://fivethirtyeight.com/features/whats-new-in-our-nba-player-projections-for-2017-18), [2018-19](https://fivethirtyeight.com/features/our-nba-player-projections-are-ready-for-2018-19).
- No peer-reviewed or working paper applying frequency-severity, zero-inflated, survival or hierarchical Bayesian models specifically to NBA games missed was found this session. The method recommendations above are my inferences from base-rate facts, not literature findings.
- No information was found on how real disability or contract insurers (for example temporary total disability policies for NBA contracts) price or set exclusions; that is likely in another researcher's scope.

## Key Question 6: How predictable is player performance separate from injury?

### Takeaway
Evidence gathered here is thin: box-score composites are fairly stable year to year (PER about 0.75) while pure plus-minus impact measures are very noisy (about 0.24), players peak in their mid-to-late twenties, and major injuries cost roughly 20 to 25% of production in the first one to two seasons back. I did not find published projection-error sizes for modern impact metrics.

### Cited Findings
- Year-to-year correlation for a context-dependent, play-by-play impact score (2012-13 to 2013-14) was 0.242, versus 0.75 for PER. — [Deshpande and Jensen, arXiv 1604.03186](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.03186)
- Usage%, steal%, turnover% and block% are fairly stable year to year and explained mostly by the player's own previous performance rather than role; assist% is very stable but heavily role-dependent. (search summary) — [Neil Paine: Which Rate Stats Correlate More With Role Than Skill](https://neilpaine.substack.com/p/which-rate-stats-correlate-more-with)
- Single-season RAPM needs a large sample before coefficients are sensible, and is not useful until well into a season (practitioner forum). (search summary) — [APBR forum](https://www.apbr.org/metrics/viewtopic.php?p=41210)
- Ageing: a Bayesian study (Vaci et al. 2019) finds NBA players peak in their late twenties and decline fairly rapidly afterwards; one BPM analysis of 438 players who entered the league at 23 or younger found most peaked at 24 to 25 with a marked drop after 28. (search summary; the second figure's outlet is unverified) — [Psychonomic Society summary of Vaci et al.](https://featuredcontent.psychonomic.org/the-early-shining-bird-flies-longer-career-development-determines-nba-players-performance-de/); [D Magazine](https://www.dmagazine.com/?p=905777)
- Post-injury decline: Achilles, VORP 24.1% below pre-injury two seasons later, with BPM and offensive rating significantly down versus controls; ACL, PER 19.3% lower in the first season back (12.5 to 7.6 in another series) and near baseline by the second. (search summary) — [Chauhan et al. 2021](https://www.pubmed.gov/34010095/); [PubMed 33738307](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33738307/)
- Achilles cohort (47 players): game utilisation remained below controls at three years even when per-game performance did not. (search summary) — [PMC8436311](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8436311)
- Even the best public player model leaves large game-level error: EPM-based predictions miss final margins by about 11 points on average and pick 68.4% of winners live (67.5% for the baseline). — [Dunks and Threes: About EPM](https://dunksandthrees.com/about/epm)

### Inferences
- A performance-shortfall contract should be written on volume-inclusive, box-score-anchored metrics (Win Shares, VORP, or a counting stat) rather than per-possession plus-minus metrics: with a year-to-year correlation near 0.24, a pure plus-minus contract would pay out mostly on noise, while a correlation near 0.75 still leaves about 44% of variance (1 − 0.75²) unexplained by last season alone.
- Volume metrics such as VORP and Win Shares mix performance with availability (minutes), so a "performance" contract on them is partly an injury contract. To isolate performance risk, settle on a rate metric conditional on a minimum-minutes threshold, and price the availability leg separately.
- Ageing and post-injury effects can enter as mean shifts in a simple projection (weighted three-season average plus age adjustment plus a first-season-back penalty of about 20%); the residual standard deviation from the team's own back-test is the quantity the insurer is exposed to and should be reported directly.

### Gaps
- No published year-to-year correlations for EPM, LEBRON, DARKO, RAPTOR, BPM, VORP or Win Shares were found this session; the team can compute these in an afternoon from Basketball Reference and the RAPTOR archive.
- No published projection error sizes (RMSE or MAE of next-season BPM, EPM or WAR) were found.
- No quantified ageing curve (change in metric per year of age) was retrieved; the APBR RAPM ageing thread and Vaci et al. full text were not read.
- No sources on performance decline after stress fractures or back surgery.

## Key Question 7: How correlated are these risks across players and teams?

### Takeaway
I found no study that decomposes NBA injury variance into player, team and season components. The clearest evidence of correlated risk is league-wide: compressed schedules raised injury rates 40 to 60% for everyone at once (2011-12 and 2020-21), and COVID protocols removed 2,413 player-games; team-level effects are plausible through medical staff and coaching rest decisions but unquantified in the sources I saw.

### Cited Findings
- League-wide shock seasons: incidence was 21.68 per 1,000 in lockout 2011-12 versus 13.54 in 2008-09, with severe injuries roughly doubling; other seasons were largely stable. — [Bullock et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8207289)
- Condensed 2020-21: injury proportion ratio 1.42 (95% CI 1.32 to 1.52) versus 2015-18, across all lower-extremity injury types. — [Morikawa et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9445480)
- COVID-19 health-and-safety protocols accounted for 2,413 missed player-games in 2017-23 data. — [NBER WP 35803](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w35803/w35803.pdf)
- Secular trend affecting all stars: 10.6 games missed per season in the 1990s versus 23.9 in the 2020s; the league study attributes part of this to team medical and performance staffs gaining influence over participation decisions. — [NBA.com load management study](https://www.nba.com/news/nba-sends-data-load-management-study)
- Within-team mechanical dependence: when teammates are absent, remaining players are assigned more minutes (the NBER instrument relies on this), and more assigned minutes causally raise injury risk (about 0.1 percentage point per minute for a star). — [NBER WP 35803](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w35803/w35803.pdf)
- Coaches and medical staff act on private information about individual risk (injury risk is higher after a coach-chosen rest than after illness or personal absence), so team decision-making is a channel for team-specific differences in recorded absences. — [NBER WP 35803](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w35803/w35803.pdf)
- Team-level outcomes differ widely in a given season: Spotrac-based tallies showed the Pacers losing $79.4M in cash to injury in 2025-26. Media rankings of training staffs use "Adjusted Games Lost" but concede that totals partly reflect luck and roster composition. (search summary) — [BetMGM: Most Injured Teams 2025-26](https://sports.betmgm.com/en/blog/nba/most-injured-teams-in-nba-bm23/); [Rotowire: Evaluating Team Health](https://www.rotowire.com/article/national-athletic-training-month-evaluating-team-health-81004)
- Player-level position effects are negligible (guards 18.17, forwards 17.23, centres 18.16 per 1,000), so position diversification buys little. — [Bullock et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8207289)

### Inferences
- An insurance book on NBA games missed is not a book of independent risks. There is at least a common season factor (schedule density, pandemics, rule changes such as the Player Participation Policy and 65-game rule) that moved league-wide rates by 40% or more in two of the last fifteen seasons. Capital and loading should be set from a model with a season random effect, or by re-sampling whole seasons rather than individual player-seasons.
- Within a team, risks are positively dependent in the short run (one injury raises teammates' minutes and therefore their hazard), and teams that fall out of contention have incentives to shut players down, which correlates "games missed" across a tanking roster late in the season. Writing several contracts on the same team concentrates risk.
- A team random effect in a hierarchical model, estimated on 2005 to 2026 player-seasons, would directly answer how much variance is team-level; its year-to-year persistence (does a low-injury team stay low?) is the test of whether medical staff quality is real or luck. This is a feasible and original student contribution.
- Performance risk is also correlated within teams (shared usage, coaching, role changes after a teammate's injury), with sign that can be negative: a co-star's injury raises a player's counting stats while hurting team wins. That is a natural partial hedge inside a same-team book.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed estimate of team-level variance or intraclass correlation in NBA injury rates was found, nor any study of persistence of team injury rates across seasons or of medical-staff effects.
- No study of playing-style effects (pace, drives, three-point rate at team level) on injury rates was found; Lu et al. list individual three-point and free-throw attempt rates as predictors only.
- No quantitative evidence on correlation between a player's injury and team win-total or playoff-odds moves (the hedge ratio) was found.

### Inferences
- The feasible back-test for a student team is static: for each season since 2003, take the pre-season win total (Basketball Reference), the star's realised games missed, and the team's realised wins, and estimate how much of the insurer's payout is offset by a pre-season "under" position. That gives about 23 seasons times 30 teams of data, which is enough to estimate a hedge ratio with wide error bars.
- A dynamic hedge (trade the win total or playoff odds when the injury happens) needs time-stamped in-season futures. Free sources for that are essentially Kalshi (recent seasons only) and whatever can be recovered from Wayback snapshots of sportsbook or SportsOddsHistory pages; plan for this to be a small-sample illustration rather than a statistical test.
- Game-level closing lines (from a Sportsbook Review scrape) can be turned into a market-implied team strength before and after an injury, which is a workable proxy for the futures move when futures history is missing.
- Win-total markets only settle on team outcomes, so the hedge has large basis risk for non-star players; expect it to matter only for the top one or two players per team.

### Gaps
- Kalshi: I could not load the documentation, so the live-versus-historical cutoff, authentication requirement, rate limits, candlestick intervals and the first date of NBA markets are unverified. The team should test the API directly.
- SportsOddsHistory under Covers: coverage years for NBA win totals, MVP and title futures, and current terms of use, could not be confirmed because the page returned 503.
- No free source of historical NBA playoff-odds or in-season futures time series from sportsbooks was confirmed. No Polymarket NBA history was researched.
- Licensing for redistribution or scraping of Basketball Reference odds tables beyond the 20-requests-per-minute rule was not checked (a separate Data Use page exists and was not read).
