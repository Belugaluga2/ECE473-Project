## 1. How much salary is paid to injured NBA players each season (league-wide and per team), and what is the trend?

### Takeaway
In 2025-26 NBA teams paid roughly $1.3 billion in guaranteed salary to players who were unavailable (Spotrac-based tallies), about 28% of the aggregate salary cap and the highest non-pandemic share since tracking began in 2016-17; team totals ranged from about $16M (Timberwolves) to about $80M (Pacers). The loss is heavily concentrated in the top earners: the 30 highest-paid players alone were on pace for more than $525M of "empty salary" in 2025-26 versus $79M in 2015-16.

### Cited Findings
League-wide dollars
- 2025-26: "more than $1.3 billion" in guaranteed pay went to injured or sidelined players. Method: Casino Guru's data team summed Spotrac's injury tracker; counts injury, illness, rest and injury management, excludes personal absences. Published August 3, 2026. **[tracker, compiled by a third party]** — [ClutchPoints](https://clutchpoints.com/nba/nba-stories/nba-teams-paid-stunning-1-3-billion-for-injured-players)
- 2025-26: share of salary cap spent on injured players = 28.08% after scaling from the 72-game mark to 82 games (24.66% unscaled as of March 24, 2026); described as the highest since 2016-17 excluding pandemic seasons, with 2024-25 the previous non-COVID high. Method: Spotrac team injured-list tracker (cash earned while out), data "reliably goes back to 2016-17", a few days-missed errors hand-corrected by the author. The article says the NBA is "spending over a billion dollars a year" on injured players. **[tracker + author's projection]** — [Above the Break (N. B. Lindberg), March 25, 2026](https://abovethebreak.substack.com/p/the-nba-is-in-the-red-zone)
- Top-30 earners only ("empty salary" = pay for team games not played, ESPN salary data): 2015-16 about $79M (top-30 combined salary about $560M, 14% of team games missed); 2024-25 "more than $352 million"; 2025-26 "on pace to exceed $525 million" as of December 10, 2025 (top-30 combined salary more than $1.49B, 35% of team games missed). Share of games missed by top-30 earners ran 14% to 20% in the four seasons before 2019-20 and 24% to 35% since; 2023-24 was 24%. **[measured for past seasons from salary and games data; 2025-26 figure is an early-season projection]** — [Ben Golliver, Washington Post via Philadelphia Inquirer, Dec. 11, 2025](https://www.inquirer.com/sixers/joel-embiid-stephen-curry-nba-injuries-costs-20251211.html)

Games and days missed
- 2024-25 regular season: 6,779 cumulative games missed to injury or illness (excluding COVID-related), the most on record per Jeff Stotts of In Street Clothes; six Achilles ruptures that season. **[tracker]** — [Front Office Sports, May 14, 2025](https://frontofficesports.com/nba-injury-crisis-puts-82-game-schedule-under-new-scrutiny/)
- A different count for the same 2024-25 season, from Spotrac data: 7,625 games missed and more than 15,000 days lost; 2025-26 through March 24, 2026: 6,428 games missed, 451 injuries (unscaled), 13,674 days projected. Injuries per season rose "from 410 to 510" across the tracked period (seasons not specified in the text). Average days missed per injury has ranged 12.21 (2016-17) to 15.76 (2017-18). **[tracker]** — [Above the Break](https://abovethebreak.substack.com/p/the-nba-is-in-the-red-zone). Conflict: Stotts 6,779 vs Spotrac-derived 7,625 for 2024-25; the two trackers define absences differently (Spotrac includes rest and counts days on the list).
- Early-season pace, Stotts data relayed by Tom Haberstroh (December 2025): games lost through week 8 were 1,579 (2023-24), 1,962 (2024-25), 2,062 (2025-26); games missed up 35% versus the same point of 2023-24. **[snippet]** — [RealGM wiretap](https://basketball.realgm.com/wiretap/278024/NBA-Injuries-Up-35-perc-Over-Last-Season)

Per-team distribution, 2025-26
- Final-season tally (Casino Guru from Spotrac): Pacers $81.5M (highest), Mavericks $72.5M, Warriors $72M, Grizzlies $71.8M (league-high 22 injuries and 1,081 missed days); lowest were Timberwolves $16.1M, Knicks $18M, Nets $22.3M. **[tracker]** — [ClutchPoints](https://clutchpoints.com/nba/nba-stories/nba-teams-paid-stunning-1-3-billion-for-injured-players)
- Near-end-of-season snapshot (article dated April 9, 2026, Spotrac tracker): Pacers $79,359,136; Mavericks $71,467,639; Grizzlies $70,609,592; Kings $69,699,670; Warriors $68,846,728; Wizards $56,097,554; Trail Blazers $55,669,012; Heat $54,136,609; Celtics $49,621,885; Bulls $49,234,164. **[tracker]** — [BetMGM](https://sports.betmgm.com/en/blog/nba/most-injured-teams-in-nba-bm23/)
- Per-player cost in 2025-26 (same Spotrac-based tally): Tyrese Haliburton $44.5M (full season, Achilles), Kyrie Irving $35.9M (full season, ACL), Jimmy Butler $27.6M (44 games, ACL), Stephen Curry $24.3M (40 games), Anthony Davis $17.1M (55 games), Obi Toppin $9.7M (58 games). **[tracker]** — [ClutchPoints](https://clutchpoints.com/nba/nba-stories/nba-teams-paid-stunning-1-3-billion-for-injured-players)
- A different, much larger kind of number circulates in the press: the full-season salaries of everyone on the injury report on a given night. Example: Warriors "$193.5 million sidelined, more than 93% of payroll" in March 2026; 76ers $160.2M (about 85%), Kings $132.3M (68.82%), Jazz $116.1M (78.08%), Grizzlies $108.5M (80.70%). These are point-in-time snapshots of annual salaries, not cash paid for missed games. **[rough]** — [UB Law Sports and Entertainment Forum, March 29, 2026](https://ublawsportsforum.com/2026/03/29/what-the-warriors-193-million-injury-crisis-reveals-about-the-nbas-broken-model/)
- Anecdotes from December 2025: Tatum, Lillard, Haliburton and Irving ($204.4M combined 2025-26 salary) had not played a game; the Pelicans' five highest-paid players (more than $140M combined) were all absent in one loss; Mavericks coach Jason Kidd said "over, I think, $100 million" was on the sideline. **[rough]** — [Washington Post via Inquirer](https://www.inquirer.com/sixers/joel-embiid-stephen-curry-nba-injuries-costs-20251211.html)

Offsets the owner already has
- Madison Square Garden Sports (Knicks owner) FY2026 10-K: player salary insurance "generally pays a portion" of an injured player's salary after a deductible number of missed games; the policies do not cover lost revenue from reduced popularity or competitive success; disability insurance does not cover NBA luxury tax; an injured player's salary still counts toward team salary for luxury-tax purposes for at least one year. **[measured: issuer disclosure]** — [MSG Sports Form 10-K, fiscal year ended June 30, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001636519/000162828026056812/msgs-20260630.htm)
- MSG Sports booked provisions for season-ending player injuries (net of anticipated insurance recoveries) of $0 in FY2026 and $133 thousand in FY2025. **[measured]** — [MSG Sports 10-K FY2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001636519/000162828026056812/msgs-20260630.htm)
- The NBA requires temporary-total-disability or permanent-disability cover on each team's highest-paid players; premiums for such policies across sports "well exceed $1 million a year"; most teams insure only a percentage of guaranteed salary. **[rough: trade newsletter, May 15, 2025]** — [Beyond the Sideline](https://beyondthesideline.beehiiv.com/p/easing-the-pain-with-insurance)
- Commonly repeated terms of the league-wide policy: top five salaries per team covered, 41-game waiting period, 80% of base salary for games missed thereafter, capped at $175,000 per game. I found these only in a fan-forum post quoting an FAQ and in low-quality explainer sites. **[snippet, unverified]** — [RealGM forum](https://forums.realgm.com/boards/viewtopic.php?p=34486932). ESPN's Bobby Marks is quoted that teams "already have a disability policy in place for their top earners" which protects teams, not players. — [Hoops Rumors, July 2020](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2020/07/nba-nbpa-reach-agreements-on-disability-insurance-salary-withholding.html)

### Inferences
- The $1.3B total and the 28.08% share are the same number seen two ways: 30 teams x the $154.647M cap = $4.64B, and 28.08% of that is $1.30B. So "share of cap" in the Above the Break series means injured cash divided by aggregate cap, not by actual payroll (actual payrolls exceed the cap, so the share of real payroll is lower).
- League average is about $43M per team per season ($1.3B / 30) with a wide, right-skewed spread: the 2025-26 team range was roughly $16M to $81M, about a 5x ratio between best and worst. The tail is driven by one or two max-contract players (Haliburton alone was $44.5M of Indiana's $81.5M).
- Per-game unit cost of a max player in 2025-26: the 35% max salary of $54,126,450 over 82 games is about $660,000 per regular-season game missed (salary only, before tax and before insurance).
- The exposure has grown faster than salaries. Top-30 empty salary rose from $79M (2015-16) to $352M (2024-25), about 4.5x, while the cap rose from $70M to $140.6M (2x); the rest is the higher share of games missed (14% to roughly 30%).
- If the 41-game / 80% terms are right, a covered max player who misses all 82 games would be reimbursed for at most 80% x 41/82 = 40% of salary (less if the $175k per-game cap binds: 41 x $175k = $7.2M, which is only 13% of a $54.1M salary). That would leave most of the salary loss with the owner. This rests on unverified terms.
- This loss component is the most index-friendly: games missed per player is public (official injury reports, Spotrac, In Street Clothes) and salary per game is contractual, so "salary x games missed" is a transparent parametric trigger.

### Gaps
- Spotrac's own team table for 2025-26 is behind a paywall (HTTP 402); all team figures above come through third parties (Casino Guru, BetMGM, Above the Break). The year-by-year series of injured-salary share for 2016-17 to 2024-25 exists only as chart images in the Above the Break post; I could not extract the values.
- No official NBA figure exists for salary paid to injured players; all totals are tracker tallies with definitional differences (rest days, illness, two-way players).
- The actual terms of the league-wide disability policy (covered players, waiting period, percentage, per-game cap, exclusions for pre-existing conditions, who pays premiums) are not confirmed by any primary source I could reach. Actual insurance recoveries by team are not disclosed; MSG Sports reports provisions net of recoveries without the gross amounts.
- Jeff Stotts's full season-by-season games-missed series was not retrieved.

## 2. What is the marginal revenue value of a win, of making the playoffs, and of each playoff round or home playoff game?

### Takeaway
Regular-season wins are worth little in direct revenue (the best-cited academic estimate is under $1M per win in 2011 dollars, and it is dated), while playoff home games are worth an order of magnitude more per event: a measured $66.9M year-on-year increase in Knicks playoff revenue in fiscal 2026, and analyst estimates of roughly $8M to $20M per home game rising by round for a top market. Playoff revenue is gross: the league takes 25% of the gate and the Knicks' playoff-related expenses rose $49.5M against that $66.9M.

### Cited Findings
Measured, from a listed team owner
- MSG Sports (Knicks and Rangers) FY2026 (year ended June 30, 2026): total revenue $1,153.8M vs $1,039.2M (+$114.6M, +11%); operating income $28.9M vs $14.8M; adjusted operating income $58.7M vs $38.2M. Itemised revenue change: playoff-related revenues +$66.9M; league distributions +$41.4M; pre/regular-season ticket-related +$12.7M; suites +$12.5M; sponsorship and signage +$7.6M; food, beverage and merchandise +$4.7M; local media rights fees -$31.6M. Direct operating expense change: playoff-related expenses +$49.5M; team personnel compensation +$41.3M; revenue sharing and luxury tax +$23.3M. The Knicks played nine home playoff games in both FY2026 (championship) and FY2025 (conference finals); the Rangers missed the playoffs in both years. **[measured]** — [MSG Sports 10-K FY2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001636519/000162828026056812/msgs-20260630.htm); headline figures also in the [company release](https://investor.msgsports.com/press-releases/news-details/2026/Madison-Square-Garden-Sports-Corp--Reports-Fiscal-2026-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-Results/default.aspx)
- The 10-K states playoff home games provide "a significant source of additional revenue" and gives no per-game figure. **[measured disclosure]** — [MSG Sports 10-K FY2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001636519/000162828026056812/msgs-20260630.htm)

Analyst estimates, Knicks 2026 playoff run
- Seaport Research (David Joyce): at least $145M total playoff revenue; about $20.3M per Finals home game ("likely conservative"); the first- and second-round sweeps cost about $29M of potential revenue by removing home dates. JPMorgan (May 8, 2026 note): about $12M revenue per home playoff date. Regular-season comparison: about $209M ticket revenue for fiscal 2026, about $5M ticket revenue per home game, total revenue per regular-season home game in the high single-digit millions. Teams keep 75% of playoff ticket revenue; the league keeps 25%. **[estimate]** — [Front Office Sports, June 4, 2026](https://frontofficesports.com/knicks-finals-money-tickets/)
- Per-round ladder attributed to Joyce: about $8M per home game in round one, $12M in round two, $17M in the conference finals, at least $20M in the Finals, versus about $4M for a regular-season game; a separate JPMorgan figure of about $5M profit per playoff home game. **[snippet]** — [Front Office Sports search result](https://frontofficesports.com/knicks-finals-money-tickets/)
- Older benchmarks: a 2017 report put Warriors revenue at roughly $12M per home playoff date and a sweep's cost at $22M in lost gate. **[rough, 2017; snippet]** — [NBC Sports](https://www.nbcsports.com/nba/news/report-warriors-sweep-could-cost-team-22-million-in-lost-gate-revenue)

Academic revenue-per-win
- Berri, Leeds and von Allmen (2015, International Journal of Sport Finance 10(1): 5-25) regression values, as relayed second-hand: $486,524 of revenue per current-season win and $358,241 per prior-season (lagged) win, in 2011 US dollars. **[measured in the original paper; here second-hand, not checked against the paper]** — [Kyle Newcombe, Jan. 28, 2025](https://kylenewcombe.substack.com/p/marginal-revenue-product-analysis)
- A working paper covering MLB, NBA and NFL finds contemporaneous performance has a positive effect on team revenue and franchise value in the NBA and MLB; no effect size in the summary. **[snippet]** — [Econ One](https://econone.com/?p=5684)

League-level evidence that playoff matchups move revenue
- 2024-25 basketball-related income came in at $10.25B, below projection; Sportico's Kurt Badenhausen attributed the miss to a turbulent year for local media and to small-market teams (Pacers, Thunder) making deep runs, which depressed playoff gate. **[measured BRI, journalist attribution]** — [Hoops Rumors, July 4, 2025](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2025/07/nba-players-lose-484mm-from-2024-25-salaries.html); [Hoops Rumors, Nov. 12, 2025](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2025/11/nba-projecting-14-3-billion-in-revenue-for-2025-26-season.html)

### Inferences
- With nine Knicks home playoff games in both years, the +$66.9M of playoff revenue is entirely a round-and-price effect (conference finals exit vs championship, sweeps early), about +$7.4M per home date on average. It supports the analyst ladder in which later-round games are worth roughly two to three times a first-round game.
- Net is far below gross. Playoff-related expenses rose $49.5M against the $66.9M revenue gain, leaving roughly $17M of incremental contribution before any other cost. An insurance product written on "playoff revenue" should be sized on contribution margin, not on the $12M to $20M per-game headline.
- The Berri et al. figures (about $0.85M per win combining current and lagged, 2011 dollars) are small next to the payroll cost of a win (about $3.4M to $4.3M, see question 7). On that evidence the P&L effect of losing, say, 10 regular-season wins to an injury is single-digit millions in direct revenue; the larger money is (a) the binary playoff threshold and number of home dates, and (b) salary paid for nothing.
- "Number of home playoff games by round" is a clean, publicly observable index. Its dollar value per unit is market-specific (Knicks figures are the league ceiling) and needs to be calibrated per team.
- Sweeps are a distinct risk: winning too efficiently cost the Knicks an estimated $29M in 2026. A product keyed to rounds won would not pay for that; a product keyed to home dates would.

### Gaps
- No current (post-2023) peer-reviewed estimate of marginal revenue per NBA win was found; the Berri et al. numbers use data that predate the 2016 and 2025 media deals and I did not read the original paper.
- No per-round playoff revenue for a small- or mid-market team was found. The only audited figures are from the largest market in the league.
- MSG Sports does not disclose total playoff revenue in dollars (only the year-on-year change) or the split of playoff-related expenses between league assessments, player playoff pay and revenue sharing.
- No published estimate of the probability-weighted revenue value of "making the playoffs" as a single threshold (as exists for MLB) was found for the NBA.

## 3. What is the measured revenue effect of a star player being absent?

### Takeaway
The cleanest measurements are on the secondary ticket market: an announced superstar absence cuts resale prices by roughly 4% to 25% per game (up to 21% or about $73 a ticket for LeBron James road games), and star presence historically added thousands of fans per game. Evidence for the team's own revenue lines (primary gate, local TV ratings, sponsorship, merchandise) is thin; the only TV study is national and finds a modest $15M to $20M per season league-wide advertising loss.

### Cited Findings
Secondary-market ticket prices
- Kaplan (2022), "Putting a price on popularity: Evidence from superstars in the National Basketball Association", Economic Inquiry 60(3): 1357-1381. Method: high-frequency listing microdata from an online secondary marketplace around the timing of absence announcements. Result: 4% to 22% ($4 to $41 per ticket) price reductions when a superstar is announced out; LeBron James away games -21% (about $73 per ticket), Stephen Curry away games -18% (about $50 per ticket); popularity predicts willingness to pay better than on-court productivity, and the popularity effect is convex. **[measured, peer-reviewed]** — [EconPapers record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:ecinqu:v:60:y:2022:i:3:p:1357-1381)
- Conference version (Kaplan, Ramamoorthy, Gupte, Sagar, Premkumar, Wilbur, Zilberman): absences of Curry, Kyrie Irving, Anthony Davis and others reduce average ticket price 7% to 25% ($9 to $25); Curry and Durant matter more on the road, Davis and Porzingis more at home; the effect is much smaller in larger markets; a season of absences produces "millions of dollars" of welfare loss. **[measured, working paper]** — [MIT Sloan Sports Analytics Conference](https://www.sloansportsconference.com/research-papers/the-economic-impact-of-nba-superstars-evidence-from-missed-games-using-ticket-microdata-from-a-secondary-marketplace)
- A podcast summary of the same research gives 9% to 24% ($9 to $33 per ticket). **[snippet]** — [MIT Sloan Management Review](https://sloanreview.mit.edu/?p=67186). The three ranges differ because they come from different versions and samples of the same project; the Economic Inquiry figures are the refereed ones.

Attendance
- Humphreys and Johnson (2020), "The Effect of Superstars on Game Attendance: Evidence From the NBA", Journal of Sports Economics 21(2): 152-175. Data: game-level attendance 1981-82 to 2013-14, with controls for sellout censoring, team quality and expected outcome. Result: superstars raise both home and away attendance; Michael Jordan's externality was about 4,837 extra fans per home game and 4,236 per away game (the 2017 working paper had 5,021 and 5,631). **[measured, peer-reviewed; figures via search summary of abstract]** — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:sae:jospec:v:21:y:2020:i:2:p:152-175); [working paper](https://ideas.repec.org/p/wvu/wpaper/17-16.html)
- A 2024 undergraduate study finds superstars significantly raise attendance, more so at home; it did not test ticket prices or load management. **[student work]** — [UNC Office for Undergraduate Research](https://our.unc.edu/abstract/dunbar-dunking-dollars-assessing-the-economic-impact-of-superstars-on-nba-game-attendance)

Television
- Reilly, Solow and von Allmen (2023), "When the Stars Are Out", Journal of Sports Economics 24(7): 877-902. Data: national NBA telecasts, 2018-19. Result: doubling the proportion of star players missing games reduces TV audience by about 6.5 million household viewings per regular season; advertising revenue lost to stars missing games is roughly $15M to $20M per season. **[measured, peer-reviewed; via search summary of abstract]** — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:sae:jospec:v:24:y:2023:i:7:p:877-902)
- 2019-20 industry commentary: Altman Vilandrie and MoffettNathanson linked part of a 16% early-season national ratings drop to injured stars (survey-based); local RSN telecasts were off about 7% in the same period; other analysts blamed cord-cutting. **[rough, 2019-20]** — [Consulting.us](https://www.consulting.us/news/3511/injured-superstars-behind-nba-ratings-drop-says-consulting-firm); [Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/live-feed/why-nba-ratings-have-missed-far-year-1262338)

What the insurer does not cover
- MSG Sports states its player insurance does not cover lost revenue from reduced popularity or competitive success after an injury. **[measured disclosure]** — [MSG Sports 10-K FY2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001636519/000162828026056812/msgs-20260630.htm)

### Inferences
- Secondary-market price drops are not team revenue. Most seats are sold in advance (MSG Sports carried $118.2M of deferred revenue, mainly tickets and suites, at June 30, 2026 per the same 10-K), so a star sitting out one game mostly hurts ticket holders and resellers. The owner's exposure arrives with a lag through season-ticket renewal rates, next-season pricing, single-game dynamic pricing and no-show-driven concession sales. Kaplan's percentages are best read as an upper bound on the per-game demand shock, not as realised team losses.
- The road-game result (LeBron and Curry matter most away) means part of a star's value is an externality captured by the other 29 owners; conversely, a home owner is exposed to visiting stars' health, which the owner cannot control. That is a natural candidate for an index product (a "visiting star availability" index) since no single team's medical staff can influence it.
- The smaller effect in large markets suggests small- and mid-market owners with one star have the highest proportional revenue sensitivity to that star's absence.
- The national TV loss ($15M to $20M a season league-wide, 2018-19 dollars) falls on the broadcasters under fixed-fee contracts, not on owners, until the next rights negotiation.

### Gaps
- I found no published study that measures a star absence's effect on a team's primary gate receipts, local TV ratings, sponsorship or merchandise sales. Local ratings data by team are reported by trade press, but no causal estimate tied to star absences was found.
- No study was found that quantifies the multi-season effect of a long star absence (for example a full missed season) on season-ticket renewals.
- Per-player coefficients beyond the handful named above require the full papers, which I did not open.

## 4. How do the luxury tax, the aprons and revenue sharing change the cost of a lost season? What does a max-player injury cost a team over the tax line?

### Takeaway
An injured player's salary keeps counting toward the tax, and the tax is not insurable under existing team policies, so for a taxpaying team each marginal payroll dollar in 2025-26 cost between $1.00 and $4.75 of tax (or $3.00 to $6.75 for repeaters) on top of the salary itself. Teams respond by dumping salary when a star goes down: after Jayson Tatum's Achilles tear the Celtics cut a projected $512M salary-plus-tax bill to about $274M, and league-wide tax collections fell to $223M in 2025-26.

### Cited Findings
Thresholds and rates
- 2025-26 figures: salary cap $154,647,000 (up 10% from $140,588,000); minimum team salary $139,182,000; luxury tax line $187,895,000; first apron $195,945,000; second apron $207,824,000; maximum salaries $38,661,750 (0-6 years), $46,394,100 (7-9 years), $54,126,450 (10+ years). **[measured, league-set]** — [Hoops Rumors, June 2025](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2025/06/salary-cap-tax-line-set-for-2025-26-nba-season.html)
- 2026-27 projections (ESPN, March 2026): cap $165M, floor $149M, tax line $201M, first apron $209M, second apron $222M; the cap rise is about $1M smaller than first projected, "largely because of lost revenue from the collapse of Main Street Sports". **[projection]** — [Front Office Sports, March 24, 2026](https://frontofficesports.com/nba-rsn-salary-cap/)
- Tax rates per dollar over the line, by bracket (bracket width was $5,168,000 in 2024-25 and grows with the cap). Through 2024-25: standard $1.50 / $1.75 / $2.50 / $3.25; repeater $2.50 / $2.75 / $3.50 / $4.25. From 2025-26: standard $1.00 / $1.25 / $3.50 / $4.75; repeater $3.00 / $3.25 / $5.50 / $6.75; each further bracket adds $0.50. A repeater has paid tax in at least three of the previous four seasons. Half of tax proceeds goes to "league purposes" (in practice funding revenue sharing) and up to half is split equally among non-taxpayers. **[measured, CBA terms]** — [Hoops Rumors glossary](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2024/11/hoops-rumors-glossary-luxury-tax-penalties-4.html)
- Second-apron teams lose all mid-level exceptions, cannot aggregate salaries or take back more salary than they send in trades, and have a first-round pick seven years out frozen (Celtics, Suns and Timberwolves had 2032 picks frozen after 2024-25). **[measured, CBA terms as reported]** — [CBS Sports](https://www.cbssports.com/nba/news/nba-trade-deadline-luxury-tax-apron-explainer-2026); [CBS Sports, June 25, 2025](https://www.cbssports.com/nba/news/why-celtics-are-trading-away-key-players-money-not-the-only-reason-for-jrue-holiday-kristaps-porzingis-deals)

Actual tax bills
- 2025-26 (as of April 12, 2026, ESPN's Bobby Marks): seven taxpayers, $223.1M total: Cavaliers $68.7M, Warriors $67.9M (repeater), Knicks $44.4M, Lakers $22.2M (repeater), Rockets $7.2M, Clippers $6.7M (repeater), Timberwolves $6.0M. Each of 23 non-taxpayers projected to receive about $4.9M, the lowest since 2020-21 (versus $11.4M in 2024-25). Before the February 5, 2026 trade deadline 14 teams projected as taxpayers; about half ducked under. Injury interaction
- Disability insurance does not cover luxury tax, and an injured player's salary counts toward team salary for tax purposes for at least one year. **[measured disclosure]** — [MSG Sports 10-K FY2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001636519/000162828026056812/msgs-20260630.htm)

Revenue sharing and the escrow system
- Players are entitled to 51% of basketball-related income; 10% of salaries was withheld in escrow in 2024-25; because BRI ($10.25B) missed projection, 91% of the escrow went back to teams, players received 90.9% of stated salaries, and the give-back was about $484M (Stephen Curry's actual pay was about $5M below his $55.76M cap figure). **[measured]** — [Hoops Rumors, July 4, 2025](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2025/07/nba-players-lose-484mm-from-2024-25-salaries.html)

### Inferences
- Worked example (my arithmetic, assuming the 2025-26 bracket width is the 2024-25 width grown 10%, about $5.685M): a non-repeater $20M over the line owes about $46.7M of tax (5.685 x 1.00 + 5.685 x 1.25 + 5.685 x 3.50 + 2.945 x 4.75); a repeater at the same level owes about $86.7M. For such a team, every dollar of a $54.1M injured max salary sits in the top brackets, so the all-in cost of that roster spot is salary plus the entire tax bill it causes.
- The tax turns a health event into a sharply non-linear payoff. If the injured star means the team will not contend, the rational response is a salary dump (Celtics 2025, half the projected 2025-26 taxpayers), which converts the loss into forgone talent and sweeteners (draft picks attached to shed salary) rather than cash tax. The observable trace is "projected tax at opening night minus tax actually paid".
- For non-taxpayers the reverse exposure is small and shrinking: the tax distribution fell from about $11.4M to $4.9M per team between 2024-25 and 2025-26, so a team that drifts over the line gives up under $5M of distribution in addition to paying tax.
- The escrow mechanism is an existing league-wide hedge: when league revenue disappoints, roughly half the shortfall is clawed back from player salaries (up to the 10% withheld). It protects owners against systematic revenue risk but not against team-specific injury or performance risk, which is where a new product would have to work.
- The tax is a deterministic function of team salary, bracket and repeater status, so the tax component of an injury loss can be computed exactly ex ante; it is fully "indexable" given the team's cap sheet.

### Gaps
- The exact 2025-26 bracket width was not found in a source; I assumed it grows with the cap as the glossary states.
- Final audited 2025-26 tax payments (after the 15 unresolved bonuses) were not found; the $223.1M is the April 12, 2026 projection.
- I found no source that prices the non-cash second-apron penalties (frozen picks, trade restrictions) in dollars.
- The mechanics of an injured player's salary being removed from the books (career-ending injury exclusion, disabled player exception) were not researched in detail here; the 10-K only says the salary counts "for at least one year".
- Team-by-team net revenue-sharing payers and receivers for 2025-26 were not found.

## 5. How do injuries and team performance affect franchise valuations and sale prices?

### Takeaway
Valuations are driven by market size, league-wide media money and scarcity far more than by a season's results or a star's injury: the average team is valued at $6.1B (Forbes) to $6.64B (Sportico) at about 14.6 times revenue, the Lakers resold for $12.5B fourteen months after a $10B sale, and the Celtics are valued at $8B after a year without Tatum. Evidence that winning moves value is weak and mostly qualitative; where it appears it works through sustained revenue rather than single seasons.

### Inferences
- At 14.6x revenue, a sustained $1M change in annual revenue is worth about $14.6M of enterprise value on Sportico's arithmetic, while a one-off $1M loss is worth $1M. So injuries and bad seasons matter to value only insofar as they shift the revenue baseline (renewals, sponsorship tiers, local rights). One-season salary losses to injury (about $43M per team on average) are 0.6% of the average team's value.
- Team-specific health shocks have not visibly dented prices: the Celtics sold at a record two months before Tatum's injury and are valued 31% higher ($8B) eighteen months later; the Pacers lost Haliburton for all of 2025-26 with no reported markdown. The laggards in 2026 (Grizzlies, Pelicans, Timberwolves, Trail Blazers, growth of 6% or less against 21% average) are small markets, several of them hit by the regional sports network collapse, which points to local-media exposure rather than on-court results as the differentiator.
- The Knicks (+30% with a title) and Bulls/Heat (+33% without one) grew at similar rates, which argues that the 2026 revaluation was market- and media-driven, with the title at most a secondary factor.
- Franchise value is therefore a poor target for a health or performance hedge: it is appraised annually, moved mainly by league-wide factors, and has almost no measurable short-run link to wins. Products should target annual cash flow items instead.

- Whether the $12.5B Lakers sale has been formally approved was not confirmed.
- No reporting was found on whether buyers price in specific contract liabilities (for example a max player rehabbing from Achilles surgery) at closing.

## 6. How does the 2025-26 national media deal change the split between fixed national revenue and local, performance-sensitive revenue?

### Takeaway
The new deals lifted each team's national TV money from about $103M to about $143M a year with roughly 7% annual escalators, which is about 31% of average team revenue ($456M) but only about 16% for the Warriors; at the same time local TV, historically the largest local line for small markets, is collapsing (Main Street Sports failed in 2026, affecting 13 teams). The result is a larger fixed floor for every owner, a salary cap that grows with national money regardless of a team's own local revenue, and a local revenue base that is shrinking toward gate, premium seating and sponsorship, the lines most sensitive to team quality and star availability.

### Cited Findings
- The 11-year agreements with Disney/ESPN, NBCUniversal and Amazon run 2025-26 to 2035-36 and are reported at $76B to $77B, about $6.9B per year on average versus about $2.7B per year under the previous nine-year deal. **[measured: reported contract value]** — [CBS Sports](https://www.cbssports.com/nba/news/nba-signs-new-tv-deal-details-on-11-year-76-billion-deal-with-espn-nbc-amazon-as-tnt-gets-left-out/amp); [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/nba-rsn-salary-cap/)
- League projection shared with owners in September 2025 (Sportico): gross revenue of $14.3B in 2025-26, up 12% from $12.75B; each team's national TV revenue rises from $103M to $143M, then grows about 7% a year. **[projection]** — [Hoops Rumors, Nov. 12, 2025](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2025/11/nba-projecting-14-3-billion-in-revenue-for-2025-26-season.html)
- Sportico's later tally of 2025-26 team revenue: $13.7B, about $456M per team. **[estimate]** — [ClutchPoints](https://clutchpoints.com/nba/nba-stories/nba-news-warriors-reach-14-5-billion-valuation-no-1-spot-over-knicks-lakers). - An owner quoted by ESPN: "National revenues drive up the cap, but local revenues are needed to keep up with player salaries. If a team can't generate enough local revenues, they lose money." **[opinion; snippet]** — [Hoops Rumors media-rights page](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/media-rights)
- Cap growth is smoothed to at most 10% a year under the 2023 CBA; the cap rose 10% for 2025-26 and is projected to rise about 6.7% for 2026-27. **[measured / projection]** — [Hoops Rumors](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2025/06/salary-cap-tax-line-set-for-2025-26-nba-season.html); [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/nba-rsn-salary-cap/)

### Inferences
- National TV at $143M is about 31% of the average team's $456M revenue and about 16% of the Warriors' $895M. By subtraction, roughly two-thirds of the average team's revenue and well over 80% of a top-market team's is local or otherwise non-equal-share. I did not find a source that states the local share directly.
- The Knicks show the mix shift in one filing: +$41.4M fixed national money, -$31.6M local media, and the remaining growth from playoff, ticket, suite and sponsorship lines. Local media is being replaced by national money one for one in a big market; in a Main Street market the local line may fall much further.
- Because 51% of BRI goes to players and the cap is set from league-wide revenue, national-deal growth raises every team's salary bill equally, while local revenue differs by a factor of several across teams. A small-market owner's salary obligations are therefore indexed to league revenue the owner does not control, and financed partly by local revenue that depends on team quality. This raises operating leverage on performance for low-revenue teams even as the fixed floor rises.
- The salary bill, and therefore the dollar value of injured-player salary, grows with the cap: at 7% to 10% annual cap growth the average team's $43M injured-salary loss would be on the order of $50M within two seasons with no change in injury rates.
- For product design: national revenue is fixed and needs no hedge; local media is a counterparty/credit problem, not a performance problem; the performance-sensitive residual is gate, premium, sponsorship renewals and playoff dates.

- Dollar values of individual teams' lost Main Street Sports rights fees and of their replacement deals for 2026-27 were not found; the "more than 80%" figure is from a single secondary source.
- The year-by-year payment schedule of the national deals (how back-loaded they are) was not found beyond the 7% escalator.

## 7. What is the size of the "dead money" problem: guaranteed contracts for players who underperform without being injured?

### Takeaway
Benchmarks put the average payroll cost of a regular-season win at $3.35M (2021-22), $3.83M (2022-23) and $4.29M (2023-24), and against those benchmarks a large share of contracts carry negative surplus; when a contract becomes untradeable the realised cost shows up as waive-and-stretch charges such as the Bucks' roughly $113M owed to Damian Lillard and the Suns' $19.4M a year for five years to Bradley Beal. Unlike injury salary, I found no league-wide annual dollar total for underperformance.

### Cited Findings
Dollars-per-win benchmarks (all are league payroll divided by wins, so they are average cost, not marginal market price)
- 2021-22: regular-season wins "cost about $3.35 million each"; player value measured in Estimated Wins from Dunks and Threes' EPM; Stephen Curry produced 15.28 eWins on a $45.8M salary. **[estimate]** — [TrueHoop (Abbott and Ilardi), June 30, 2022](https://www.truehoop.com/p/the-dollar-value-of-a-winning-nba)
- 2022-23: $3.83M per win ($4.722B total salary / 1,230 wins); player wins = average of Win Shares and VORP x 2.7; surplus = wins x cost per win minus salary. Nikola Jokic had the top surplus, $41.7M on a $32.5M salary (19.33 wins); 315 players produced negative value; Tobias Harris ($37.6M salary, 5.9 Win Shares) is given as a negative-value example. **[estimate]** — [Above the Break, Aug. 7, 2023](https://abovethebreak.substack.com/p/the-most-valuable-players-of-the)
- 2023-24: teams averaged $169.8M in player compensation and $4.29M per win. **[estimate; snippet]** — [JohnWallStreet](https://www.johnwallstreet.com/p/200mm-players-may-soon-be-underpaid-in-nba)
- Revenue side for comparison: $486,524 per current win plus $358,241 per lagged win in 2011 dollars (Berri, Leeds and von Allmen 2015). **[second-hand]** — [Kyle Newcombe](https://kylenewcombe.substack.com/p/marginal-revenue-product-analysis)
- Team-level illustration of pay versus marginal revenue product: on the 2019 champion Raptors, Norman Powell and Serge Ibaka were paid 287% and 216% of estimated MRP, Pascal Siakam 7%, Danny Green 49%. **[estimate, blog]** — [Kyle Newcombe](https://kylenewcombe.substack.com/p/marginal-revenue-product-analysis)

Realised dead money
- Bradley Beal (Suns, July 2025): gave back $13.9M in a buyout; the remainder was waived and stretched over five seasons at $19.4M a year; Suns total stretched dead money more than $23M a year including two smaller contracts (ESPN's Bobby Marks). A team may not carry stretched dead money above 15% of the cap ($23,197,050 on the 2025-26 cap). **[measured: reported contract terms; snippet]** — [Bleacher Report](https://bleacherreport.com/articles/25226771-suns-updated-roster-salary-cap-after-bradley-beals-contract-buyout)
- Damian Lillard (Bucks, July 2025): waived and stretched after an Achilles tear; Milwaukee owes $113M over five seasons (about $22.5M a year). Note this one is injury-triggered, not pure underperformance. **[measured: reported contract terms]** — [Washington Post via Inquirer](https://www.inquirer.com/sixers/joel-embiid-stephen-curry-nba-injuries-costs-20251211.html)
- MSG Sports net provisions for "certain team personnel transactions" (Knicks and Rangers): FY2025 $49.1M, of which waivers and contract terminations $39.2M, player trades $9.8M, season-ending injuries $0.1M; FY2026 a net credit of $0.03M (waivers $2.6M, trades -$2.7M, injuries nil). **[measured]** — [MSG Sports 10-K FY2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001636519/000162828026056812/msgs-20260630.htm)
- Payroll does not buy results: in 2023-24 the Warriors and Suns paid about $245.5M of luxury tax between them without winning a playoff game. **[estimate]** — [Front Office Sports, April 29, 2024](https://frontofficesports.com/article/the-nbas-highest-spending-teams-are-struggling-this-postseason/)

### Inferences
- The cost-per-win series ($3.35M, $3.83M, $4.29M) has grown about 13% a year, in line with payroll growth. Scaling by the 10% cap rises for 2024-25 and 2025-26 would put the 2025-26 benchmark somewhere around $5M per win; I found no published 2025-26 figure, so treat this as a rough extrapolation.
- On these benchmarks a $54.1M max player must produce roughly 11 to 13 wins to break even, a level only a handful of players reach each year. Underperformance on a max contract of even five wins is a $20M to $25M annual shortfall on the payroll-per-win yardstick, comparable to a serious injury. But the same shortfall is worth only a few million dollars on the revenue-per-win yardstick, so the "loss" is mostly an opportunity cost under a hard payroll constraint (cap, tax, aprons), not a cash loss.
- Underperformance is harder to insure than injury because the index is contestable: Win Shares, VORP and EPM disagree, and the owner's own coaching decisions affect minutes and usage. The observable, hard-dollar manifestations are (a) stretch and buyout charges, (b) draft picks attached to dump salary, and (c) tax paid on unproductive salary. MSG Sports's line item shows these can be large and lumpy ($49.1M one year, about zero the next).
- The 15%-of-cap limit on stretched money caps the annual cash run-off of dead contracts per team at about $23M in 2025-26, which bounds the per-year severity of this risk for a product designer.

### Gaps
- No league-wide annual total for dead money or negative surplus value was found for any recent season; the TrueHoop ranking of 2025-26 contracts by Earned Wins per salary dollar is paywalled.
- No published market price per win from free-agent signings (marginal, not average) was found for 2025 or 2026.
- The 2023-24 $4.29M figure and the Beal details come from search summaries, not opened pages.
- I found no systematic count of draft-pick compensation paid to shed bad contracts.

## 8. What is the upside-cost exposure: bonuses, incentives and supermax eligibility triggered by good performance?

### Takeaway
The large exposure is binary and award-driven: an All-NBA, MVP or Defensive Player of the Year selection moves a young star's next contract from 25% to 30% of the cap (worth about $45M to $48M over five years at current cap levels, about $9M a year) or opens a 35% supermax for veterans (five-year values quoted around $345M). Ordinary incentive bonuses are now small and rare, because unearned bonuses count against the aprons: in 2025-26 only a handful were earned, each under $2M.

### Cited Findings
Award-triggered maximum escalators
- Jalen Duren (Pistons), All-NBA Third Team in May 2026: eligible for five years and $287M instead of a maximum of $239.3M, about $9M a year more. He had not signed a rookie extension, so the 30% tier is negotiable rather than automatic. He was the only player whose pay tier changed with the 2026 All-NBA announcement. **[measured: CBA arithmetic as reported]** — [Front Office Sports, May 24, 2026](https://frontofficesports.com/article/all-nba-nod-makes-jalen-duren-eligible-for-287m-deal/). The article's own figures are inconsistent (text implies $47.7M, sub-headline says $37M).
- Cade Cunningham (Pistons), All-NBA in 2025: his rookie extension contained the escalator and rose from $224M to $269M, about $45M over five years. Evan Mobley (Cavaliers) received the same roughly $9M-a-year bump in 2025 for winning Defensive Player of the Year. ### Inferences
- The award escalator is the cleanest insurable event in this whole topic: a binary, publicly announced outcome (media vote) with a payout computable in advance from the cap (5% of cap in year one with 8% raises, about $45M to $48M over five years at a $155M to $165M cap). It also scales with cap growth.
- It is a "good news" loss, positively correlated with team success and revenue, so an owner hedging it is giving up some upside rather than covering a disaster. For a taxpaying team the true cost is a multiple: $9M a year of extra salary at 2025-26 marginal rates of $3.50 to $4.75 costs an additional $31M to $43M a year in tax if the team is in the third or fourth bracket (my arithmetic).
- Health and upside cost are negatively linked through the 65-game rule: a star who misses 18 or more games is normally ineligible for the award that would raise his pay. An injury that costs the owner salary for missed games can simultaneously save the owner a supermax trigger, which partially offsets within a combined product.
- The Pistons carry a stacked version of this risk: two escalators triggered in consecutive years (Cunningham 2025, Duren 2026), roughly $90M+ of extra five-year commitments from awards alone.
- Ordinary incentive bonuses have become immaterial (four earned, $5.1M in total in 2025-26 on the FOS/Marks count) and are not worth designing around.
