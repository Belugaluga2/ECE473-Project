# Existing insurance market for NBA player injury, disability and contract risk (team/owner side), as of November 2026

Reading notes for the report writer:
- "Fetched" means the page itself was read. "Snippet only" means the fact came from a search-result summary and the page was not opened; treat those as leads, not confirmed facts.
- The NBA league-wide program is not published. Every term below comes from press reporting, not from a policy document or the CBA text. The best current source is Sportico (Kurt Badenhausen, July 11, 2025).

## 1. How does the NBA's league-wide temporary total disability (TTD) program work, and how have its terms changed?

### Takeaway
The NBA runs a league-administered TTD program in which each club must insure at least four of its highest-paid players (five in earlier versions, six before 1999). It pays 50% or 80% of salary (team-selected tier) only after 41 consecutive missed regular-season games, subject to a per-game cap of roughly $492,000 in 2024-25. The current carrier and the premium are not public, and I found no evidence that the 2017 or 2023 CBA changed the terms; changes appear to come through league/insurer renewals.

### Cited Findings

Current terms (reported July 2025)
- The NBA oversees a temporary total disability program for teams. Each club must insure at least four of its highest-paid players, ranked by either current-season salary or total remaining salary; teams may insure more. — [Sportico via Yahoo Sports, Jul 11, 2025](https://sports.yahoo.com/article/nba-stars-fall-teams-help-150000419.html); original at [Sportico](https://www.sportico.com/leagues/basketball/2025/nba-injured-stars-salary-insurance-1234861718/)
- Coverage pays only after a player misses 41 consecutive regular-season games; the 41 games can span two seasons. — [Sportico via Yahoo Sports, Jul 11, 2025](https://sports.yahoo.com/article/nba-stars-fall-teams-help-150000419.html)
- The benefit is 50% or 80% of salary depending on the coverage tier the team selects; the higher tier carries a higher annual premium. — [Sportico via Yahoo Sports, Jul 11, 2025](https://sports.yahoo.com/article/nba-stars-fall-teams-help-150000419.html)
- The maximum payout in 2024-25 was roughly $492,000 per game. Earlier versions capped at $175,000 per game, later raised to $275,000 per game. — [Sportico via Yahoo Sports, Jul 11, 2025](https://sports.yahoo.com/article/nba-stars-fall-teams-help-150000419.html)
- A Polish business outlet repeats the same structure (minimum four players, 41 consecutive games across one or two seasons, 50%/80% tiers chosen before the season, $492,000 cap up from $175,000) and adds that the cap is tied to the league maximum salary and changes yearly. It cites no sources and appears to derive from the Sportico piece. — [XYZ.pl, Jul 19, 2025](https://xyz.pl/?p=94529)
- Sportico's article does not name the carrier or broker, the premium, exclusions, or the treatment of playoff games. — [Sportico via Yahoo Sports, Jul 11, 2025](https://sports.yahoo.com/article/nba-stars-fall-teams-help-150000419.html)

Historical terms and carriers
- 2003: the carrier was Trustmark Insurance on a five-year deal expiring after 2003-04; owners voted in January 2003 to extend three years at higher premiums. Each team had to insure its top five earners, down from six under the previous agreement. Coverage started after 41 consecutive games missed from the same injury and paid 80% of salary for each additional game missed. Dallas and Atlanta voted against the extension. — [ESPN, Darren Rovell, Mar 2003](http://www.espn.com/sportsbusiness/s/2003/0311/1521986.html)
- 2003: BWD Group was described as "the NBA's longtime broker" (executive Marc Blumencranz). Trustmark and the league declined to disclose premiums. — [ESPN, Mar 2003](http://www.espn.com/sportsbusiness/s/2003/0311/1521986.html)
- 2003: Mark Cuban on the league policy: "This is no longer really about insurance, but rather a financial management tool." — [ESPN, Mar 2003](http://www.espn.com/sportsbusiness/s/2003/0311/1521986.html)
- 2013: the carrier was MetLife, "which offers the plan to teams annually." Teams had to insure the top five highest-paid players "if the league's insurance issuer offers coverage." A 41-game deductible applied, after which the team recouped 80% of remaining base salary. — [ESPN Chicago, Jon Greenberg, Apr 23, 2013](https://www.espn.com/blog/chicago/bulls/post/_/id/13432/insurance-helps-bulls-foot-roses-bill)
- 2013: The Economist reported the NBA policy "is run by the BWD Group and costs 4% of salaries," is mandatory only for each club's top five players, and "has limited exclusions for pre-existing conditions." — [The Economist, reprinted at CFO.com, Jan 25, 2013](https://www.cfo.com/news/the-economics-of-sports-insurance-claim-game/666530/)
- BWD Group was acquired by NFP in August 2015; in 2020 NFP combined BWD Sports & Entertainment with Team Scotti into one Sports and Entertainment Group. NFP lists the NBA, NHL, MLB, NFL and WNBA among its sports clients. — [Insurance Journal, Aug 3, 2015](https://amp.insurancejournal.com/news/east/2015/08/03/377300.htm); [NFP sports page](https://www.nfp.com/property-and-casualty/specialties/sports)
- A consumer-insurance blog (dated June 2022, content partly older) says premiums for top-tier players "have historically cost 4% to 7% of the player's salary," that roughly 150-160 top-tier players are insured through one company, and that the insurer can name 14 specific player exclusions each season. Low-quality source with no citations for these figures. — [PolicyAdvisor, Jun 2022](https://www.policyadvisor.com/magazine/cost-to-insure-nba)
- A 2011 sports-law article says the NBA "operates a league-administered disability plan funded by team contributions" and that the waiting period "is believed" to be 41 games; it also reports that insurers generally covered 50-70% of a contract in 2004, down from 80% earlier. — [Wong & Deubert, Sports Litigation Alert, Feb 25, 2011](https://sportslitigationalert.com/the-legal-business-aspects-of-career-ending-disability-insurance-policies-in-professional-and-college-sports/)

Player-side league benefit (for contrast)
- July 2020: the NBA and NBPA agreed to raise the league-funded career-ending injury benefit paid to the player to $2.5 million (from about $312,000), for active players up to age 35, on or off the court, paid on top of contract money. ESPN's Bobby Marks noted that clubs already carry a disability policy for top earners "but it protects the teams rather than the players." — [Hoops Rumors, Jul 30, 2020](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2020/07/nba-nbpa-reach-agreements-on-disability-insurance-salary-withholding.html)

### Inferences
- The reported payouts reconcile with the stated formula. Haliburton: $45.6M salary x (41 remaining games / 82) x 80% = $18.24M, matching the reported $18.2M. Rose (2012-13): $16,402,500 x 50% x 80% = $6.561M, matching exactly.
- The per-game cap already binds for supermax players. Tatum: $54.1M x 50% x 80% = $21.6M uncapped, but 41 games x $492,000 = $20.2M, matching Sportico's "just over $20 million." At a $492,000 cap and 80% tier, the cap binds for any salary above about $50.4M ($492,000 x 82 / 0.8). So on the largest contracts the effective reimbursement rate is below 80% even after the waiting period.
- Maximum first-season recovery for a full-season injury is about 40% of salary (80% of the second half), and 0% for anything under 41 consecutive games. The team always retains at least 60% of the first season's salary on an injury that starts on opening night.
- The mandated pool has shrunk (6 → 5 → minimum 4 players per team) while salaries have grown, consistent with capacity/price pressure, though no source states this as the reason.
- 30 teams x 5 players = 150, consistent with the "150-160 players through one company" figure, which suggests that figure dates from the five-player era.
- The program looks like an owner-side league arrangement renewed with the insurer rather than a CBA-negotiated term: the 2003 extension was decided by an owners' vote, and no source located ties a change in terms to the 2017 or 2023 CBA.

### Gaps
- Current carrier and broker (2025-26, 2026-27) not found. The last named carriers are Trustmark (2003) and MetLife (2013); the last named broker is BWD Group (2013), now part of NFP. I believe NFP was itself acquired by Aon in 2024 but did not verify this in a source.
- Current premium not found. The only figures are "4% of salaries" (Economist, 2013) and "4-7%" (unsourced blog). No source gives the premium differential between the 50% and 80% tiers.
- The 2025-26 and 2026-27 per-game caps were not found; $492,000 is the 2024-25 figure.
- Whether the 2017 CBA or 2023 CBA changed any TTD terms: no evidence found either way. The dates on which the cap moved from $175,000 to $275,000 to ~$492,000, and on which the mandate moved from five to four players, are not reported.
- Whether benefits continue for multiple seasons, how a recurrence is treated, and the exact "return to play" rule that resets the 41-game count are not confirmed. ESPN (2003) says coverage "continues until the athlete returns to play 41 consecutive games," which may be garbled; treat as unverified.
- Whether the league policy follows the player when traded was not found.
- Larry Coon's CBA FAQ text on insurance could not be retrieved.

## 2. How do pre-existing-condition exclusions work in practice? Named examples of exclusions and of collections

### Takeaway
The league insurer can exclude a whole player or a specific body part from the mandated pool, after which the team either self-insures or seeks supplemental private cover. Documented exclusions include Embiid's right foot, Stoudemire's knees and Luol Deng's back; documented recoveries include Grant Hill (Magic), Derrick Rose (Bulls), and the 2025 Achilles cases (Haliburton, Tatum).

### Cited Findings

How exclusions work
- "The insurance company can put exclusions on certain players or even body parts"; if that happens the team can opt out of the plan for that player and try to find another policy. — [ESPN Chicago, Apr 23, 2013](https://www.espn.com/blog/chicago/bulls/post/_/id/13432/insurance-helps-bulls-foot-roses-bill)
- The insurer has "traditionally been able to name 14 specific player exclusions each season," which can be specific to a body part and player. Low-quality source; this figure is commonly attributed to Larry Coon's FAQ, which I could not open. — [PolicyAdvisor, Jun 2022](https://www.policyadvisor.com/magazine/cost-to-insure-nba)
- The Economist described the league policy as having "limited exclusions for pre-existing conditions," in contrast to one-off policies with "many exclusions." — [The Economist via CFO.com, Jan 25, 2013](https://www.cfo.com/news/the-economics-of-sports-insurance-claim-game/666530/)
- Insurers generally require detailed medical histories and sometimes insurer-directed exams; inaccurate disclosure can void cover (Boston Mutual v. N.Y. Islanders, 1st Cir. 1999). The NBA uniform contract also has a prior-injury exclusion form that lets the team limit its own guarantee. — [Wong & Deubert, Sports Litigation Alert, Feb 25, 2011](https://sportslitigationalert.com/the-legal-business-aspects-of-career-ending-disability-insurance-policies-in-professional-and-college-sports/)

Named exclusions / uninsured cases
- Joel Embiid (76ers, 2015): contract was insured as one of the team's five highest salaries, but his right foot was excluded as a pre-existing injury (surgery the week before the 2014 draft). The 76ers would pay $9M over his first two seasons with no recovery. Reported by ESPN's Darren Rovell citing sources. — [FOX Sports, Aug 17, 2015](https://www.foxsports.com/stories/nba/insurance-wont-cover-joel-embiids-foot-injury-for-sixers)
- Had the foot been insured, the 76ers would have recovered roughly 60%, or $5.4M. Snippet only, from a forum repost of the ESPN story. — [Cardboard Connection forum repost](https://community.cardboardconnection.com/forums/forum/hobby-talk/basketball-talk/64565-joel-embiid-out-for-2015-16-nba-season)
- Amar'e Stoudemire (Knicks, 2010, ~$100M deal): knees deemed uninsurable under the league plan; an industry source said the Knicks found supplemental coverage for his knees and eyes, still with a 41-consecutive-game threshold. — [ESPN Chicago, Apr 23, 2013](https://www.espn.com/blog/chicago/bulls/post/_/id/13432/insurance-helps-bulls-foot-roses-bill)
- Luol Deng (Bulls, 2008-09): excluded due to a history of back injuries, per cbafaq.com as cited by ESPN. — [ESPN Chicago, Apr 23, 2013](https://www.espn.com/blog/chicago/bulls/post/_/id/13432/insurance-helps-bulls-foot-roses-bill)
- Andrew Bynum (76ers, 2012-13): team president Rod Thorn said the team had insurance on his salary and that he was on the same league policy as everyone else because he had no pre-existing-condition exclusion. Snippet only. — [SLAM](https://www.slamonline.com/?p=257877)
- Deron Williams (Nets): described as a case where the team paid full salary without insurance assistance. Snippet only, low-quality source; not verified. — [nbablast.com](https://nbablast.com/do-nba-players-get-paid-when-injured/)
- Damian Lillard (Bucks, 2025): after his Achilles tear the Bucks waived him and stretched the remaining $112.6M over five years; it is unclear whether insurance covers any of it and the Bucks declined to comment. — [Sportico via Yahoo Sports, Jul 11, 2025](https://sports.yahoo.com/article/nba-stars-fall-teams-help-150000419.html)

Named collections
- Grant Hill (Magic): seven-year, $93M contract signed 2000; by March 2003 the Magic "will have collected at least $15M" for missed games. Per-game coverage rose from $94,243 (2000-01) to $117,780 (2002-03) and was scheduled to reach $164,892 (2006-07). — [ESPN, Mar 2003](http://www.espn.com/sportsbusiness/s/2003/0311/1521986.html)
- Antonio McDyess (Knicks, 2002-03): Knicks to collect $5.4M. Terrell Brandon (Timberwolves, 2002-03): about $4.5M of a $10.2M salary. — [ESPN, Mar 2003](http://www.espn.com/sportsbusiness/s/2003/0311/1521986.html)
- Derrick Rose (Bulls, 2012-13): salary $16,402,500; Bulls to recover $6.561M. He "was easily approved by the league plan given his age and lack of prior injury history." — [ESPN Chicago, Apr 23, 2013](https://www.espn.com/blog/chicago/bulls/post/_/id/13432/insurance-helps-bulls-foot-roses-bill)
- Tyrese Haliburton (Pacers, 2025-26): insurance may cover up to $18.2M of $45.6M guaranteed salary, assuming the 80% tier. — [Sportico via Yahoo Sports, Jul 11, 2025](https://sports.yahoo.com/article/nba-stars-fall-teams-help-150000419.html)
- Jayson Tatum (Celtics, 2025-26): salary $54.1M; Boston "will collect just over $20 million based on maximum coverage." — [Sportico via Yahoo Sports, Jul 11, 2025](https://sports.yahoo.com/article/nba-stars-fall-teams-help-150000419.html)
- Kyrie Irving (Mavericks, $36.6M) and Dejounte Murray (Pelicans, $26.8M) were listed as likely partial-recovery cases because their missed games at the end of 2024-25 (20 and 33 respectively) count toward the 41. — [Sportico via Yahoo Sports, Jul 11, 2025](https://sports.yahoo.com/article/nba-stars-fall-teams-help-150000419.html)
- XYZ.pl states Boston and Indiana chose the higher tier and that Dallas chose the lower (50%) tier for Irving. No source is given and I could not confirm it in English-language reporting. — [XYZ.pl, Jul 19, 2025](https://xyz.pl/?p=94529)
- Lonzo Ball (Bulls, 2023-24): K.C. Johnson of NBC Sports Chicago said insurance was paying the bulk of his contract. Snippet only; the "80%" figure seen online comes from reader comments, not reporting. — [Bleacher Report](https://bleacherreport.com/articles/10105021-nba-insider-conceivable-bulls-use-lonzo-balls-contract-for-potential-trade)

### Inferences
- Exclusions are applied at the worst moment for the team: the players whose bodies carry known risk (Embiid's foot, Stoudemire's knees) are exactly the ones carved out, so the league program covers "unexpected" injury to previously healthy stars and leaves known-risk contracts to self-insurance or thin supplemental markets.
- Because recoveries depend on consecutive games and the first 41 can be banked at the end of the prior season (Irving, Murray), late-season injuries are relatively better insured than opening-night injuries in first-season terms.
- Teams almost never confirm their insurance position. Lillard, Tatum and Ball are all cases where outsiders are inferring from the program rules.

### Gaps
- No confirmed, primary-sourced list of which players or body parts are excluded in any recent season (2023-24 through 2026-27). Exclusions are not published.
- Could not verify the "14 exclusions per season" figure in a primary source, nor whether it still applies with the four-player minimum.
- No confirmed 2025-26 or 2026-27 claim amounts actually paid (all figures are projections from July 2025). Whether Tatum's actual return date reduced Boston's recovery was not found.
- Chris Bosh (Heat), Klay Thompson (Warriors), John Wall, Ben Simmons and Kawhi Leonard are frequently cited online as insured or uninsured cases; targeted searches returned nothing citable.
- A Hoops Rumors item citing Bobby Marks said insurance money gave Brooklyn's ownership relief but did not affect the tax bill; I could not locate the specific article or the player it referred to.

## 3. What private-market products exist beyond the league program? Premiums, limits, capacity

### Takeaway
Beyond the league TTD pool, teams can buy one-off contract-protection (TTD), permanent total disability, death/life cover and, in principle, "key-man" playoff-miss cover from the specialty accident and health market centred on Lloyd's, placed by a handful of sports brokers. Public pricing is sparse: the best data points put team-side contract cover at roughly 2-7% of insured salary per year, one-off policies carry more exclusions, and terms rarely exceed three years.

### Cited Findings

Product types
- Permanent total disability (career-ending; usually bought by players; typically requires a finding that the athlete can never return and a 12-month inability to play); temporary total disability (usually bought by teams to offset guaranteed salary); key-man (pays a team that would likely have made the playoffs but missed because a star was injured); loss-of-value and loss-of-draft (player-side). — [Wong & Deubert, Sports Litigation Alert, Feb 25, 2011](https://sportslitigationalert.com/the-legal-business-aspects-of-career-ending-disability-insurance-policies-in-professional-and-college-sports/)
- Teams typically rely on two policy types, PTD and TTD; policies can run above $1M a year depending on sport, contract value, age and condition; teams usually insure a share of guaranteed salary rather than all of it. Brokers named as building custom policies with various underwriters: Marsh, K&K Insurance, NFP, HUB International, Insurance Office of America, American Specialty Insurance. — [Beyond The Sideline, May 15, 2025](https://beyondthesideline.beehiiv.com/p/easing-the-pain-with-insurance)
- NFP says it places "all forms of contractual protection, including coverage for disability, death and loss of value." — [NFP sports page](https://www.nfp.com/property-and-casualty/specialties/sports)
- Death cover is not uniform in the NBA. After Brandon Clarke's death in May 2026, the Grizzlies terminated his contract on July 3, 2026 and will not pay the final year ($12.5M for 2026-27) because that money was not protected for death; it also comes off the cap. Teams differ in how they insure contracts against death; where a team insures, the insurer reimburses the team for remaining guaranteed money, which goes to the estate. — [Front Office Sports, Aug 13, 2026](https://frontofficesports.com/brandon-clarkes-nba-contract-death/)

Market structure
- Lloyd's is the dominant market for specialist sports personal-accident cover, mainly career-ending injury and temporary disability. Snippet only. — [Insurance Business UK](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/uk/news/breaking-news/rooneys-hair-policy-shows-the-gap-between-headline-cover-and-real-sports-insurance-592222.aspx)
- Named market participants: Watkins syndicate at Lloyd's (underwriter Jonathan Thomas); Team Scotti (lead insurer/broker on Alex Rodriguez's $275M Yankees contract); BWD Group (NBA program). — [The Economist via CFO.com, Jan 25, 2013](https://www.cfo.com/news/the-economics-of-sports-insurance-claim-game/666530/)
- Aon's entertainment group (Lori Shaw) and Lockton's London sports division (Michael Owen) are active in athlete disability; Owen said in 2015 that more insurers were writing sports business, pushing rates and commissions down, and that limits were growing with salaries. He cited an $8M NFL claim and a GBP 6M Premier League claim. — [Insurance Journal, Mar 13, 2015](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2015/03/13/360512.htm)
- Loss-of-value cover is mostly written through Lloyd's, including via coverholder International Specialty Insurance; specialist brokers named include Exceptional Risk Advisors and Petersen International Underwriters. Snippet only. — [Insurance Business America](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/guides/what-is-athlete-lossofvalue-insurance-161625.aspx)
- Athletes have sued Lloyd's syndicates alleging that only a binder was issued after premium payment and that policy terms were issued or changed after injury. Snippet only. — [Claims Journal, Dec 3, 2019](https://www.claimsjournal.com/news/national/2019/12/03/294340.htm)

- One-off club policies "carry higher premiums, many exclusions, and rarely run longer than three years." ### Inferences
- A rough working range for team-side contract TTD cover is 2-7% of insured salary per year, with the league pool at the low end because of risk pooling and the long 41-game deductible. This is a synthesis of dated and partly weak sources, not a quoted market rate.
- At 4% on roughly $45-55M supermax salaries, the premium for one star is on the order of $2M per year, against a maximum first-season recovery of about $18-20M. That implies the insurer is pricing a 41-plus consecutive-game absence at roughly a 10% annual probability before loadings. This is my arithmetic, not a sourced figure.
- The private market is structurally short-dated (three years or less) while NBA max contracts run four to five years, so any private top-up has renewal risk exactly when a player's injury history worsens.
- Named private placements for NBA teams are almost never disclosed; the Stoudemire supplemental cover is the only named NBA example found.

### Gaps
- No current (2024-2026) premium rate for NBA team-side cover from a primary or trade-press source. No Lloyd's syndicate-level capacity figure for a single NBA player or a single team's accumulation.
- No insurance trade press (Insurance Insider, Reinsurance News, Artemis, Business Insurance) article on the NBA program or on reinsurance/ILS transfer of athlete disability risk was found.
- No evidence found of an NBA team buying "key-man" playoff-miss cover, critical-illness cover, or revenue cover tied to a star. The key-man product is described in a 2011 law article as an emerging concept, with a hypothetical (Brady 2008) rather than a placed policy.
- Could not verify the Cliff Lee premium on the page itself, nor a search-snippet claim that MLB teams spend over $55M per year on individual contract policies (attribution unclear between a Fangraphs community post and Risk & Insurance).

## 4. How do CBA mechanisms interact with insurance?

### Takeaway
Insurance is cash relief to ownership only: the injured player's salary generally stays on the cap and tax books, and the CBA's own tools (Disabled Player Exception, career-ending salary removal) are roster/cap relief, not money. Rules aimed at availability (65-game awards rule, Player Participation Policy fines) add costs and contract-value swings around missed games that insurance does not touch.

### Cited Findings

Disabled Player Exception (DPE)
- A DPE requires an NBA-designated physician to find the player "substantially more likely than not" to be out through June 15 of that league year. It is worth the lesser of 50% of the injured player's salary or the non-taxpayer mid-level exception ($12,822,000 in 2024-25). Apply by January 15; use by March 10. It covers one player only: a rest-of-season free-agent deal, or a trade/waiver acquisition of a player in the final year of his contract (trade limit is DPE amount plus $100,000). It does not create a roster spot. If the injured player returns early the team may keep both; if he returns or is traded before the DPE is used, it is forfeited. — [Hoops Rumors Glossary, Aug 24, 2024](https://hoopsrumors.com/2024/08/hoops-rumors-glossary-disabled-player-exception-3.html)
- The non-taxpayer MLE cap on the DPE was $14.1M for 2025-26. — [Sportico via Yahoo Sports, Jul 11, 2025](https://sports.yahoo.com/article/nba-stars-fall-teams-help-150000419.html)
- Whatever portion of a DPE is used counts against both the cap and the luxury tax. Snippet only (page paywalled on fetch). — [Spotrac](https://www.spotrac.com/news/_/id/1670/what-is-the-nba-disabled-player-exception)
- 2025-26 DPEs: Pacers $14,104,000 (Haliburton, granted July 2025); Rockets $12,500,000 (VanVleet, applied October 2025); Bulls $2,714,760 (Essengue); Clippers $2,677,000 (Beal); Mavericks $2,626,680 (Lively) and $1,148,137 (Exum); Thunder $2,327,520 (Sorber); Wizards $1,769,880 (Whitmore, January 2026). Many DPEs go unused because the rules are restrictive and amounts small. — [Hoops Rumors, Nov 28, 2025, updated](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2025/11/2025-26-nba-disabled-player-exceptions.html)
- The Bulls received a $10.2M DPE for Lonzo Ball in July 2023. Snippet only. — [Sportsnaut](https://sportsnaut.com/nba/report-bulls-get-10-2m-injury-exception-for-lonzo-ball)

Career-ending injury or illness salary removal
- A team can apply for cap relief one year after the player's last game; an independent physician or the Fitness to Play panel must rule that a return would be a medically unacceptable risk; the player still receives his remaining salary. Example: Timofey Mozgov's $16.72M (stretched at about $5.57M per year over three seasons) was removed from Orlando's books in November 2019, moving the Magic from $2.3M to $7.8M below the tax line. — [Hoops Rumors, Nov 27, 2019](https://hoopsrumors.com/2019/11/mozgovs-remaining-salary-removed-from-magics-books.html)
- If the player later plays 25 or more NBA games in a season, the excluded salary is reinstated. Snippet only; source page not identified and 2023 CBA status not confirmed. — [RealGM CBA Encyclopedia](https://basketball.realgm.com/analysis/240416/CBA-Encyclopedia-Disabled-Player-Exception)
- The NBA denied the Lakers' career-ending injury application on money owed to Luol Deng. Snippet only. — [CBS Sports](https://www.cbssports.com/nba/news/nba-denies-lakers-career-ending-injury-exemption-application-on-money-owed-to-luol-deng-per-report)
- In 2003 the Magic's cap relief on Grant Hill "would come no earlier than mid-January 2005, two years after Hill's last game" (older CBA: two-year wait). — [ESPN, Mar 2003](http://www.espn.com/sportsbusiness/s/2003/0311/1521986.html)

Cap and tax treatment of insured salary
- Insurance money gave Brooklyn's ownership financial relief "but it won't affect Brooklyn's end-of-season tax bill," per Bobby Marks. Snippet only; specific article not located. — [Hoops Rumors author archive](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/author/luke-adams/page/847)
- Contrast: in the NFL, insurance recoveries are treated as a refund from the player and generate salary-cap credit the following season; premiums do not count against the cap. — [ESPN, Sep 18, 2024](https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/41274295/nfl-insurance-policies-star-players-aaron-rodgers-tua-tagovailoa-jared-goff-joe-burrow-christian-mccaffrey)
- Waive-and-stretch is used as an alternative to carrying an injured star: the Bucks stretched Lillard's $112.6M over five years, which XYZ.pl says freed over $30M per year. — [Sportico via Yahoo Sports, Jul 11, 2025](https://sports.yahoo.com/article/nba-stars-fall-teams-help-150000419.html); [XYZ.pl, Jul 19, 2025](https://xyz.pl/?p=94529)
- Teams can shift risk to the player by contract: Lonzo Ball's deal reportedly included an Exhibit 3 prior-injury exclusion that would release the team from the guarantee on re-injury. Snippet only. — [Hoops Rumors, Jul 2025](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2025/07/cap-notes-bulls-nuggets-harden-minott-mles.html)

65-game rule (awards eligibility)
- The rule dates to the 2023 CBA and 2023-24 season: at least 20 minutes in 63 games and at least 15 minutes in two more to qualify for MVP, All-NBA and most other awards. There is a season-ending-injury exception at 62 games and an "Extraordinary Circumstances Challenge." Snippet only. — [Hoops Rumors, Apr 2026](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2026/04/nba-deems-luka-doncic-cade-cunningham-award-eligible.html)
- April 2026: Luka Doncic and Cade Cunningham were granted eligibility on appeal despite missing the threshold; Anthony Edwards was reported as denied. The NBPA has called the rule an "arbitrary and overly rigid quota"; Adam Silver says it is working. Snippet only. — [Hoops Rumors, Apr 2026](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2026/04/nba-deems-luka-doncic-cade-cunningham-award-eligible.html); [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/article/cunningham-edwards-nba-season-awards-65-game-rule-eligibility/)

Player Participation Policy (PPP)
- Adopted September 2023: teams may not rest more than one healthy "star" (All-Star or All-NBA in the previous three seasons) in a game and must play healthy stars in national TV and in-season tournament games. — [Front Office Sports, Sep 11, 2023](https://frontofficesports.com/nba-teams-could-be-fined-millions-for-resting-stars/)
- Fines: $100,000 for a first violation, $250,000 for a second, and $1M more than the previous fine for each subsequent violation. First enforcement: Nets fined $100,000 (January 2024) for a December 27, 2023 game. Snippet only. — [KSL/AP, Jan 2024](https://www.ksl.com/article/50835052)
- 2025-26 enforcement: Cavaliers fined $100,000 for resting Donovan Mitchell and Evan Mobley (Nov 12 game) and then $250,000 for resting Darius Garland in a national TV game (Nov 24 game); the Jazz were fined $100,000 over Lauri Markkanen. Snippet only; I take the year as 2025 from context. — [theScore](https://thescore.com/nba/news/3402131/nba-fines-cavs-100-k-for-resting-all-stars-mitchell-mobley); [Rapid City Post](https://rapidcitypost.com/nba-fines-cavaliers-250000-for-resting-all-star-darius-garland-in-a-nationally-televised-game/); [KSL](https://www.ksl.com/article/51273996/nba-fines-jazz-100000-for-violating-player-participation-policy)
- PPP exceptions exist for injuries, personal reasons, and pre-approved absences based on age, career workload or serious injury history. Snippet only. — [Hoops Rumors, Nov 2025](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2025/11/nba-stars-affected-by-player-participation-policy-in-2025-26.html)

### Inferences
- The owner's exposure on an injured star has three separate layers, each handled by a different mechanism: cash salary (partly insured after 41 games), cap/tax room (not relieved except via the career-ending rule after a year), and replacement talent (DPE capped at $14.1M and often unusable for tax or hard-cap teams). Insurance addresses only the first.
- For a tax-paying team, the injured player's salary continues to drive luxury tax and apron status while he sits. The tax cost attached to an unusable salary is an uninsured loss that can exceed the insurance recovery for deep-tax teams. This is my inference; no source quantifies it.
- The 41-consecutive-game insurance trigger, the June 15 DPE test and the one-year career-ending test are all long-duration thresholds. Nothing in the CBA or the league policy responds to absences in the 1-40 game range, which is where most star absences fall.
- The 65-game rule creates a cliff in player compensation (supermax and award-linked escalators) and thus in team payroll; a product that pays on games-played thresholds would hedge an exposure the current market ignores. Inference only.
- PPP fines are small relative to salaries and are a penalty rather than an insurable loss, but they do put a price on discretionary rest that a hedging product would need to avoid rewarding.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve 2023 CBA text or Larry Coon's FAQ for the exact career-ending rule under the current CBA (waiting period, games thresholds for reinstatement, whether tax is also relieved). The commonly cited "10 games" threshold is unverified here.
- No primary source confirming that NBA insurance recoveries are excluded from cap and tax computations; this is the common understanding and one unlocated Marks citation.
- Whether waiving or stretching an insured player (the Lillard case) preserves the team's TTD claim was not found.
- No source found on how insurance proceeds are treated in Basketball Related Income.

## 5. What is NOT insurable or not insured today?

### Takeaway
By construction the league program pays nothing for absences under 41 consecutive regular-season games, for playoff absences, for excluded body parts, for players outside the insured top four or five, or for any non-injury loss. I found no evidence of any placed NBA team policy covering underperformance, load management, revenue loss or franchise value, and the uninsured "empty salary" pool is very large.

### Cited Findings
- The trigger is 41 consecutive regular-season games, so shorter or intermittent absences generate no recovery. — [Sportico via Yahoo Sports, Jul 11, 2025](https://sports.yahoo.com/article/nba-stars-fall-teams-help-150000419.html)
- Only a minimum of four players per team must be insured; extra players are optional. — [Sportico via Yahoo Sports, Jul 11, 2025](https://sports.yahoo.com/article/nba-stars-fall-teams-help-150000419.html)
- Recovery is capped per game (about $492,000 in 2024-25) and at 50% or 80% of salary. — [Sportico via Yahoo Sports, Jul 11, 2025](https://sports.yahoo.com/article/nba-stars-fall-teams-help-150000419.html)
- Pre-existing body parts can be carved out (Embiid's foot: $9M over two seasons unrecovered). — [FOX Sports, Aug 17, 2015](https://www.foxsports.com/stories/nba/insurance-wont-cover-joel-embiids-foot-injury-for-sixers)
- "Empty salary" (pay owed to injured players for games missed): about $79M in 2015-16; more than $352M in 2024-25; the 30 highest-paid players alone were projected to cost teams more than $525M in 2025-26. Stephen Curry earns $59.6M, Joel Embiid $55.2M, Kawhi Leonard $50M. — [Washington Post (Ben Golliver) via Philadelphia Inquirer, Dec 11, 2025](https://www.inquirer.com/sixers/joel-embiid-stephen-curry-nba-injuries-costs-20251211.html)
- March 2026: the Warriors had about $193.5M of salary sidelined at once (over 93% of payroll), the 76ers about $160.2M, the Kings $132.3M. Games missed to injury or illness in 2024-25 were 6,779. Student law blog citing MSN, Marca and The Athletic. — [UB Law Sports & Entertainment Forum, Mar 29, 2026](https://ublawsportsforum.com/2026/03/29/what-the-warriors-193-million-injury-crisis-reveals-about-the-nbas-broken-model/)
- NBA teams paid a reported $1.3B to injured players. Snippet only (headline); season and method not verified. — [ClutchPoints](https://clutchpoints.com/nba/nba-stories/nba-teams-paid-stunning-1-3-billion-for-injured-players)
- The only team-side product in the literature that pays on a sporting or revenue outcome rather than salary is "key-man" cover for a team that misses the playoffs because of a star's injury, described in 2011 as a relatively new arrangement. — [Wong & Deubert, Sports Litigation Alert, Feb 25, 2011](https://sportslitigationalert.com/the-legal-business-aspects-of-career-ending-disability-insurance-policies-in-professional-and-college-sports/)
- Death is not automatically covered: Clarke's final-year $12.5M was not protected and practice varies by team. — [Front Office Sports, Aug 13, 2026](https://frontofficesports.com/brandon-clarkes-nba-contract-death/)
- Healthy rest is regulated by fines, not insured (see PPP in section 4). — [Front Office Sports, Sep 11, 2023](https://frontofficesports.com/nba-teams-could-be-fined-millions-for-resting-stars/)

### Inferences
Gap map for product design (inferred from the terms above; no source lists these gaps explicitly):

| Exposure | Covered today? | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Salary for games 1-41 of any absence | No | 41-consecutive-game waiting period |
| Intermittent or recurring absences (for example 30 games, return, 30 more) | No | "Consecutive" requirement |
| Salary above the per-game cap (salaries above about $50M at the 80% tier) | No | $492,000 per-game cap, 2024-25 |
| The uninsured 20% or 50% coinsurance share | No | Tier structure |
| Players outside the insured top four or five | Only if team opts in | Minimum-four mandate |
| Excluded body parts and excluded players | No, unless supplemental private cover is found | Embiid, Stoudemire, Deng |
| Playoff games missed | No | Trigger and benefit are defined on regular-season games; salary is paid for the regular season |
| Load management or healthy rest | No | Not a disability; subject to PPP fines |
| Underperformance or decline without injury | No | Disability trigger only; no product found |
| Luxury tax and apron cost attached to dead salary | No | Insurance is cash only; cap/tax unchanged |
| Lost gate, local media, sponsorship or playoff revenue when a star is out | No evidence of any placed NBA policy | Only the 2011 key-man concept |
| Loss of franchise value or competitive window | No | No product found |
| Death (remaining guaranteed salary) | Varies by team and contract | Clarke, 2026 |

- Playoff exposure is the clearest uninsured tail: the three 2025 Achilles tears (Tatum, Haliburton, Lillard) all happened in the playoffs, where the team bears the competitive and revenue loss immediately and the insurance clock only starts with the next regular season.
- The scale mismatch is large: with $352M-plus of empty salary in 2024-25 and a program that only responds past 41 consecutive games for four or five players per team, most injury-related salary loss is retained by owners. I could not find a figure for total league-wide insurance recoveries to quantify the retained share.

### Gaps
- No source states total annual claims paid under the NBA program, so the insured share of empty salary cannot be computed.
- No source confirms explicitly how playoff games are treated by the league policy; the "regular-season games" wording is from Sportico's description of the trigger.
- No evidence found, in either direction, of NBA teams buying contingency or revenue cover linked to player availability. Absence of reporting is not proof that no such policy exists, since teams do not disclose policies.
- A 2026 statistic that about 28% of all NBA salary went to players on the injured list appeared in a search snippet with unclear attribution; not used.

## 6. Comparable practice in other leagues showing designs the NBA lacks

### Takeaway
The NFL shows bespoke per-contract policies with waiting periods as short as one game and cap credit for recoveries; MLB shows event-specific cover (World Baseball Classic) with explicit, published-in-practice underwriting rules that leave older and "chronic" players uninsurable; FIFA's Club Protection Programme shows a governing-body-funded scheme with a 28-day waiting period, a daily indemnity and a per-player cap. Each has a feature the NBA program lacks.

### Cited Findings

NFL (ESPN, September 2024)
- Insurance is optional and club-by-club; policies are usually TTD and are documented in an insurance addendum to the player contract. Clubs need not file policies with the league or NFLPA. Premiums do not count against the cap; recoveries create cap credit the following season (a CBA provision since 2006). — [ESPN, Kalyn Kahler, Sep 18, 2024](https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/41274295/nfl-insurance-policies-star-players-aaron-rodgers-tua-tagovailoa-jared-goff-joe-burrow-christian-mccaffrey)
- Waiting periods vary: some policies pay after one missed game, cheaper ones only after the eighth consecutive missed game. — [ESPN, Sep 18, 2024](https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/41274295/nfl-insurance-policies-star-players-aaron-rodgers-tua-tagovailoa-jared-goff-joe-burrow-christian-mccaffrey)
- Insured amounts (2024): Jalen Hurts $63.75M of $110M guaranteed; A.J. Brown $29.8M of $51M; Dak Prescott $87.1M; Trevor Lawrence about $75M; Tua Tagovailoa up to $49.3M with concussions likely excluded (two unnamed sources); Christian McCaffrey up to $15M. Jared Goff's $113M guarantee was uninsured; the Jets did not insure $37M of Aaron Rodgers' 2023 guarantees. — [ESPN, Sep 18, 2024](https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/41274295/nfl-insurance-policies-star-players-aaron-rodgers-tua-tagovailoa-jared-goff-joe-burrow-christian-mccaffrey)
- Cost: about $1-2M per year to insure $40-50M; premiums up an estimated 30-40% over five years; the number of insured contracts doubled in five years; two main brokers (unnamed). Bears, Colts, Panthers, Steelers and Jets were reported as not buying. Cap credits: 49ers $54.3M over ten years (includes other credits), Eagles $19.1M over ten years, Browns $4.1M in 2024 (Watson). The NFL Management Council has discussed eliminating the cap relief. — [ESPN, Sep 18, 2024](https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/41274295/nfl-insurance-policies-star-players-aaron-rodgers-tua-tagovailoa-jared-goff-joe-burrow-christian-mccaffrey)

MLB
- No league-wide program in 2003; teams bought individually and MLB had hired BWD Group and another firm to seek a league-wide policy. — [ESPN, Mar 2003](http://www.espn.com/sportsbusiness/s/2003/0311/1521986.html)
- Team TTD waiting periods are 60 days, 90 days (most common) or full season. — [Wong & Deubert, Sports Litigation Alert, Feb 25, 2011](https://sportslitigationalert.com/the-legal-business-aspects-of-career-ending-disability-insurance-policies-in-professional-and-college-sports/)
- The Diamondbacks could not obtain a policy on a pitcher (reported as Brandon Webb) without an exclusion for arm, shoulder or elbow injuries. Snippet only. — [Fangraphs Community](https://community.fangraphs.com/insurance-in-baseball-is-like-a-black-hole)
- World Baseball Classic: MLB and the MLBPA agreed a single insurer/broker, NFP, the only option since the WBC began. In 2023 it covered Edwin Diaz's $17.25M salary and part of Jose Altuve's. For 2026, NFP no longer insures players once they turn 37, and players classed as "chronic" (60-plus days on the injured list in a prior season, offseason surgery, more than one career surgery, on the IL on the last day of August, and similar triggers) are hard to cover. Denied for 2026: Francisco Lindor, Carlos Correa, Jose Altuve, Jose Alvarado, Mike Trout; Shohei Ohtani unlikely to be insured as a pitcher. Arizona chose to carry Carlos Santana's $2M uninsured. — [ESPN (Alden Gonzalez) via TSN, Mar 6, 2026](https://www.tsn.ca/mlb/article/how-insurance-mess-shaped-2026-world-baseball-classic-n1-48112059/)
- WBC cover typically insures two years of salary for position players and four years for pitchers. Snippet only. — [MLB Trade Rumors, Feb 2026](https://www.mlbtraderumors.com/2026/02/team-puerto-rico-considering-withdrawal-from-world-baseball-classic-amid-insurance-issues.html)

NHL
- The NHL has a league-purchased disability program (2003). Waiting period 30 games; 2002 premium about $6.50 per $1,000 insured. — [ESPN, Mar 2003](http://www.espn.com/sportsbusiness/s/2003/0311/1521986.html); [Wong & Deubert, 2011](https://sportslitigationalert.com/the-legal-business-aspects-of-career-ending-disability-insurance-policies-in-professional-and-college-sports/)
- Whether a contract is insured affects trade value: in the Clarkson-for-Horton trade the Leafs could absorb Horton's uninsured contract and the Blue Jackets could not. — [PolicyAdvisor, Jun 2022](https://www.policyadvisor.com/magazine/cost-to-insure-nba)

European football and the FIFA Club Protection Programme (CPP)
- FIFA Circular no. 1852 (July 29, 2023) extended the CPP to cover 2023-2026 with a revised technical bulletin. Snippet only. — [LawInSport](https://lawinsport.com/topics/news/item/circular-no-1852-fifa-club-protection-programme-2023-2026)
- The CPP compensates clubs up to EUR 7,500,000 per player per accident, calculated at a daily pro rata rate of up to EUR 20,548 (1/365), payable for a maximum of 365 days. Snippet of the FIFA technical bulletin; the PDF redirected and was not opened. — [FIFA technical bulletin hosted by ECA/EFC](https://www.efcfootball.com/media/2786/technical-bulletin-fifa-club-protection-programme.pdf)

- The FIFA Club Benefits Programme (a per-day payment to clubs for releasing players to the World Cup, roughly $11,000 per player per day for 2026) is a separate scheme and not insurance. Snippet only. — [FIFA Club Benefits Programme FAQ](https://inside.fifa.com/about-fifa/stakeholders/fifa-club-benefit-programme/fifa-club-benefit-programme-faq)
- Private football cover: the sum insured is normally the player's contract, though asset (transfer) value can be taken into account. Snippet only. — [Insurance Business UK](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/uk/news/breaking-news/rooneys-hair-policy-shows-the-gap-between-headline-cover-and-real-sports-insurance-592222.aspx)

### Inferences
Design features seen elsewhere that the NBA program lacks:

| Feature | Where it exists | NBA today |
|---|---|---|
| Short waiting period (1 to 8 games; 28 days) | NFL bespoke policies; FIFA CPP | 41 consecutive games, about half a season |
| Daily or per-game indemnity from early in the absence | FIFA CPP (daily after day 28); NFL per-game | Per-game only after game 41 |
| Cap credit for insurance recoveries | NFL | None reported; salary stays on cap and tax |
| Team-chosen insured amount per contract, across many players (Eagles: 16-plus players) | NFL | Standardised tiers for a minimum of four players |
| Third party (governing body) funds cover for a specific exposure | FIFA CPP for national-team duty; MLB/MLBPA WBC cover | No equivalent for Olympic or FIBA duty found in this research |
| Asset-value (transfer value) insurance | European football | Not applicable: no transfer fees |
| Explicit, rule-based underwriting cut-offs (age 37; "chronic" definition) | WBC/NFP 2026 | Exclusions exist but are not published |

- The NFL's cap-credit rule is what makes insurance competitively valuable there; in the NBA an insured recovery cannot be redeployed into roster spending beyond the DPE, which weakens the sporting case for buying more cover and leaves it as a pure ownership cash hedge.
- The WBC episode shows the insurability frontier in 2026: a single-broker market declined star players on age and injury-history rules even for a short tournament. Any new NBA product aimed at the players with the most risk (older stars, players with surgical history) should expect the same underwriting wall, which is an argument for parametric or market-based hedges rather than indemnity cover.
- FIFA's 28-day waiting period plus daily indemnity is the closest existing template for a "short-absence" layer that would sit below the NBA's 41-game attachment point.

### Gaps
- Whether the FIFA CPP has been renewed or revised for the period after 2026 was not found; the 2023-2026 terms are the latest confirmed.
- The CPP's insurer/broker and its annual aggregate were not confirmed from the bulletin itself.
- No source found on insurance for NBA players on Olympic or FIBA national-team duty (who pays, limits), which would be the direct analogue of the CPP and WBC cover.
- Nothing citable found on how European clubs insure wage bills in practice (rates, typical waiting periods) beyond a low-quality guide quoting 0.5-2% of sum insured, which I did not use.
- NHL program details are from 2002-2011 sources; current terms not researched.
