# Product designs and precedents for transferring sports performance and athlete health risk (state as of November 2026)

Reader note on evidence quality: items marked "(snippet-level)" come from search-result summaries of the linked page and were not read in full; items marked "(single source)" have no independent confirmation. Everything else was read from the linked page during this research session. The buyer of protection is assumed to be an NBA team owner throughout.

## 1. Parametric (index-based) insurance: trigger design in other fields and applications to sport

### Takeaway
Parametric cover pays on an objective, third-party-observable index instead of an adjusted loss, which removes claims disputes and moral hazard over the loss figure but creates basis risk. In sport, the only live "games-missed" parametric injury product found is the Zurich / Players Health NIL policy for US colleges (trigger: listed injury plus at least 40% of season missed); nothing equivalent was found for NBA teams, and an earlier consumer product (fantasy-football injury insurance, 2014) used a similar games-missed trigger.

### Cited Findings
- Catastrophe bonds use four families of trigger: indemnity (sponsor's own losses, the most common), industry-loss index, parametric (physical measure such as wind speed or earthquake magnitude), and modelled-loss or hybrid triggers that sit in between (snippet-level) — [FINRA investor alert](https://www.finra.org/investors/alerts/catastrophe-bonds-and-other-event-linked-securities); [OECD, Fostering Catastrophe Bond Markets in Asia and the Pacific](https://oecd.org/en/publications/fostering-catastrophe-bond-markets-in-asia-and-the-pacific_ab1e49ef-en/full-report/component-6.html); [Wikipedia: Catastrophe bond](https://www.wikipedia.com/wiki/Catastrophe_bond)
- The standard trade-off: indemnity triggers leave the sponsor the least basis risk but the most moral hazard, because the sponsor controls loss reporting and can change its underwriting after issuance; parametric and index triggers largely remove sponsor influence but leave the most basis risk; modelled-loss triggers are a compromise (snippet-level) — [OECD](https://oecd.org/en/publications/fostering-catastrophe-bond-markets-in-asia-and-the-pacific_ab1e49ef-en/full-report/component-6.html); [arXiv 2609.22052, "Design and pricing of a transparent parametric-modeled loss CAT bond: application to German windstorm"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.22052)
- Industry-loss triggers settle more slowly than parametric ones because the final industry loss takes a long time to compile, which adds uncertainty for investors (snippet-level) — [arXiv 2609.22052](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.22052)
- Zurich North America and Players Health launched a "critical injury protection" policy for the NIL era (reported January 6, 2026). The insured is the college, university or collective; the policy covers "NIL contract payments made or owed" to an athlete who is injured in official play, practice or training and misses a significant portion of the season, "typically 40% or more". It is surplus-lines paper underwritten by Zurich, distributed only through Players Health, and integrated with Teamworks (used by most Division I athletic departments). A later version for athletes' loss of future NIL income is contemplated. Payout schedule, limits and price are not disclosed — [Insurance Journal, Jan 6, 2026](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/midwest/2026/01/06/853263.htm)
- Earlier trade coverage of the same Players Health product describes it as parametric: a list of predetermined injuries is covered, the policy triggers when the athlete has one of those injuries and misses at least 40% of a season, and premiums are set from an injury probability estimated for each athlete (snippet-level) — [Carrier Management, June 5, 2025](https://www.carriermanagement.com/news/2025/06/05/275720.htm)
- A 2014 consumer product, FantasyPlayerProtect (sold via MiniCo Insurance Agency, underwritten by Hudson Insurance Group), paid fantasy-football team owners when a named NFL player missed at least 8 games of a 14- or 15-week season or at least 9 games of a 16- or 17-week season. Premium and payout were not stated in the source, and its later fate is not reported — [Milliman, "Real insurance for fantasy football", July 28, 2014](https://us.milliman.com/en/insight/Real-insurance-for-fantasy-football)
- Event-side parametric and cancellation cover is large and established: FIFA reportedly had about $900 million of event-cancellation cover for Qatar 2022 and about $1 billion for the 2026 World Cup according to Morningstar DBRS (snippet-level) — [Insurance Business, "World Cup final puts $1 billion event insurance market in focus"](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/breaking-news/world-cup-final-puts-1-billion-event-insurance-market-in-focus-582784.aspx)
- An Olympics sponsor used a Guernsey captive to buy $120 million of parametric pandemic cover (headline-level only) — [Artemis](https://www.artemis.bm/news/olympics-sponsor-uses-guernsey-captive-to-buy-120m-parametric-pandemic-cover/)
- Weather-parametric cancellation cover for sports events is marketed by parametric specialists (white paper exists; contents not read) — [Descartes Underwriting, "Event Cancellation Insurance for the Sports Industry Against Adverse Weather"](https://descartesunderwriting.com/whitepaper/event-cancellation-insurance)
- Existing team disability policies already have a quasi-parametric element in their waiting periods: the Mets' policy on David Wright reimbursed 75% of salary once he had missed at least 60 games — [CBS Sports](https://www.cbssports.com/mlb/news/the-david-wright-insurance-mess-is-a-perfect-reason-why-mets-fans-deserve-better-from-ownership); [Fangraphs](https://blogs.fangraphs.com/david-wright-peter-alonso-and-the-law-of-metropolitan-frugality)

### Inferences
- The Players Health design is a "double trigger": (a) a medically verified listed injury and (b) a games-missed index. The first leg limits the insured's ability to manufacture a claim by simply resting a player; the second leg makes the payout amount objective. An NBA version would map naturally onto "listed injury + N games on the inactive list for that injury".
- A pure index on NBA games missed (no medical leg) is highly manipulable by the buyer, because the team decides who is active. Any NBA parametric design needs either a medical leg, an independent data source the team cannot move, or coinsurance.
- Payout schedules could be stepped (lump sum past a threshold, as in Players Health and FantasyPlayerProtect) or linear per game missed after a deductible (as the NBA league disability program is reported to work; see section 4/6). Linear-per-game schedules reduce the cliff incentive to hold a player out for one more game to cross a threshold (the David Wright "60 games" problem).

### Gaps
- No insurer, MGA or startup was found offering a parametric games-missed or days-on-injured-list product to NBA (or other major-league) teams as of November 2026. Absence in search results is not proof of absence; contingency markets are private.
- Payout schedule, limits and rates for the Zurich / Players Health product are not public.
- Weather-index and crop-index trigger design details (strike, exit, tick size; station versus satellite indices) were not sourced in this session beyond the cat-bond trigger taxonomy. The Descartes white paper and the World Bank / BASIS literature (section 7) are the places to pull those from.
- Whether FantasyPlayerProtect survived past 2014 is not documented in the sources found.

## 2. Contingency and prize-indemnity insurance (over-redemption, contractual bonus, prize indemnity, revenue protection)

### Takeaway
Contingency underwriters (Lloyd's syndicates, Tokio Marine HCC, Hedgehog Risk, Howden-placed markets, Catlin/XL historically) routinely insure the "cost of success" (player, coach and sponsor bonuses) and, in some markets, the cost of failure (relegation, missing the Champions League); quoted prices run at a clear multiple of market-implied odds. The most important recent data point for an NBA owner is February 2026: broker Game Point Capital hedged two NBA teams' bonus exposures on Kalshi at 6% and 2% of limit versus OTC insurance quotes of 12-13% and 7-8%.

### Cited Findings
- Definitions: prize indemnity pays when any eligible participant in a public promotion wins; contractual bonus insurance pays when a specific named individual or team earns a contractual bonus. Typical prize-indemnity premiums are quoted at 3% to 15% of prize value (for example $300 to $1,500 for a $10,000 prize), driven by the odds (snippet-level; general reference site) — [SuperMoney](https://www.supermoney.com/encyclopedia/prize-indemnity-insurance); [HUB glossary](https://www.hubinternational.com/en-us/insurance-glossary/p/prize-indemnity-insurance/)
- Underwriters will not take risks whose payout probability is high, and very large prizes can become prohibitively expensive; only a handful of insurers write the class, each contest is individually underwritten, and claims can be denied if a participant had an unfair advantage (snippet-level) — [Insurance Journal, Aug 4, 2025](https://www.insurancejournal.com/magazines/mag-features/2025/08/04/834129.htm); [Business Insurance](https://www.businessinsurance.com/contests-popularity-increases-demand-for-prize-indemnity-cover/)
- Hedgehog Risk (London underwriter-broker formed 2013) took a "significant" strategic investment from SIG Insurance Holdings, a Susquehanna International Group subsidiary, in late January 2025. It writes prize indemnity, contractual bonus and revenue protection and helps cover upwards of $100 million of such risk a year. Contractual bonus caps the "cost of success" (player and staff bonuses, extra postseason costs, often several million dollars); revenue protection covers negative outcomes (relegation, a finals sweep hurting media or ticketing partners) and never covers the full loss. Buyers were historically sponsors; pro teams, college athletic departments and other corporates now buy too. US lead Saul Paine gives the hypothetical of covering the "hit to the balance sheet" if a "highly touted team failed to make the playoffs" — [JohnWallStreet, Mar 11, 2025](https://www.johnwallstreet.com/p/rising-costs-driving-demand-for-contractual-bonus-revenue-protection-insurance)
- NBA example (the most direct precedent): on February 12-13, 2026 Kalshi announced a partnership with sports insurance broker Game Point Capital (CEO Will Hall) to hedge team performance bonuses. Game Point had already executed hedges for two NBA teams: a bonus tied to making the playoffs priced at 6% on Kalshi versus a 12-13% OTC quote, and a bonus for advancing to the second round priced at 2% versus 7-8% OTC. Trade sizes were not disclosed. Kalshi's CEO put the annual sports insurance and reinsurance market at about $9 billion and projected it to double by 2030 (company claim) — [Decrypt, Feb 13, 2026](https://decrypt.co/358000/kalshi-taps-sports-insurance-market-with-game-point-capital-deal-as-regulatory-battles-mount); [The Block, Feb 13, 2026](https://theblock.co/news/deals/2026-02-13-kalshi-inks-sports-hedging-deal-with-game-point-on-the-heels-of-over-1-billion-in-super-bowl-trading-389841)
- College example via exchange: InGame reported (August 2026) that a third-party hedging firm placed roughly $3 million of Kalshi trades to offset bonus exposure tied to LSU football coach Lane Kiffin; the same report says there are few known examples of sports contracts being used to hedge risks that directly involve a team (snippet-level; URL attribution between two InGame items is uncertain) — [InGame](https://www.ingame.com/?p=53319); [InGame](https://www.ingame.com/?p=43848)
- College example via insurance: Clemson paid $272,000 for contractual bonus insurance on coaches' incentives and the coaches received a $1.3 million cheque from the broker (premium roughly 21% of the payout) (snippet-level) — [Insurance Business](https://insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/breaking-news/how-clemson-insured-against-winning-in-college-football-64052.aspx)
- Rugby examples: Lloyd's said in 2007 that England players stood to receive about £60,000 each (over £2 million in total) for retaining the World Cup, and that clubs, governing bodies or sponsors insure such bonuses; in 2011 the RFU paid a premium to cover player and management win bonuses for the World Cup (snippet-level) — [Claims Journal, Oct 15, 2007](https://www.claimsjournal.com/news/international/2007/10/15/84281.htm); [Claims Journal, Apr 22, 2011](https://www.claimsjournal.com/news/international/2011/04/22/184875.htm)
- Negative-outcome cover, football: Borussia Dortmund bought insurance in 2012 against lost revenue from missing the Champions League, renewed three times, placed with about 12 underwriters led by Catlin and XL. One source said Dortmund paid closer to 30% of the insured amount. Underwriters could cancel if coach Juergen Klopp or three top players left mid-year, and only three executives knew of the policy, a confidentiality condition imposed so the coach and players would not ease off. By comparison, Sportsrisq's Tom Mitchell said Real Madrid might pay about €500,000 (5%) for €10 million of cover against missing the Champions League. Paddy Power's Airton Risk Management uses bookmaker odds to help set premiums — [Insurance Journal / Bloomberg, Apr 23, 2015](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/international/2015/04/23/365411.htm)
- Same source on market limits: in England negative-outcome cover was banned in 2012 and clubs may insure only positive outcomes (such as performance bonuses) with FA approval; HCC Specialty's Philip Hall said "The moral risk can be significant" when a club seeks cover against its own poor performance and that he would rather insure a sponsor than a club because clubs know their own risk better — [Insurance Journal / Bloomberg, Apr 23, 2015](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/international/2015/04/23/365411.htm)
- Miller's Steve Moore likewise says Premier League clubs cannot insure the impact of relegation because insurers do not generally offer terms where the trigger is a negative outcome, but can cover positive outcomes such as stay-up bonuses (snippet-level) — [Miller Insurance](https://www.miller-insurance.com/news-and-insights/the-deloitte-football-money-league-the-risks-behind-rising-revenue-in-elite-football/). The first-year revenue hit from Premier League relegation is estimated at £85m-£100m (snippet-level) — [City A.M.](https://cityam.com/?p=2005808)
- Osasuna (La Liga, 2026): the club reportedly paid €1.2 million for relegation insurance placed by Howden; the case was linked in the press to a Kalshi prediction-market position, the club said it bought a standard product and placed no bets, it survived on goal difference on the final matchday and the premium was lost (snippet-level; primary page returned HTTP 403) — [iGamingFuture](https://igamingfuture.com/the-relegation-game-lowly-spanish-laliga-club-osasuna-hedges-via-kalshi/)
- Over-redemption / promotion hedging with betting markets: Jim "Mattress Mack" McIngvale's 2019 Astros refund promotion carried a potential refund bill above $15 million; he bought insurance (reported premium 10-20% of the refund amount, not enough to cover the exposure) and also hedged with sportsbook bets, including $3.5 million at +220 to win $7.7 million; he lost more than $13 million in bets when the Astros lost Game 7. In 2017 he refunded $13 million when the Astros won (snippet-level) — [Las Vegas Review-Journal](https://www.reviewjournal.com/sports/sports-columns/todd-dewey/houston-furniture-store-owner-hedges-promotion-with-bets-on-astros/amp); [Covers](https://www.covers.com/Editorial/Article/902af1c6-e6b7-11e9-a97f-0a73013d6078/Even-with-millions-riding-on-MLB-World-Series-odds-Mattress-Mack-is-all-business); [Action Network](https://www.actionnetwork.com/mlb/mattress-mack-world-series-bets-loses-millions)
- Product pages confirming the class is standard: Tokio Marine HCC and Howden both market "contractual bonus" cover — [TMHCC](https://tmhcc.com/en-us/products/contingency/contractual-bonus); [Howden](https://www.howdengroup.com/uk-en/cover/contractual-bonus-insurance)

### Inferences
- Pricing relative to odds: an exchange price is approximately the market-implied probability, so the Game Point figures imply OTC contingency loadings of about 2x (playoff berth: 12-13% versus 6%) and 3.5-4x (second round: 7-8% versus 2%) over market odds. Loadings are proportionally largest on low-probability triggers. Dortmund's roughly 30% rate-on-line for a club that had just won two titles implies a large adverse-selection and moral-hazard load.
- Insurers are structurally more willing to insure a team's success (the team cannot easily "over-win", and trying to do so is aligned with integrity) than its failure (the team can cause failure). An NBA owner's natural exposures run both ways: success creates bonus, luxury-tax and incentive costs; failure costs playoff gate and local revenue. The "failure" side is where moral hazard objections and tanking incentives bite.
- The Dortmund conditions are a ready-made mitigation template: key-person cancellation clauses, secrecy from players and coaches, partial cover only.
- Market capacity is small: Hedgehog's roughly $100 million a year of covered risk is tiny against one NBA max contract, so large placements need reinsurance, capital markets or exchange liquidity.

### Gaps
- No documented NBA case of a named team's bonus insurance with numbers was found other than the two anonymous Game Point hedges.
- Trade size and available Kalshi depth for the NBA hedges are undisclosed; whether a hedge of tens of millions could be executed near screen price is unknown.
- The Leicester City 2016 title-bonus placement and the Insurance Insider piece on England World Cup prize indemnity appeared only as headlines and were not read.
- The exact payout Osasuna would have received is not in the sources found.

## 3. Capital-markets structures: cat bonds, athlete-earnings securitisation, third-party ownership, outcome derivatives

### Takeaway
Sport has reached the capital markets only episodically: one successful cancellation cat bond (FIFA, 2003), a string of retail athlete-equity ventures that failed for lack of liquidity and fan demand (ProTrade, Fantex, Mojo), private income-share funds that work financially but generate litigation (Big League Advance), and a regulatory ban on the closest football analogue (third-party ownership, 2015). The one structure with real momentum as of 2026 is exchange-traded sports event contracts (Kalshi, Polymarket), which are now being used for team hedging while facing state-level legal challenges.

### Cited Findings
- FIFA World Cup cancellation bond: Golden Goal Finance Ltd issued about $260 million (Artemis lists $262 million) in 2003, structured by CSFB, to cover cancellation of the 2006 World Cup after insurers proved unwilling to write cancellation cover post-9/11. Four tranches, about three-year expected maturity, rated A3 by Moody's, with RMS modelling terrorism risk. It covered cancellation of the final from natural or man-made causes excluding world war and boycott. RMS says the price FIFA paid was lower than the cheapest insurance option and that it remains the only stand-alone securitisation of terrorism risk. The tournament was played and the bond was not triggered (snippet-level) — [Artemis deal directory](https://www.artemis.bm/deal-directory/golden-goal-finance-ltd/); [Insurance Journal, Oct 9, 2003](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/international/2003/10/09/33031.htm); [RMS blog, 2014](https://rms.com/blog/2014/07/16/rms-and-the-fifa-world-cup-insuring-against-terrorism); [PropertyCasualty360, 2006](https://www.propertycasualty360.com/amp/2006/07/14/terror-bonds-prove-their-worth-at-world-cup/)
- Fantex (2013-2017): Fantex bought a share of an athlete's future income and sold a tracking stock linked to that "brand contract"; holders actually owned an interest in Fantex, Inc. as a whole, not the athlete contract. The first IPO was Vernon Davis at $10 a share (November 2013); four stocks existed early (Davis, Alshon Jeffery, Mohamed Sanu, EJ Manuel). Demand for the Manuel IPO was weak; shares traded only on Fantex's own platform, making exit hard; the board could convert tracking stocks into Fantex common stock. A late-2015 filing for a $29.5 million registered offering was dropped in favour of a roughly $59-60 million private placement in mid-2016. Fantex sold shares from 2013 to 2016 and closed the following year (snippet-level except last point) — [ESPN](https://www.espn.com/blog/buffalo-bills/post/_/id/13852/ej-manuels-stock-dropping-literally); [Palisades Hudson](https://palisadeshudson.com/?p=11402); [Law360](https://law360.com/amp/articles/821020); [Front Office Sports, Mar 3, 2024](https://frontofficesports.com/bear-market-for-sacks-and-ints-the-difficult-road-of-sports-stock-markets)
- Conflict between sources: Front Office Sports describes Fantex shares as tied to athletes' endorsement income rather than on-field salary, whereas other coverage describes the purchase of rights to a player's future earnings generally — [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/bear-market-for-sacks-and-ints-the-difficult-road-of-sports-stock-markets); contradicted by [Fortune, 2014](https://fortune.com/2014/06/06/fantex-vernon-davis-buck-french-nfl/) (snippet-level)
- Other athlete "stock markets": ProTrade (2004) could not scale, pivoted to fantasy apps as Citizen Sports and was sold to Yahoo!; Mojo (backed by Alex Rodriguez and Marc Lore) sold or shut much of its business in late 2023; PredictionStrike raised $10 million in fall 2023; Vestible (Kansas, SEC-qualified) planned a March 2024 IPO on Broncos linebacker Baron Browning with $10 shares tied to 1% of salary income, the athlete keeping 80% of IPO proceeds. The article's diagnosis: "an inability to reach large swaths of casual fans has helped prove fatal" — [Front Office Sports, Mar 3, 2024](https://frontofficesports.com/bear-market-for-sacks-and-ints-the-difficult-road-of-sports-stock-markets)
- Big League Advance (income-share fund): Fernando Tatis Jr. received $2 million as a 17-year-old minor leaguer in exchange for 10% of future earnings, with nothing owed if he never reached the majors. Applied to his 14-year, $340 million Padres contract the share is about $34 million. He sued in June 2025 arguing BLA was an unlicensed lender using terms barred by California consumer law; he lost, and in May 2026 was ordered to pay $3.2 million past due plus attorney's fees. His side says the judge characterised the advance as a loan, leaving room for further challenges (snippet-level) — [CBS Sports](https://www.cbssports.com/mlb/news/padres-star-fernando-tatis-jr-sues-big-league-advance-in-effort-to-get-out-of-deal-he-signed-at-17); [Sports Illustrated](https://www.si.com/mlb/padres/why-fernando-tatis-jr-pay-investment-fund-10-percent-career-earnings); [Times of San Diego, May 22, 2026](https://timesofsandiego.com/sports/2026/05/22/fernando-tatis-lawsuit-contract-advance-teen-big-league-advance/)
- Pando Pooling (peer risk pool, 2018-2019): minor-league players form pools; each contributes a fixed share of MLB salary once he has earned at least $1.6 million; members must approve each other; Pando takes 10% of each pool. About 140 players were pooled by 2019 and three had reached the majors. NPR flagged incentive worries (a pitcher easing off against a pool-mate; reduced motivation) (snippet-level) — [Sports Illustrated, Nov 21, 2018](https://www.si.com/mlb/2018/11/21/mlb-prospects-salary-risk-pando-pooling); [NPR via WAMC, Oct 25, 2019](https://www.wamc.org/2019-10-25/some-baseball-players-are-entering-income-pooling-agreements-to-fix-imbalance)
- Third-party ownership (TPO) in football: FIFA banned third-party ownership of players' economic rights from May 1, 2015 (Article 18ter RSTP) on the grounds that outside investors could influence clubs' employment and transfer decisions. RFC Seraing's challenge failed at the Swiss Federal Supreme Court (decision of February 20, 2018), and the Spanish and Portuguese leagues complained to the European Commission. A reported workaround is a floating charge over the accounts into which transfer proceeds are paid, reportedly used by FC Porto (snippet-level; the workaround is from a wiki source) — [Wikipedia: Third-party ownership in association football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third-party_ownership_in_association_football); [Kluwer Arbitration Blog, Oct 1, 2018](https://arbitrationblog.kluwerarbitration.com/2018/10/01/fifa-ban-on-third-party-ownership-a-pyrrhic-victory-for-fifa-in-front-of-the-swiss-federal-supreme-court/); [International Sports Law Journal (Springer)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40318-015-0080-x)
- Sports-outcome derivatives: Kalshi's sports event contracts accounted for more than 80% of its business by early 2026 (Dune data); Super Bowl trading exceeded $1 billion; industry prediction-market volume rose from $15.8 billion in 2024 to about $63.5 billion in 2025 (CertiK). Kalshi argues its contracts are swaps; courts in Massachusetts, Nevada and Connecticut have allowed regulators to pursue temporary bans as unlicensed sports betting, while the CFTC chair said the agency would join suits to assert federal jurisdiction. Kalshi also offers a rebate to sportsbooks buying at least 300,000 contracts in one order for hedging — [Decrypt, Feb 13, 2026](https://decrypt.co/358000/kalshi-taps-sports-insurance-market-with-game-point-capital-deal-as-regulatory-battles-mount); [The Block](https://theblock.co/news/deals/2026-02-13-kalshi-inks-sports-hedging-deal-with-game-point-on-the-heels-of-over-1-billion-in-super-bowl-trading-389841); [InGame](https://www.ingame.com/?p=43848)

### Inferences
- What killed retail athlete securities was the investor side (illiquidity, weak fan demand, counterparty exposure to the issuer), not the athlete side. An owner-facing risk-transfer product does not need retail investors; institutional ILS capital (as in the FIFA bond) or exchange liquidity is the more realistic funding source.
- Income-share deals (BLA, Pando, Vestible) transfer risk from the player to investors. They are the mirror image of what an owner needs, but they show that capital exists to take athlete-outcome risk at prices athletes accept (Tatis: $2 million for an eventual claim of about $34 million), and they show the legal re-characterisation risk (equity-like claim treated as a consumer loan).
- The TPO ban is the cautionary tale for any product giving a financier an economic interest in a specific player: regulators objected to third-party influence over sporting decisions, which is the same integrity concern an NBA-linked hedge raises.
- A cat-bond-style "team availability bond" (principal reduced when league-wide or team-wide games lost to injury exceed a level) has no precedent found; the FIFA bond shows capital markets will take a binary sport-event risk when it is modelled by an independent agent and outside the sponsor's control.

### Gaps
- No source was found for a sports-performance or athlete-injury cat bond; the GlobalCapital headline "Premier League titans vie for catastrophe bond trophy" was not read.
- Transfer-fee receivables financing (for example 23 Capital, Macquarie, Score Capital) returned no usable sources in this session; the collapse of 23 Capital and bank factoring of transfer instalments are not documented here.
- The exact reason for Fantex's 2017 closure (SEC deregistration documents) was not retrieved; only pre-closure structural weaknesses and the Front Office Sports summary are sourced.
- Outcome of Vestible's Browning IPO and current status of Pando Pooling and PredictionStrike are unknown.
- No academic proposal for exchange-listed "team wins futures" for owners was located, although the Kalshi / Game Point use is effectively that.

## 4. Risk sharing already written into NBA player contracts

### Takeaway
NBA contracts already contain bespoke risk-transfer terms that shift health and availability risk back to the player: body-part-specific guarantee carve-outs (Embiid 2017), games-played and weight conditions on future guarantees (Zion Williamson 2022), games-played bonuses classed as "unlikely" (so they stay off the cap until earned), and prior-injury exclusions (Exhibit 3). These terms are free of insurer loadings but are limited by CBA rules, and they only work for players with weak bargaining positions (injury history at signing).

### Cited Findings
- Joel Embiid, October 2017 five-year maximum rookie extension (reported as $146.5 million to $148 million): essentially guaranteed, but the 76ers got protection if he suffered a "contractually specific catastrophic injury" causing him to miss significant time. The language centres on his feet and back; a new injury elsewhere (knee, wrist, shoulder) would give the team no right to waive him for savings. Initial reports said the team could recover up to half the contract. The team's waiver right lapses if Embiid plays 1,650 minutes in three consecutive seasons or in three of four seasons including 2017-18. Separately, the starting salary would rise from 25% to 30% of the cap with First Team All-NBA or MVP in 2017-18 — [Hoops Rumors, Oct 2017](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2017/10/details-on-joel-embiids-contract-extension.html); [6abc / ESPN](https://6abc.com/post/joel-embiids-deal-protects-76ers-in-case-of-contractually-specific-injury/2517902/); [Sports Illustrated, Oct 11, 2017](https://www.si.com/nba/2017/10/11/joel-embiid-max-contract-injury-provisions) (all snippet-level; outlets disagree on totals and on whether pre-existing injuries are covered)
- Insurers took the opposite carve-out on the same player: Embiid's contract was insured but, per anonymous sources, his injured right foot was excluded as pre-existing (snippet-level, anonymous-sourced) — [ABC11 tag page](https://abc11.com/tag/insurance-policy/)
- Zion Williamson, July 2022 five-year maximum extension (about $193 million): includes a conditioning clause under which the sum of his weight in pounds and body-fat percentage must stay below 295, with guaranteed money reducible if not met (one summary says failure requires two consecutive weigh-ins). After he played 29 games in 2022-23 (reported threshold: missing more than 22 games), salary for 2025-26, 2026-27 and 2027-28 became non-guaranteed. Guarantees can be re-earned: 61 games in the prior season plus the weight marks restores the money, with tiers reported as 40% at 41 games, a further 20% at 51 games and a further 20% at 61 games (snippet-level; sources differ on details) — [Sports Illustrated, July 28, 2022](https://www.si.com/nba/2022/07/28/zion-williamson-max-contract-has-weight-stipulations-per-report); [Sports Illustrated, Dec 21, 2023](https://www.si.com/nba/2023/12/21/zion-williamson-contract-no-longer-guaranteed-final-three-years-clause); [CBS Sports](https://www.cbssports.com/nba/news/zion-williamson-contract-explaining-pelicans-stars-reportedly-non-guaranteed-deal); [Spotrac](https://spotrac.com/nba/player/_/id/31558)
- Likely versus unlikely bonuses: classification is based on the prior season, so a games-played bonus is "unlikely" if the player did not reach the mark last year. Likely bonuses count on the cap, unlikely ones do not (but count toward hard-cap/apron calculations). Example: John Henson's 60- and 75-game bonuses were unlikely because he had played 57 games — [Hoops Rumors, Nov 2016](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2016/11/players-with-incentive-bonuses-for-201617.html); [Hoops Rumors, Sep 2017](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2017/09/contract-incentives-millsap-lowry-holiday-more.html); [Spotrac](https://spotrac.com/news/_/id/2520/the-complicated-case-of-nba-bonuses-taxes-and-aprons) (snippet-level)
- Kyrie Irving's 2019 Nets contract carried eight unlikely $125,000 bonuses, one requiring 70 games played — [ESPN](https://africa.espn.com/nba/story/_/id/27162275/inside-contracts-led-kd-kyrie-deals) (snippet-level)
- Jonathan Isaac triggered a $2.6 million bonus in 2025 by playing his 70th game after several seasons below 70; earned unlikely bonuses can push teams over tax/apron lines late in the season — [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/nba-bonus-apron-chris-paul) (snippet-level)
- Exhibit 3 of the Uniform Player Contract is the "Prior Injury Exclusion", where the parties agree to limit or eliminate the player's right to base compensation in respect of a specified prior injury — [Sports Business Classroom](https://sportsbusinessclassroom.com/what-exhibits-comprise-an-nba-contract/); [NBA CBA, Uniform Player Contract](https://atlhawksfanatic.github.io/NBA-CBA/uniform-player-contract.html) (snippet-level)
- Baseline: NBA, MLB and NHL contracts are generally guaranteed, including for off-court injuries provided the hazardous-activities clause is not breached; NFL, MLS and WNBA contracts are not required to be guaranteed — [Sports Litigation Alert](https://sportslitigationalert.com/the-legal-business-aspects-of-career-ending-disability-insurance-policies-in-professional-and-college-sports/); [Beyond the Sideline, May 15, 2025](https://beyondthesideline.beehiiv.com/p/easing-the-pain-with-insurance)
- The league-level backstop that sits alongside contract terms (reported, weakly sourced): the NBA requires teams to insure their five highest-paid players through a league program, with about 150-160 players insured through one carrier; a 41-game waiting period, after which the insurer pays 80% of guaranteed base salary up to $175,000 per regular-season game; the carrier could traditionally name 14 specific player exclusions per season and can exclude a specific body part; individual policies historically cost 4-7% of salary; no insurer would cover Amar'e Stoudemire's knees on his 2010 Knicks contract — [PolicyAdvisor, June 1, 2022](https://policyadvisor.com/magazine/cost-to-insure-nba); [RealGM forum quoting a CBA FAQ](https://forums.realgm.com/boards/viewtopic.php?p=34486932); [Sports Litigation Alert](https://sportslitigationalert.com/the-legal-business-aspects-of-career-ending-disability-insurance-policies-in-professional-and-college-sports/) (the last hedges the 41-game figure as "it is believed"; the $175,000 cap is an old figure of unknown date)

### Inferences
- How much risk these clauses move: for a healthy star with leverage, almost none (contracts are fully guaranteed and bonuses are capped as a share of salary under the CBA). For a player with a documented injury history at signing, a great deal: Zion's three final seasons (the bulk of a roughly $193 million deal) became contingent on games played and conditioning; Embiid's team could reportedly have shed up to about half the deal for listed injuries.
- Contract clauses and insurance are complements with opposite coverage: the Embiid contract protected the team precisely for the body parts (feet, back) that the insurer excluded. Contract design covers known, pre-existing, player-specific risk; insurance covers new, unforeseen risk. A new product is most valuable in the gap: new injuries to players with full guarantees, and the first 41 games that the league program does not reimburse.
- Games-played tiers (41/51/61 for Zion; 60/70/75-game bonuses elsewhere) are already a privately negotiated parametric index on availability, with the player as the "insurer". They show the trigger is accepted by both union and league as contractible.
- The Embiid minutes test (1,650 minutes in three seasons extinguishes the team's right) is an "experience-rated" release: demonstrated health retires the protection. The same idea could be used to step down premiums or deductibles in an insurance product.

### Gaps
- The precise Embiid payout schedule (amounts recoverable by year and the games-missed threshold) is in the Hoops Rumors page but was not extracted verbatim; reports conflict.
- The current (2023 CBA) parameters of the league disability program (per-game cap, number of exclusions, treatment of playoffs, cap relief) were not verified from a primary source; Larry Coon's CBA FAQ was unreachable (expired certificate). The figures above should be treated as indicative and dated.
- Michael Porter Jr., Jonathan Isaac (2024 renegotiation) and Lonzo Ball contract protections were searched for and not found in this session.
- The CBA limit on incentive compensation as a share of salary and the rules on which seasons can be non-guaranteed were not sourced here.

## 5. Moral hazard and information problems specific to this setting

### Takeaway
Both sides control the insured outcome: the team decides who plays, how injuries are treated and reported, and may prefer to lose (the NBA fined Utah $500,000 and Indiana $100,000 for sitting healthy players in February 2026 and rewrote the lottery in May 2026), while the player controls effort and return-to-play timing. The academic evidence for player shirking is mixed for performance but more consistent for injury time (players in contract years spend less time on the injured list), and real disputes show insureds timing roster decisions around policy thresholds.

### Cited Findings
- Team-side manipulation of availability is live and documented: the NBA fined the Utah Jazz $500,000 (under a broader "conduct detrimental" / integrity standard) and the Indiana Pacers $100,000 (Player Participation Policy) in February 2026 for holding out players who could have played; the Jazz were fined a further $100,000 for sitting Lauri Markkanen on March 5, 2026. The Player Participation Policy dates from September 2023, with fines of $100,000, $250,000 and then at least $1 million for successive offences — [ESPN](https://africa.espn.com/nba/story/_/id/47914175/nba-fines-jazz-500k-pacers-100k-sitting-players); [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/nba-fines-jazz-for-markkanen-absence-amid-tanking-concerns); [AP via ClickOrlando, Feb 13, 2026](https://www.clickorlando.com/gallery/sports/2026/02/13/tankers-beware-jazz-fined-500000-pacers-100000-by-nba-for-player-participation-policy-breach/) (snippet-level)
- League response: in May 2026 the Board of Governors voted 29-1 for a "3-2-1" lottery starting with the 2027 draft: 16 lottery teams, the three worst records get two balls each and the 4th-10th worst get three, the three worst cannot fall below pick 12, no back-to-back No. 1 picks, no top-five pick three years running, and expanded league power to cut lottery odds, change draft position and fine for tanking. The format sunsets after 2029 — [ESPN](https://www.espn.com/nba/story/_/id/48903380/sources-nba-bog-votes-expand-lottery-disincentivizes-tanking); [AP via WTOP, May 2026](https://wtop.com/nba/2026/05/nbas-board-of-governors-passes-anti-tanking-changes-to-draft-lottery-process-ap-source-says/) (snippet-level)
- Insured-side timing around a threshold: the Mets recovered 75% of David Wright's salary once he had missed 60 games; the club kept him on the disabled list rather than activating him and publicly wanted insurance to keep paying, prompting commentary on whether that amounted to gaming the policy (Fangraphs concluded it was not fraud; no court ruled). He was activated only for a farewell homestand in September 2018 — [Fangraphs](https://blogs.fangraphs.com/david-wright-peter-alonso-and-the-law-of-metropolitan-frugality); [NBC Sports](https://www.nbcsports.com/mlb/news/mets-want-insurance-to-continue-paying-david-wrights-salary); [CBS Sports](https://www.cbssports.com/mlb/news/the-david-wright-insurance-mess-is-a-perfect-reason-why-mets-fans-deserve-better-from-ownership) (snippet-level)
- Insurer-insured disputes: Marqise Lee bought a $5 million loss-of-value policy for a $94,600 premium; Lloyd's underwriters denied his roughly $4.5 million claim alleging non-disclosure of a knee injury, and he sued for breach and bad faith in 2015 (C.D. Cal.), alleging more than nine months of delay; Morgan Breslin brought a similar suit — [Insurance Journal, Mar 13, 2015](https://insurancejournal.com/news/national/2015/03/13/360512.htm); [Sports Litigation Alert](https://sportslitigationalert.com/former-usc-wide-receiver-sues-insurance-company-over-disability-payout/) (snippet-level)
- The NHL and 20 teams are suing five insurers for more than $1 billion of unreimbursed pandemic losses, an example of large contingency claims ending in coverage litigation (snippet-level; article date unclear) — [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/article/nhl-20-teams-sue-insurers-over-pandemic-related-reimbursements/)
- Underwriter view of buyer moral hazard: "The moral risk can be significant" when a club insures against its own poor performance; Dortmund's underwriters required secrecy from coach and players and a right to cancel if the coach or three top players left — [Insurance Journal / Bloomberg, 2015](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/international/2015/04/23/365411.htm)
- Player-side evidence, NBA performance: Stiroh (2007, data 1988-2002) found performance rises in the contract year and falls significantly afterwards; Berri and Krautmann (2006) found the result depends on the productivity measure and is statistically insignificant on some; Pedicelli (2015, student paper) found no shirking; Owsik and Tan (September 2025 working paper) study contract-status effects and find no effect in the postseason — [Owsik and Tan (2025)](https://kerrymtan.github.io/econ/research/NBA_OwsikTan.pdf); [Pedicelli (2015)](https://blogs.elon.edu/ipe/files/2021/02/v24-Pedicelli-2015.pdf) (snippet-level; Stiroh and Berri-Krautmann as cited within these papers)
- Player-side evidence, injury time (MLB): Paulsen (Economic Inquiry) finds players in contract years spend fewer games on the injured list than those with more guaranteed years; Krautmann and Solow (Contemporary Economic Policy, 2015, "(Ma)Lingering on the Disabled List") find players likely to retire at contract end have more incentive to shirk; Lehn (1982) originally found disabled-list time rose with the spread of long-term contracts. Caveats: only the best players get long deals (selection), and injury severity data are privately held by teams — [StudyFinds summary of Paulsen](https://studyfinds.com/mlb-players-return-from-injuries-faster-when-contracts-about-to-expire/); [Paulsen, Sloan Sports Analytics Conference](https://www.sloansportsconference.com/research-papers/new-evidence-in-the-study-of-shirking-in-major-league-baseball); [Krautmann and Solow (2015)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/coecpo/v33y2015i4p689-697.html) (snippet-level)
- Mitigants seen in practice: waiting periods (41 games reported for the NBA program; 60 games for Wright), coinsurance (80% and 75% reimbursement; LongBall's cap of 60%), body-part exclusions and a carrier right to exclude named players, key-person cancellation clauses, partial cover only for revenue protection, and double triggers combining a listed injury with a games-missed threshold — [PolicyAdvisor](https://policyadvisor.com/magazine/cost-to-insure-nba); [RealGM](https://forums.realgm.com/boards/viewtopic.php?p=34486932); [JohnWallStreet on LongBall](https://www.johnwallstreet.com/p/disability-plus-gives-mlb-clubs-way-to-hedge-against-players-underperforming); [JohnWallStreet on Hedgehog](https://www.johnwallstreet.com/p/rising-costs-driving-demand-for-contractual-bonus-revenue-protection-insurance); [Carrier Management](https://www.carriermanagement.com/news/2025/06/05/275720.htm)

### Inferences
- The injury-time literature implies reported days missed are partly a choice variable responding to incentives, for both the player (contract status) and the team (insurance thresholds, standings). An index built on games missed therefore inherits moral hazard that a weather index does not have. This is the central difference between sports parametrics and natural-catastrophe parametrics.
- Threshold (cliff) triggers concentrate the manipulation incentive at the threshold (Wright at 60 games; an NBA player at game 40 of an injury absence). Linear per-game payouts with a deductible and coinsurance spread and weaken it.
- Tanking makes "team wins" a contaminated index: an owner holding a hedge that pays when the team loses has a second reason to lose on top of draft position. The 2026 lottery reform reduces but does not remove the draft incentive, and the league's new discretionary penalty powers add regulatory risk to any product whose payout rises as the team loses.
- Design responses suggested by the precedents: (1) pay on outcomes the owner cannot cheaply cause or that are costly to cause (a star's listed injury verified by an independent physician); (2) keep the owner's retained share large (coinsurance of 20-40%); (3) use league-wide indices (league-average games lost) for part of the payout; (4) include key-person and "roster change" cancellation or repricing clauses; (5) independent medical review, which is standard in disability claims but was not specifically sourced here.

### Gaps
- No public NBA team-versus-insurer coverage dispute over a specific player claim was found; such disputes are likely arbitrated or settled privately.
- The outcome of the Lee v. Lloyd's case was not found.
- No empirical study was found measuring whether teams' injury reporting changes around insurance waiting-period thresholds in the NBA.
- Independent medical examination practice in team disability policies was not sourced.
- Evidence on misreporting of injuries (for example fines for injury-report violations, "phantom" injured-list stints) was not searched in depth.

## 6. Adverse selection: the team's private medical information

### Takeaway
Underwriters respond to the team's information advantage with mandatory league-wide participation (the NBA program reportedly must cover each team's five highest-paid players), named-player and body-part exclusions, medical disclosure with rescission for misrepresentation, and a stated preference for insuring third parties (sponsors) over clubs. The practical result is that the players a team most wants to insure are the ones the market excludes.

### Cited Findings
- Mandatory pooled participation: every NBA team is reported to be required to insure its five most valuable contracts through the league program, with about 150-160 players insured through one company; players outside the top five are insured at team discretion in the specialty market. The NBA and NHL both require such cover for the highest-paid players; MLB and the NFL leave it to teams — [PolicyAdvisor, 2022](https://policyadvisor.com/magazine/cost-to-insure-nba); [Beyond the Sideline, 2025](https://beyondthesideline.beehiiv.com/p/easing-the-pain-with-insurance)
- Exclusions: the carrier could traditionally name 14 specific player exclusions per season, and exclusions can target a specific body part on a specific player; players with very high injury rates may be rejected; no insurer would cover Amar'e Stoudemire's knees (2010); Embiid's right foot was reportedly excluded — [PolicyAdvisor](https://policyadvisor.com/magazine/cost-to-insure-nba); [ABC11](https://abc11.com/tag/insurance-policy/) (the latter snippet-level)
- Uninsured contracts become tradeable liabilities: in the NHL, Toronto took Nathan Horton's uninsured contract so that it could move David Clarkson's contract to Columbus, illustrating that insured status changes a contract's value between teams with different budgets — [PolicyAdvisor](https://policyadvisor.com/magazine/cost-to-insure-nba)
- A team can also simply decline cover: the Jets did not renew the policy the Packers had held on Aaron Rodgers before his 2023 Achilles injury (per Sportico, as cited); the Celtics had not said whether Jayson Tatum's contract was insured after his 2025 Achilles tear — [Beyond the Sideline, May 15, 2025](https://beyondthesideline.beehiiv.com/p/easing-the-pain-with-insurance)
- Disclosure and rescission: Lloyd's underwriters denied Marqise Lee's loss-of-value claim on the ground that he had not disclosed knee problems at application — [Insurance Journal, 2015](https://insurancejournal.com/news/national/2015/03/13/360512.htm) (snippet-level)
- Underwriter statement on informational asymmetry: HCC's Philip Hall prefers insuring a sponsor to dealing directly with clubs because clubs know their own risk better — [Insurance Journal / Bloomberg, 2015](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/international/2015/04/23/365411.htm)
- Model-based pricing as a partial answer: Players Health prices from an injury probability for each athlete and offers injury-risk insights from its database; LongBall prices from a quantitative projection of each player's expected performance — [Carrier Management](https://www.carriermanagement.com/news/2025/06/05/275720.htm) (snippet-level); [JohnWallStreet](https://www.johnwallstreet.com/p/disability-plus-gives-mlb-clubs-way-to-hedge-against-players-underperforming)
- Traditional disability policies "often exclude certain body parts" (for example a pitcher's previously injured shoulder) and "almost always include a deductible period" — [JohnWallStreet](https://www.johnwallstreet.com/p/disability-plus-gives-mlb-clubs-way-to-hedge-against-players-underperforming)

### Inferences
- The "top five by salary" rule is an anti-selection device: selection is by an observable, non-medical criterion, so teams cannot insure only the players they privately believe are fragile. Any new voluntary product should copy this: require coverage of a rule-defined set (top N salaries, or the whole roster) and not allow the owner to pick names.
- Body-part exclusions reintroduce the gap. Contract carve-outs (Embiid, Zion) are the team's self-help where the insurer excludes; a product that covers pre-existing conditions would need either the player's own participation (lower guarantees) or a price loading large enough to make it unattractive.
- Because public tracking and injury data are now rich, an insurer's information disadvantage is concentrated in private medical files (imaging, training-room data). Requiring disclosure of those files with a rescission right, as in the Lee case, is the standard response but generates disputes.
- Index products on public data (games missed, league-wide injury counts) reduce adverse selection on the loss amount but not on the decision to buy: a team will buy availability cover when it privately knows a star is carrying an injury. Multi-year or league-mandated purchase reduces this timing selection.

### Gaps
- The number of exclusions, pre-existing-condition definitions and medical-disclosure obligations under the current NBA league policy were not verified from a primary source.
- Whether underwriters get access to team medical records or league-held injury databases (and under what privacy terms) is not sourced.
- No academic paper specifically on adverse selection in team-purchased athlete disability insurance was found.

## 7. Basis risk: measuring and limiting the mismatch between index and actual loss

### Takeaway
The index-insurance literature finds basis risk is large even in well-designed products (a flagship Kenyan livestock index product left buyers with about 69% of their original catastrophic risk) and recommends measuring it directly, especially the probability of no payout when the buyer suffers a severe loss ("false negative" or "catastrophic basis risk"). No published estimate exists for the basis risk between an NBA owner's financial loss and candidate indices such as wins, playoff qualification or games missed; the team would have to estimate it.

### Cited Findings
- Jensen, Barrett and Mude ("Index insurance quality and basis risk: evidence from northern Kenya"): index-based livestock insurance (IBLI) reduces exposure to covariate risk from high-loss events by an average of 63%, yet policyholders are left with an average of 69% of their original risk from high-loss events, because much of household risk is idiosyncratic and not captured by the area index (snippet-level) — [AGRIS record](https://agris.fao.org/search/ru/records/672364098aa2fad1ce470c9a)
- Morsink, Clarke and Mapfumo (World Bank Policy Research Working Paper 7744, 2016, "How to Measure Whether Index Insurance Provides Reliable Protection") propose two indicators: the probability of catastrophic basis risk and the catastrophic performance ratio; an earlier Clarke framework proposed reporting the probability of receiving a payment by level of loss (snippet-level) — [World Bank WPS7744](https://financialprotectionforum.org/sites/default/files/WPS7744.pdf); [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:wbk:wbrwps:7744)
- A false negative is the case where the buyer suffers a shock but the index does not trigger, leaving the buyer worse off than with no insurance because the premium has been paid; a Zambia rainfall study estimates false-negative probability from historical records as a direct measure of spatial basis risk (snippet-level) — [BASIS (UC Davis) policy brief, "Measuring Index Insurance Quality"](https://basis.ucdavis.edu/publication/policy-brief-measuring-index-insurance-quality)
- In cat bonds, basis risk ranks lowest for indemnity triggers and highest for pure parametric triggers; modelled-loss and parametric-index (weighted, calibrated to the sponsor's exposure) triggers are used to cut basis risk while keeping transparency (snippet-level) — [OECD](https://oecd.org/en/publications/fostering-catastrophe-bond-markets-in-asia-and-the-pacific_ab1e49ef-en/full-report/component-6.html); [arXiv 2609.22052](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.22052)
- Sports example of basis risk in practice: Mattress Mack's bets and insurance did not match the refund liability one-for-one (the insurance "was not enough to cover his potential losses"; hedge bets were placed game by game as the postseason unfolded) — [Covers](https://www.covers.com/Editorial/Article/902af1c6-e6b7-11e9-a97f-0a73013d6078/Even-with-millions-riding-on-MLB-World-Series-odds-Mattress-Mack-is-all-business) (snippet-level)
- Sports example of low basis risk: hedging a contractual bonus that pays on "team makes the playoffs" with an exchange contract on the same event has essentially zero basis risk, which is why the first documented NBA exchange hedges were bonus hedges — [Decrypt, Feb 13, 2026](https://decrypt.co/358000/kalshi-taps-sports-insurance-market-with-game-point-capital-deal-as-regulatory-battles-mount)
- Revenue-protection policies deliberately do not cover the full loss — [JohnWallStreet](https://www.johnwallstreet.com/p/rising-costs-driving-demand-for-contractual-bonus-revenue-protection-insurance)

### Inferences
- Applying the Clarke / Morsink metrics to the NBA: for each candidate index, use historical seasons to estimate (a) the probability the hedge pays nothing in seasons where the owner's loss (dead salary paid to injured players, lost playoff gate) is in its worst decile, and (b) the ratio of average payout to average loss in those seasons per premium dollar. These are computable from public injury, salary and standings data and would be an original contribution for the project.
- Expected ordering of basis risk for an owner hedging an injury to a star: lowest for player-specific games-missed cover on that star (but highest moral hazard); intermediate for team games-lost-to-injury weighted by salary; high for team wins or playoff qualification (wins depend on many things other than health, and a team can miss the playoffs healthy or make them injured); highest for a league-wide injury index (almost purely idiosyncratic risk remains). This mirrors the IBLI result that an area index removes covariate risk only.
- The classic way to limit basis risk is a salary-weighted, player-specific index (analogous to exposure-weighted parametric indices in cat bonds) or a hybrid: index payout plus a small indemnity "gap" layer, or a double trigger.
- Basis risk and moral hazard trade off exactly as in cat bonds: the more the index is tied to the owner's own players and decisions, the lower the basis risk and the higher the manipulability.

### Gaps
- No study quantifying the correlation between NBA team financial results and wins, playoff rounds or injury games lost was found in this session; the size of the mismatch is therefore an open empirical question for the team.
- The primary texts of Clarke (2016, "A Theory of Rational Demand for Index Insurance") and Elabed et al. on multi-scale contracts were not retrieved; they are standard references on why high basis risk depresses rational demand and how to design around it, but are not cited here from a verified source.
- The World Bank and BASIS papers were seen at abstract/snippet level only; exact formulae for the catastrophic performance ratio should be taken from the paper.

## 8. Portfolio and pooling: league mutuals, captives, reinsurance, multi-team owners

### Takeaway
League-wide pooled programs exist (the NBA's mandatory top-five disability program through a single carrier; MLB's league-wide broker program after a failed Bermuda captive), and brokers sell captive implementation to sports organisations, but no publicly documented captive owned by an NBA team-owning group for player-performance risk was found. Peer pooling has been tried on the player side (Pando).

### Cited Findings
- NBA: league program covering each team's five highest-paid players, with about 150-160 players insured through one company — [PolicyAdvisor, 2022](https://policyadvisor.com/magazine/cost-to-insure-nba)
- NBA league-level career-ending cover was raised to $2.5 million per player for the 2020 Orlando restart by agreement between the NBA and NBPA (headline-level) — [Yahoo Sports](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/nba-increases-insurance-policy-coverage-for-players-to-25-million-as-league-restarts-amid-pandemic-215555489.html); [Hoops Rumors, July 2020](https://hoopsrumors.com/2020/07/nba-nbpa-reach-agreements-on-disability-insurance-salary-withholding.html)
- MLB: an earlier group program used a Bermuda captive that failed; MLB later built a league-wide workers' compensation program through a single broker, approved by MLB and the MLBPA (snippet-level) — [Insurance Journal, Aug 21, 2006](https://www.insurancejournal.com/magazines/features/2006/08/21/72323.htm)
- NFL: insurers became unwilling to cover the league for general liability and workers' compensation over concussion exposure, with one carrier remaining (2019), which shows how a league-wide program can become dependent on a single market (snippet-level) — [Insurance Journal, Feb 1, 2019](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2019/02/01/516407.htm)
- NHL: NFP has been the league's broker for more than 30 years and became its official business insurance brokerage partner in 2024 (snippet-level) — [PR Newswire](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/nfp-and-the-national-hockey-league-announce-multiyear-north-american-partnership-302043978.html)
- Captive services are marketed to sports organisations by the same broker — [NFP Sports captive implementation and management](https://sports.nfp.com/services/captive-implementation-management/) (page exists; contents not read)
- A corporate precedent for using a captive to access parametric capacity: an Olympics sponsor used a Guernsey captive to buy $120 million of parametric pandemic cover (headline-level) — [Artemis](https://www.artemis.bm/news/olympics-sponsor-uses-guernsey-captive-to-buy-120m-parametric-pandemic-cover/)
- Team stop-loss: specialty underwriters offer stop-loss cover that attaches once a team's out-of-pocket disability losses exceed a predetermined annual limit (an aggregate, portfolio-level cover in place of player-by-player cover) (snippet-level) — [Petersen International Underwriters](https://www.piu.org/wp-content/themes/piu-new/files/athlete_disability/ProfessionalAthleteDisabilityInsurance.pdf)
- Reinsurance capacity for a new class: LongBall says it has an insurer plus a reinsurer providing "hundreds of millions of dollars" of capacity for MLB performance cover; Hiscox's 2013 football product was backed by AXA, Munich Re and Swiss Re — [JohnWallStreet](https://www.johnwallstreet.com/p/disability-plus-gives-mlb-clubs-way-to-hedge-against-players-underperforming); [Hiscox](https://hiscoxgroup.com/node/9661)
- Player-side mutual: Pando income pools (about 140 players by 2019; Pando takes 10%) — [NPR via WAMC](https://www.wamc.org/2019-10-25/some-baseball-players-are-entering-income-pooling-agreements-to-fix-imbalance) (snippet-level)

### Inferences
- Injuries across 30 teams are close to independent, so a league mutual for the first-41-games layer (the part the league disability program does not pay) is actuarially attractive: each team's injury cost is volatile but the league total is stable. A mutual also solves adverse selection by mandatory participation and creates peer monitoring; its weakness is that teams differ in medical quality and risk appetite (cross-subsidy) and that members are competitors.
- Zero-sum structure offers a natural hedge inside the league: one team's playoff miss is another's berth. A pooled "playoff revenue" swap among owners would be self-financing in aggregate, but each owner's side of it rewards losing, so it collides with the tanking problem and likely with league integrity rules.
- A multi-team or multi-sport ownership group can retain more risk in a captive (diversification across rosters and seasons) and buy aggregate stop-loss above it, which is the PIU "team stop-loss" idea scaled up. The captive also gives direct access to reinsurers and parametric markets, as in the Olympics sponsor case.
- The MLB Bermuda captive failure and the NFL's single-carrier dependence are reminders that league captives need adequate capitalisation and reinsurance, and that a pooled program concentrates counterparty risk.

### Gaps
- No documented captive insurer owned by an NBA team, a multi-team owner (for example a holding group owning NBA and other franchises) or the NBA itself was found for player-contract or performance risk. One search summary mentioned the NHL pooling premiums into a self-insured captive with reinsurance backing, but it could not be tied to a specific readable source and is unverified.
- Why MLB's Bermuda captive failed is not explained in the snippet.
- Reinsurers of the NBA league disability program, and whether the program is experience-rated by team, are not public in the sources found.

## 9. Novel proposals and products for insuring performance (not only injury)

### Takeaway
The closest real product to "performance insurance" is LongBall Capital's "Disability Plus" for MLB clubs (2024): it pays up to 60% of salary when a player's runs above replacement fall below a projected threshold for any reason, at a cost of roughly 10% of salary, with one policy issued at the time of reporting. Other precedents are Hiscox's 2013 football revenue-protection product, Dortmund-style non-qualification cover, relegation insurance and, since 2026, exchange hedges of team outcomes; no NBA performance-warranty product was found.

### Cited Findings
- LongBall Capital "Comprehensive Player Impairment & Disability Insurance" ("Disability Plus"), reported October 29, 2024: described as a league-approved product for MLB clubs; founders John Abbamondi (former Brooklyn Sports & Entertainment CEO) and Shiraz Rehman (former Cubs and Rangers assistant GM). Trigger: the player's performance, measured as runs above replacement, falls below a set threshold for any reason (injury or not). Payout: reimbursement of up to 60% of the player's salary or value, based on a quantitative projection of expected performance. Price: roughly 10% of salary. Capacity: an unnamed insurer plus a reinsurer providing "hundreds of millions of dollars". Status: one policy written at publication. Stated effect: "effectively turn a fixed cost into a variable cost". Sizing: MLB salaries about $5 billion in 2024; insuring 10% would mean about $500 million of limit and about $50 million of premium a year. Also pitched for cheap young stars whose replacement cost far exceeds salary (Adley Rutschman at $800,000) and for small-market clubs (single source) — [JohnWallStreet, Oct 29, 2024](https://www.johnwallstreet.com/p/disability-plus-gives-mlb-clubs-way-to-hedge-against-players-underperforming)
- The same article does not address moral hazard (a team benching the player, or a player easing off), and notes traditional disability cover often excludes body parts and almost always has a deductible period — [JohnWallStreet](https://www.johnwallstreet.com/p/disability-plus-gives-mlb-clubs-way-to-hedge-against-players-underperforming)
- Hiscox / Avoca Elite Sports "MAXI" football revenue protection (announced November 25, 2013): triggered by a key player's serious injury or illness; covers both the fall in the player's agreed transfer value and lost club revenue from prolonged absence; squad transfer values covered regardless of age and medical history; Hiscox lead with AXA, Munich Re and Swiss Re; AES models each club's financial risk. Pricing, limits and take-up not disclosed — [Hiscox press release](https://hiscoxgroup.com/node/9661)
- Season-outcome revenue insurance for a club: Dortmund's Champions League non-qualification cover (2012 onward; about 30% rate-on-line per one source) and the 5% indicative rate for Real Madrid — [Insurance Journal / Bloomberg, 2015](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/international/2015/04/23/365411.htm)
- Revenue protection for "a highly touted team [that] failed to make the playoffs" is described by Hedgehog Risk as a product type, as a hypothetical; relegation and finals-sweep cover are written in practice, with partial cover only — [JohnWallStreet, Mar 11, 2025](https://www.johnwallstreet.com/p/rising-costs-driving-demand-for-contractual-bonus-revenue-protection-insurance)
- Exchange-based season-outcome hedging for NBA teams exists since February 2026 (playoff berth and second-round contracts used to hedge bonuses) — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/358000/kalshi-taps-sports-insurance-market-with-game-point-capital-deal-as-regulatory-battles-mount)
- Parametric injury cover priced per athlete from an injury-probability model (Players Health / Zurich, 2025-2026) is the nearest data-driven health product — [Carrier Management](https://www.carriermanagement.com/news/2025/06/05/275720.htm); [Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/midwest/2026/01/06/853263.htm)
- Market evidence that injury risk is priced heavily in contracts: a Fangraphs analysis found teams applied very large discounts to players with disabled-list history in 2005-2010 free-agent deals, possibly more than warranted (snippet-level) — [The Hardball Times / Fangraphs](https://tht.fangraphs.com/the-impact-of-injuries-on-player-valuation)

### Inferences
- A catalogue of structures an NBA owner could be offered, with status:
  1. League TTD disability (exists; 41-game wait, 80%, top five, exclusions).
  2. Contract-embedded protection: body-part carve-outs, games-played guarantees, unlikely bonuses (exists; Embiid, Zion).
  3. Contractual bonus / cost-of-success insurance (exists OTC; exists on exchange for two NBA teams, 2026).
  4. Revenue protection against missing the playoffs (exists in European football; described but undocumented for the NBA; high moral hazard and tanking conflict).
  5. Performance-shortfall insurance on a metric (exists in MLB via LongBall with one policy; never implemented in the NBA; an NBA analogue would need a contractible metric such as EPM-, BPM- or minutes-based value and protection against the team controlling playing time).
  6. Parametric games-missed cover with a listed-injury leg (exists in college NIL; never implemented for NBA teams).
  7. Availability cat bond or ILS (never implemented; FIFA 2003 is the only sport cat bond found).
  8. League mutual or owner-group captive with stop-loss (pooled league program exists; captive for performance risk not documented).
  9. Inter-owner swaps on playoff revenue (proposal only; no precedent found).
  10. Player-side income shares (exist; BLA, Pando, Vestible) which could be combined with lower guarantees as an indirect transfer of risk from owner to outside capital.
- The LongBall price point (about 10% of salary for up to 60% reimbursement on shortfall "for any reason") versus traditional disability (4-7% of salary for 80% after 41 games, injury only, with exclusions) gives the team a first benchmark for what broader triggers cost.
- A basketball "performance warranty" faces a sharper moral-hazard problem than baseball: an NBA coach controls minutes and usage far more directly than a baseball club controls a hitter's plate appearances, and counting stats depend on role. Rate-based metrics with a minimum-minutes condition, or triggers that require an injury designation, would be needed.

### Gaps
- No academic or industry paper was found proposing wins-based guarantees or "performance warranties" for NBA contracts specifically; these appear to be unimplemented ideas, and none was located even as a formal published proposal in this session.
- LongBall's status after October 2024 (policies written, extension to the NBA, the identity of the insurer and reinsurer, how the threshold and payout curve are set, how playing-time manipulation is handled) is unknown; the product details are single-sourced.
- Take-up and claims experience of the Hiscox MAXI product are unknown; it may no longer be offered.
- No source was found on revenue insurance actually bought by a North American major-league team against missing the playoffs.
