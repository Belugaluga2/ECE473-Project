# Who could use Kalshi for sports injury cover? Legal roadmap (2026-11-11)

**Scope:** U.S. Kalshi contracts and the NBA as the example league. "Healthcare provider" means a treating clinician or clinic; a health plan or licensed insurer is treated separately. Other leagues and a real launch need their own contract- and state-specific review. This is a project feasibility map, not a legal clearance for any trade.

## The central distinction

**Buying an existing exchange contract to offset one's own risk** is an economic hedge. It does not by itself make the buyer an insurer. **Promising an athlete, team, or clinic a payment for injury loss in return for a premium** is a separate product that may be insurance. In New York, for example, the [insurance-contract definition and swap exception](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ISC/1101) distinguish those activities, while [insurer](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ISC/1102) and [producer](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ISC/2102) licensing apply to insurance business. [NAIC's certificate-of-authority process](https://content.naic.org/industry/ucaa) describes how risk-bearing insurers seek state licenses. Classification and obligations vary by state.

If an event contract is legally a **swap**, [7 U.S.C. § 16(h)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-118/t7/s16/) says the swap is not insurance and cannot be regulated as an insurance contract under state law. Whether Kalshi sports contracts qualify as swaps is contested: the [Third Circuit's New Jersey preliminary ruling](https://www2.ca3.uscourts.gov/opinarch/251922p.pdf) favored federal preemption, while the [Sixth Circuit's Ohio/Tennessee decision](https://www.opn.ca6.uscourts.gov/opinions.pdf/26a0272p-06.pdf) and [Ninth Circuit's Nevada decision](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2026/08/28/25-7516.pdf) rejected it in their cases. Kalshi's federal exchange status therefore does not supply a single nationwide answer to state sports-wagering questions.

The [CFTC/SEC insurance safe harbor](https://www.cftc.gov/sites/default/files/idc/groups/public/%40lrfederalregister/documents/file/2012-18003a.pdf) has two routes. One requires an insurable interest, proved loss, no trading separately from that interest, and an eligible provider. Another expressly covers enumerated products, including health and disability insurance, from an eligible provider without requiring that first route's product conditions. A tradable NO-on-points binary does not resemble either ordinary insurance route. The safe harbor is nonexclusive, so this comparison is an inference rather than a final legal classification.

## Who wants to trade?

| Proposed buyer or platform | Current obstacle for an NBA injury or points contract | What would have to change or be checked |
|---|---|---|
| **Player buying NO on their own points** | [NBA Constitution Art. 35(f)](https://cms.nba.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/2024/06/NBA-Consitution-By-Laws-June-2024.pdf) bars direct and indirect wagers on NBA and G League events. The [Kalshi NBA market](https://kalshi.com/markets/kxnbapts/nba-player-points/kxnbapts-26nov11clenyk) independently excludes players; [Kalshi's integrity policy](https://kalshi.com/policy-center/insider-trading) also bars people who can influence the outcome or have material nonpublic information. The player's scoring and availability affect the contract directly. | NBA rule and player-contract changes, Kalshi eligibility and integrity-rule changes, and jurisdiction-specific law review. NBA Art. 17 generally requires three-fourths of governors for a Constitution amendment. Permission alone would not remove the player's ability to affect settlement. A family member, agent, or insurer placing a player-directed trade is not a solution to an indirect-wager ban. |
| **Team, owner, coach, or staff trading NBA outcomes** | [NBA Constitution Arts. 13(e) and 35A(g)](https://cms.nba.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/2024/06/NBA-Consitution-By-Laws-June-2024.pdf) restrict team and league participants and indirect wagers; the [Kalshi market](https://kalshi.com/markets/kxnbapts/nba-player-points/kxnbapts-26nov11clenyk) excludes owners and staff. A team typically also has private medical and lineup information. | League and exchange restrictions would both need to change, plus state-law analysis. A team may instead buy a genuine policy from an independent licensed insurer; the team should not direct that insurer to place its prohibited wager. |
| **Treating doctor or clinic trading on its patient's injury** | A team-employed clinician also faces the staff exclusion. An outside clinician commonly has private diagnosis and return-to-play information, and may influence return-to-play decisions. [Kalshi's integrity policy](https://kalshi.com/policy-center/insider-trading) prohibits outcome-influencing traders. A HIPAA-covered provider's identifiable records have separate use and disclosure limits. | Establish a real financial loss, a permitted use of patient data, and exchange eligibility. [HHS](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/disclosures-treatment-payment-health-care-operations/index.html) permits defined treatment, payment, and healthcare operations, including ordinary health-claims reinsurance; a points bet is not automatically that activity. A specific patient authorization, when required, would **not** override Kalshi's insider or influence restrictions. Independent clinical judgment remains essential under [AMA ethics guidance](https://code-medical-ethics.ama-assn.org/ethics-opinions/conflicts-interest-patient-care). |
| **Health plan, capitated provider, or disability insurer hedging claims** | This entity may have an actual claims liability, but can also possess private athlete health information. A fee-for-service clinic may instead gain treatment revenue from an injury and has to show a loss before calling a trade a hedge. | Determine the actual insured liability, whether the firm and trader have disqualifying access to material nonpublic information or outcome influence under [Kalshi's integrity rules](https://kalshi.com/policy-center/insider-trading), and whether any protected health information use is permitted. If a licensed insurer trades derivatives, its home-state investment rules apply: [New York § 1410](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ISC/1410), for example, requires board authorization, a regulator-approved derivatives plan, and hedge-effectiveness controls. An information wall cannot be assumed to cure firm-wide private knowledge or influence. |
| **Kalshi or an app selling injury insurance directly** | A tradable points contract pays on a statistic, not proved financial injury loss. Calling it "insurance" does not turn it into a policy; offering a separate injury indemnity may trigger state insurer and seller licensing. Kalshi, as a designated contract market, must follow the [CFTC's contract-listing process](https://www.cftc.gov/IndustryOversight/ContractsProducts/ListingProcedures/index.htm) for any new exchange contract. | Separate the state-regulated insurer and policy from Kalshi's exchange contract, or obtain the applicable insurer and producer authority for any actual policy. The exchange would need to certify or seek approval for any new contract. An injury-only event contract faces the [CFTC's current proposed-rule concerns](https://www.cftc.gov/media/14151/NPRM_PredictionMarkets060926/download) about harm incentives, medical privacy, and objective settlement. This is a proposal, not a final categorical ban. |

The [2023 NBA–NBPA agreement](https://imgix.cosmicjs.com/25da5eb0-15eb-11ee-b5b3-fbd321202bdf-Final-2023-NBA-Collective-Bargaining-Agreement-6-28-23.pdf) also provides contract-dependent compensation protection and disability benefits. A missed scoring target does not establish a lost paycheck. For a team, HIPAA does not automatically cover employment records merely because they mention health, although the treating provider's records can be protected; [HHS explains that distinction](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-individuals/employers-health-information-workplace/index.html).

The NBA and Kalshi bans above are league and exchange rules. They are distinct from criminal or civil liability. The [CFTC's enforcement advisory](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9185-26) warns that trading based on misappropriated confidential information or deliberately influencing an outcome can also violate federal antifraud or manipulation law; a rule breach is not automatically such a violation.

## The most plausible compliant structure

1. A **state-licensed disability insurer** sells a defined injury-loss policy to an athlete or team. It underwrites the loss, pays valid claims, maintains required capital, and uses properly licensed sellers. The athlete or team buys the policy, not a player-directed Kalshi position.
2. The insurer separately asks its insurance regulator and Kalshi compliance team whether it may hedge a specified risk with a permitted, objective sports contract. It needs an eligible trader and defensible handling of any private health information. State rules can constrain insurer derivatives; the New York plan requirement above is one example.
3. Kalshi would list only an exchange contract that satisfies its rules and the applicable CFTC process. The [CFTC's June 2026 proposal](https://www.cftc.gov/media/14151/NPRM_PredictionMarkets060926/download) views aggregate scores and statistics more favorably than injury-only contracts, but gives no safe harbor for a particular market. The [player unions asked to prohibit under and injury contracts](https://comments.cftc.gov/Handlers/PdfHandler.ashx?id=35929); that filing is a policy request, not enacted law.

This structure has a practical problem even if counsel approves it: a standard points contract settles a did-not-play outcome at an exchange-determined fair price and pays on healthy underperformance. The [feasibility note](sports_injury_hedge_feasibility.md) measures that payoff mismatch in an explicitly hypothetical model. Legal access would not make the hedge useful.

For a real implementation decision, the missing facts are the **state, league, exact Kalshi series, identity and affiliations of the trader, actual dollar loss, medical information held, and whether anyone is promising an indemnity**. Those facts determine which exchange, league, insurance, privacy, and state-gaming rules apply.
