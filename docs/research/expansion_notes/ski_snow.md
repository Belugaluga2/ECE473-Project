# Snow and Winter-Temperature Risk: Ski Resorts, Snow-Exposed Businesses, and How They Hedge

Research date: 2026-11-19. US focus, with international examples noted. Several primary pages (SAM magazine, City University open-access PDF, CME OpenMarkets, a PR Newswire release) returned 403 or timed out, so some figures come from search-result excerpts or secondary coverage. Those are flagged inline.

## 1. Revenue sensitivity: how much do skier visits and revenue fall in low-snow seasons, and does snowmaking make temperature the real risk?

### Takeaway
Low-snow US seasons cut skier visits about 8-15% nationally, and much more in bad regions: Vail's Rockies visits fell 25% in 2025-26. The losses are asymmetric: bad years hurt more than big years help. Snowmaking turns much of the snowfall risk into a temperature risk, because guns only work below roughly 27-28°F wet-bulb. Early-season (Nov-Dec) cold-hour availability is the exposure that resorts have actually insured.

### Cited Findings

**Snowmaking and temperature**
- Snowmaking generally needs a wet-bulb temperature below about 28°F; a Massachusetts resort uses 27°F as its rule of thumb. — [Grist](https://grist.org/energy/ski-resorts-are-increasingly-reliant-on-snowmaking-but-at-what-cost/); [ACEEE 2015 paper](https://aceee.org/files/proceedings/2015/data/papers/2-175.pdf); [Massachusetts resort "Snowmaking 101"](https://massresort.com/play/hours-rates/snowmaking-101)
- Compressed-air energy per unit of snow rises sharply in warmer wet-bulb bins and can vary by a factor of 15 or more by equipment and conditions. A Colorado State study for Aspen found cost per gallon converted rises exponentially with temperature. — [ACEEE 2015](https://aceee.org/files/proceedings/2015/data/papers/2-175.pdf)
- Cost benchmarks (unverified):
  - About $1,000-2,000 to cover one acre with 12 inches (about one acre-foot of snow), attributed to meteorologist Joel Gratz. — [Grist](https://grist.org/energy/ski-resorts-are-increasingly-reliant-on-snowmaking-but-at-what-cost/) / [Snowbrains](https://snowbrains.com/the-hardships-of-making-snow-cost-reliance-environmental-impacts/)
  - "$3,000-15,000 per acre-foot", from a forum post. — [FirstTracks forum](https://www.firsttracksonline.com/boards/goto/post?id=98626)

### Inferences
- For snowmaking-equipped resorts, the binding risk is (a) too few sub-28°F wet-bulb hours in the Nov-Dec base-building window, and (b) warm spells during the holiday peak. Season snowfall matters less. The one documented US resort contract (Mountain Creek, section 2) is a temperature contract on exactly that window. A Kalshi monthly-snowfall strip would mismatch the exposure for these resorts. A temperature or degree-hour contract fits better.
- Natural snowfall matters most for (a) destination Western resorts, where terrain openings, the powder-day "product" and destination bookings depend on it (2025-26: Rockies visits -25% with snowfall -43%), and (b) small resorts without snowmaking.
- Season passes (about 65% of Vail lift revenue) already shift much of the volume risk away from the resort's current-year revenue. Uncovered exposure sits in the remainder: lift tickets, ski school, rental, F&B and lodging, plus next year's pass sales after a bad year. The 2025-26 figures (visits -14.9%, lift revenue -5.6%) give a rough revenue/visit pass-through of about 0.4 for lift revenue at Vail. Ancillary lines likely track visits more closely (inference, unverified).
- The loss distribution is asymmetric (low snow hurts more than high snow helps). The resort's natural hedge is therefore a put-like payoff on snowfall or cold-hours, not a symmetric swap.

## 2. Existing hedges: history and current (CME snow, OTC, parametric insurance, skier guarantees, Japan)

### Takeaway
Exchange-traded snow hedging in the US effectively ended when CME filed to delist its snowfall contracts in 2014. What exists now is parametric insurance (MSI GuaranteedWeather/Vortex, Demex backed by Nephila, Arbol) plus bespoke OTC temperature covers. The one well-documented US resort contract (Mountain Creek, 2011) ended in a lawsuit over which weather station counted, a direct illustration of basis risk. Skier-facing products like Epic Coverage explicitly exclude low snow, so resorts keep that risk.

### Cited Findings

They were monthly and seasonal-strip contracts traded October to April. — [CME press release, Feb 2006](https://www.cmegroup.com/media-room/press-releases/2006/2/09/cme_will_launch_snowfallindexcontractsforbostonandnewyorkcity.html); [Insurance Journal, 2006](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/east/2006/02/26/65933.htm)
- Nov 22, 2009: CME delisted the October contract for the Boston/NY monthly and seasonal-strip snowfall futures and options. — [CME advisory Q2009-265](https://www.cmegroup.com/tools-information/lookups/advisories/market-data/Q2009-265.html)
- Dec 2, 2013: CME delisted snowfall and rainfall binary options (97 contracts per SER-6940). Standard snowfall futures and options were not affected. — [CME SER-6935](https://www.cmegroup.com/tools-information/lookups/advisories/ser/SER-6935.html); [CME SER-6940](https://www.cmegroup.com/tools-information/lookups/advisories/market-regulation/SER-6940.html)

- Artemis covered the first snowfall contract trade of a winter on CME, evidence the contracts traded only sporadically. — [Artemis](https://www.artemis.bm/news/first-snowfall-contact-of-this-winter-trades-on-the-cme)

**Documented resort contracts (US)**
- **Mountain Creek (NJ), winter 2011-12:**
  - Policy from Everest Indemnity covering Dec 12-27, 2011, the key snowmaking window before Christmas. It paid if average daily temperature exceeded 33°F on more than 10 of the 16 days.
  - Payout was $284,970 per qualifying day beyond 10, capped at $1,710,000. The premium was not disclosed.
  - The resort claimed using its own location's temperatures. Everest used colder reporting stations showing only 9 qualifying days, and the resort sued. The outcome was not found. — [Artemis, May 15 2012](https://www.artemis.bm/news/dispute-over-reporting-stations-leads-to-weather-insurance-lawsuit/); [FirstTracks, May 2012](https://www.firsttracksonline.com/2012/05/11/mountain-creek-sues-to-recover-under-weather-insurance-policy/); [SAM headline](https://www.saminfo.com/news/sam-headline-news/7301-501-mountain-creek-sues-insurer-over-weather-policy)
- A SAM magazine piece ("Weather Bet", May 2008) notes resorts were slow to adopt weather cover and treat snowmaking as self-insurance. The page returned 403, so this is from the search excerpt only. — [SAM, 2008](https://www.saminfo.com/archives/2009-2000/2008/may-2008/weather-bet)

**Current parametric snow insurance (US)**
- **Vortex Weather Insurance** (MSI GuaranteedWeather subsidiary) launched direct-access parametric snow policies in Sep 2025.
  - The buyer sets a snow threshold and period; the policy pays if snowfall is below the threshold. It is pitched to ski resorts and snow-removal contractors, and policies were previously sold only via MSI GuaranteedWeather.
  - Underwritten by Mitsui Sumitomo Insurance USA (A+ AM Best, admitted in 50 states). No minimum eligibility, no proof of loss. - **Demex Group** (Aug 2022) launched two products:

  - "Cost Control" pays when snowfall is extremely high (property owners, facilities managers, municipalities).
  - Risk is backed by Nephila Capital weather derivatives plus unnamed reinsurers, on Evanston Insurance (Markel) non-admitted paper. Pricing is not disclosed. — [Artemis, Aug 12 2022](https://www.artemis.bm/news/demex-launches-parametric-snow-covers-backed-by-nephila-reinsurers/)

### Inferences
- US resort snow hedging has always been rare and bespoke. The only exchange product (CME Boston/NYC snowfall) sat at city airports far from resorts, traded thinly and was delisted after about 8 years. That is a cautionary precedent for any city-station snow market aimed at resorts.
- The Mountain Creek dispute is the clearest US example of station-to-site basis risk, the same problem AgriHedge found with airport rain gauges 30-55 miles from farms. Gridded settlement (Arbol's ERA5) or on-mountain stations (SNOTEL) are the obvious mitigations.
- The Mountain Creek contract was sized at about $285K per day and $1.71M total for 16 days at one mid-size resort. That gives an order-of-magnitude ticket size for a single resort's early-season temperature cover, far above current Kalshi snow-book depth (section 4).
- Vail's choice to exclude snow from Epic Coverage shows the resort prefers to keep (or self-insure) the low-snow risk rather than pass it to skiers. A skier-facing "snow guarantee" add-on is an open product niche, if an insurer prices it.

## 3. Snow-removal contractors and municipalities: contract structures, derivative use, budget overruns

### Takeaway
Contractor exposure depends on contract type. Per-push or per-event contractors lose in low-snow winters. Seasonal fixed-fee contractors lose in high-snow winters, because their costs scale with snow and revenue does not. Municipalities lose in high-snow winters, with recent overruns in the tens of millions (Boston about $47M in winter 2025-26). Insurers now market both low-snow and high-snow parametric covers to these buyers. I found no documented municipal or contractor purchase with disclosed terms.

### Cited Findings
**Contractor contract structures**
- Seasonal flat rate means one charge for a defined winter scope. Per-push or per-event means a charge per clearing. The two structures allocate weather risk differently: per-push leaves weather risk with the property owner. — [BrightView "Snow Removal Contracts 101"](https://www.brightview.com/resources/article/snow-removal-contracts-101); [Jobber](https://www.getjobber.com/academy/snow-removal/snow-removal-contract/)
- Hybrids are common, e.g., seasonal "up to 30 events; events beyond bill per-push". Caps count events, not inches, which protects the contractor's downside in heavy winters. — [Aspire, "New Rules of Snow Contracts"](https://www.youraspire.com/blog/climate-proof-snow-contracts); [Aspire, pricing volatility](https://www.youraspire.com/blog/snow-removal-contracts-pricing-volatility)
- In a mild winter, seasonal contracts offset lost per-event income. Contractors treat a mix of contract types as a portfolio hedge. — [BOSS Plow blog](https://info.bossplow.com/Blog/bid/135727/How-Snow-Removal-Contracts-And-Stock-Portfolios-Are-Alike)
- SIMA (Snow & Ice Management Association) offers "Snowtistics", a data product for structuring snow contracts. SIMA also lists MSI GuaranteedWeather/Vortex as a member-facing insurance product, so the industry body markets weather cover to contractors. — [SIMA Snowtistics](https://sima.org/products/snowtistics-transforming-snow-removal-contracts?hsLang=en); [SIMA: MSI GuaranteedWeather](https://sima.org/products/vortex-weather-insurance/msi-guaranteed-weather)
- Parametric low-snow covers are explicitly targeted at snow-removal contractors (Demex Revenue Protection, Vortex). — [Artemis, 2022](https://www.artemis.bm/news/demex-launches-parametric-snow-covers-backed-by-nephila-reinsurers/); [Reinsurance News, 2025](https://www.reinsurancene.ws/vortex-launches-parametric-snow-insurance/)

**Municipal budgets (winter 2025-26 unless noted)**
- **Boston:** "snow and winter management" ran about $47M over budget, the heaviest season since 2015. The city projects a $48.4M deficit for next fiscal year, driven by snow costs. The budget team plans around a 5-year snowfall average. — [GBH News, Mar 30 2026](https://www.wgbh.org/news/politics/2026-03-30/boston-projects-48m-budget-deficit-next-fiscal-year-driven-by-snow-removal-costs)
- **Other Massachusetts cities:**
  - Newton: costs were expected to exceed a $5M budget, with $1.5M moved from a snow reserve.
  - Haverhill: the FY2026 snow and ice account ($1.21M) was overspent, and council authorized deficit spending.
  - Methuen: shifted $700K from salary lines. — [Ocean State Media](https://www.oceanstatemedia.org/economy/plows-salt-and-overtime-snow-removal-budgets-strained-across-southern-new-england?p=21); [CNHI](https://www.cnhi.com/rss_feed/winter-storms-bury-snow-removal-budgets/)
- **Canadian examples:**
  - Barrie, ON: about $11M spent against a $6M budget (+$4.7M). — [CTV Barrie](https://www.ctvnews.ca/barrie/article/barries-brutal-winter-blows-control-operations-nearly-5m-over-budget/)
  - Sudbury, ON: one late storm cost $7.8M on top of a $2.3M expected overrun; $3.2M was drawn from the winter-control reserve. — [CTV Northern Ontario](https://www.ctvnews.ca/northern-ontario/article/late-winter-storm-cost-the-city-78m-report-says/)
  - Saskatoon: projected 2025 deficit of $3.5M. — [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/11391757/winter-maintenance-report-shortfalls-saskatoon-snow-clearing)
  - Fort St. John, BC: $484K over after 162 cm fell Oct-Dec 2025. — [CJDC](https://www.cjdctv.com/news/article/fort-st-john-exceeds-snow-removal-budget-by-484k-amid-heavy-end-of-year-snowfall)
- Municipal hedging tools in practice are reserves ("snow reserve", "winter control reserve") and inter-account transfers. — [Ocean State Media](https://www.oceanstatemedia.org/economy/plows-salt-and-overtime-snow-removal-budgets-strained-across-southern-new-england?p=21); [CTV Northern Ontario](https://www.ctvnews.ca/northern-ontario/article/late-winter-storm-cost-the-city-78m-report-says/)
- Demex's high-snow "Cost Control" cover lists municipalities as target buyers. — [Artemis, 2022](https://www.artemis.bm/news/demex-launches-parametric-snow-covers-backed-by-nephila-reinsurers/)

### Inferences
- Contractors are not uniformly "long snow". The per-push contractor is long snow and wants a low-snow put, so it sits on the same side as the resort. The seasonal fixed-fee contractor is short snow and wants a high-snow call, so it sits on the same side as the city. A market maker must tag each contractor by contract mix before pairing.
- City overruns are concentrated in extreme years. Boston budgets to a 5-year mean and was about $47M over in the heaviest year since 2015. The natural city hedge is an out-of-the-money call on seasonal snowfall at the city's own airport (Boston Logan). That is exactly the station basis CME used and that Kalshi uses now, so basis risk is small for cities but large for mountain resorts.
- Rough scale: one large city's tail need (tens of millions) dwarfs a single resort's early-season cover (about $1-2M at Mountain Creek's scale).

- The share of US commercial snow contracts that are seasonal versus per-push (SIMA survey data not retrieved).
- Snowmobile-town and winter-apparel-retailer sensitivity data (beyond the POW/UNH aggregate job figures) not retrieved.

## 4. Exchange-traded availability now: Kalshi snow markets, CME weather (HDD/CDD), and basis risk

### Takeaway
Kalshi lists per-city monthly snowfall markets settled on NWS climate reports: NYC, Boston, Chicago, Detroit, Dallas and others, plus storm, snow-day, white-Christmas and city-comparison series. ### Inferences
- Kalshi's snow markets are city/airport products whose natural users are cities and urban contractors. For mountain resorts, basis risk is severe: Vail, Breckenridge and Park City snowfall is only loosely related to Denver or Salt Lake City airport snow, and no Kalshi mountain-station market was found. This repeats the AgriHedge airport-gauge problem, likely worse because orographic snowfall decouples from valley totals.
- Kalshi depth is event-driven: Dallas reached $250K in a storm month, while routine Dec books sat at $1-6K. That supports the project's liquidity finding (doc 22) that saved depth must grow about 5-10x for meaningful hedging. A market maker would have to supply most of the liquidity.
- CME HDD/CDD is the only liquid exchange tool for winter-temperature risk, at city stations (e.g., Denver, NYC). A resort's snowmaking-hours risk could be proxied with an early-season HDD or a custom "hours below 28°F wet-bulb" index. That index exists only OTC.

## 5. Natural pairings: which opposite exposures could a market maker match?

### Takeaway
The cleanest opposite pair is a resort or per-push contractor (pays when snow is low) against a city or seasonal-contract contractor (pays when snow is high), on the same station and region. The Northeast (Boston/NYC stations with New England resorts) is the most plausible geography. Winter temperature is a different matter: a warm winter hurts resorts and gas utilities together, so utilities are not a natural counterparty for resort warm-winter risk. Heating-cost bearers (cities, schools and other large heating buyers) are.

### Cited Findings
- Resorts lose disproportionately in low-snow years (about -10% visits nationally, more than $1B; asymmetric). — [POW Economic Report, 2019](https://protectourwinters.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/POW_EconReport_v22.pdf)
- Cities lose in high-snow years. Boston was about $47M over in its heaviest season since 2015, and the Massachusetts cities above drew on reserves. — [GBH News, 2026](https://www.wgbh.org/news/politics/2026-03-30/boston-projects-48m-budget-deficit-next-fiscal-year-driven-by-snow-removal-costs); [Ocean State Media](https://www.oceanstatemedia.org/economy/plows-salt-and-overtime-snow-removal-budgets-strained-across-southern-new-england?p=21)
- Insurers already structure both sides: Demex sells low-snow Revenue Protection (contractors) and high-snow Cost Control (municipalities, facilities), backed by Nephila derivatives. A reinsurer or ILS fund is effectively warehousing and netting opposite snow exposures. — [Artemis, 2022](https://www.artemis.bm/news/demex-launches-parametric-snow-covers-backed-by-nephila-reinsurers/)
- Vortex/MSI sells "less than threshold" snow cover to resorts and contractors (the low-snow side). — [Reinsurance News, 2025](https://www.reinsurancene.ws/vortex-launches-parametric-snow-insurance/)
- CME snowfall contracts at Boston Logan and Central Park were the precedent for city-station snow pairing. They were delisted, apparently for lack of use. — [CME 2006](https://www.cmegroup.com/media-room/press-releases/2006/2/09/cme_will_launch_snowfallindexcontractsforbostonandnewyorkcity.html); [CFTC 2014](https://cftc.gov/IndustryOversight/IndustryFilings/PTCDCMRules/30664)

### Inferences
- **Pair A (snowfall, same station): Boston city budget (long a high-snow call) against New England per-push contractors and Boston-area small resorts (long a low-snow put).** The two hold opposite sides of the same Logan seasonal-snowfall distribution. A market maker can net them and hedge the residual on Kalshi's Boston monthly strikes. Basis risk is low for the city and contractors, moderate to high for resorts 50-150 miles away.
- **Pair B (snowfall, regional basis): Western destination resorts against Front Range and Wasatch cities.** The basis is weaker: mountain and city snow correlate imperfectly, and Denver city snow differs from Summit County snow. This needs gridded (ERA5) or SNOTEL settlement, which Kalshi does not offer.
- **Pair C (temperature): resort snowmaking-window warmth risk.** The resort wants protection against a warm Nov-Dec (Mountain Creek's 33°F trigger). Gas utilities and heating-oil distributors also lose in warm winters, so they are on the same side, not counterparties. Natural counterparties for warm-winter risk are parties that lose in cold winters: municipal and school heating budgets, large building operators, and power or gas buyers exposed to cold-snap price spikes. Cities thus appear on the opposite side of resorts in both the snowfall and temperature pairs, a useful double coincidence.
- **Asymmetry matters for pricing.** Resorts want deep out-of-the-money low-snow puts, and cities want deep out-of-the-money high-snow calls. Both are tail products on opposite tails, so they do not net in normal years. A market maker pairing them collects two premiums, pays at most one side in a given winter, and keeps tail risk on whichever side fires. This is an insurance book, not a swap book, and needs capital, as Demex's reliance on Nephila shows.
- **Contractor classification.** Per-push contractors pair with cities. Seasonal-fee contractors pair with resorts. A contractor with a hybrid book may already be internally hedged (per BOSS Plow's "portfolio" framing).

### Gaps
- Correlation between airport-station seasonal snowfall (Logan, Denver, SLC) and nearby resort snowfall or skier visits. That would quantify Pair A and Pair B basis risk; it is not in the literature retrieved and would need SNOTEL/GHCN analysis.
- Evidence that any party has actually netted resort and municipal snow risk in one book. Demex's two-sided product suggests it, but no portfolio disclosure was found.
- Winter-apparel retailer and snowmobile-town sensitivities, which would add more "long snow" demand to the low-snow side. Not retrieved in this pass.
