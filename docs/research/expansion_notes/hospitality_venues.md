# Wet-side buyers: rain and weather revenue risk for restaurants, patio bars, venues, events, sports, parks and golf

Scope: who would buy "wet" (too much rain) protection as a natural counterparty to farmers' "dry" protection; how weather-sensitive their revenue is; what products exist; what they cost; what contract design they need. US focus. Research date 2026-11-19. Around 20 searches and fetches. Most numbers come from secondary press or vendor sources and are marked as such.

## 1. How weather-sensitive are these businesses' revenues?

### Takeaway
Rain effects are real but modest when averaged over a month, and large on specific days. A bad-weather day removes about 25-40% of that day's in-store sales for an outdoor-exposed retailer. Monthly restaurant comps differ by roughly 1-1.5 percentage points between rainy and dry months. Golf loses about 2.2% of rounds per extra inch of monthly rain. Theme parks report quarter-level attendance swings of 6-17% blamed on rain. The loss sits in the day or hour, not the month. Rigorous per-rainy-day estimates for restaurants and patio bars specifically are scarce.

### Cited Findings
**General retail (academic, strongest evidence)**
- Roth Tran, "Sellin' in the Rain": daily store sales from 100+ stores of a national apparel and sporting-goods brand (Apr 2010 to Dec 2013), matched to NOAA station weather. A "moderately bad" weather day cuts net sales by about one-quarter of a day's sales, and a "very bad" day by about 40% (closure days excluded) — [FRBSF Economic Letter 2022-23](https://www.frbsf.org/research-and-insights/publications/economic-letter/2022/08/impact-of-weather-on-retail-sales/)
- Same study: bad-weather losses "are persistent and not offset over time". The boost from good weather is partly reversed and is no longer significant after about 4 weeks. Online sales do not rise on bad in-store weather days — [FRBSF Economic Letter 2022](https://www.frbsf.org/research-and-insights/publications/economic-letter/2022/08/impact-of-weather-on-retail-sales/)
- Same study: a one-standard-deviation one-day weather shock shifts sales by about 10% over four weeks. Published in Management Science 69(12):7423-7447 (2023) — [FRBSF working paper 2022-02](https://www.frbsf.org/research-and-insights/publications/working-papers/2022/02/sellin-in-the-rain-weather-climate-and-retail-sales/); [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormnsc:v:69:y:2023:i:12:p:7423-7447)
- Adaptation: declines from more than 2 inches of precipitation are about 80% smaller where such rain is historically common. Precipitation moves some sales from outdoor to indoor shopping centers — [FRBSF Economic Letter 2022](https://www.frbsf.org/research-and-insights/publications/economic-letter/2022/08/impact-of-weather-on-retail-sales/)
- Starr-McCluer (Fed Board FEDS 2000-08): with monthly data, unusual weather has a "modest but significant" effect on retail sales. It is largely temporary, and lagged responses wash out the effect at a quarterly frequency. Specific coefficients are not verified here (abstract only) — [Federal Reserve FEDS 2000-08](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/the-effects-of-weather-on-retail-sales.htm)
- Note the conflict: Starr-McCluer (monthly aggregate retail) finds weather washes out, while Roth Tran (daily, store-level, bad weather) finds losses persist. Both are cited above.

**Restaurants**
- APT data, June 2015: quick-service restaurant (QSR) same-store sales were +5.1% where June had fewer than 5 rainy days, versus +3.5% where it had more than 5. Full-service: +2.8% versus +2.0%. July 2015, all restaurants: +3.3% versus +2.1% — [QSR Web (2015)](https://www.qsrweb.com/news/rain-impacts-june-restaurant-sales/); [FastCasual](https://www.fastcasual.com/news/rain-impacts-june-restaurant-sales/)
- An Ohio State researcher quoted by Restaurant Dive found that "rain didn't impact sales" and "temperature was the number one factor". It was a small, single-region study — [Restaurant Dive](https://restaurantdive.com/news/fair-weather-diners-how-temperature-influences-consumer-cravings/549897)
- UK example (Tenzo, a vendor): restaurants on its system fell about 27% year on year from 28 Feb to 2 Mar during the "Beast from the East" snow event. This was an extreme multi-day event, not ordinary rain — [Tenzo](https://www.gotenzo.com/resources/insight/brace-yourself-restaurateurs-winter-is-coming-the-huge-effects-of-snow-on-restaurants-pubs-and-cafes/)
- Planalytics "weather sensitivity" metric for restaurants peaks above 5% nationally in Nov and Dec. This covers all weather, not just rain — [Planalytics](https://www.planalytics.com/?p=11419) (snippet only, unverified detail)

**Patio and outdoor dining exposure**
- National Restaurant Association (NRA) survey, Sept 2021: 65% of operators offered on-premises outdoor dining (72% of full-service, 57% of limited-service). 35% of those said outdoor seating is more than 40% of average daily sales. Only 36% planned to keep outdoor seating through fall and winter — [NRA, "Customer demand for outdoor dining rose"](https://www.restaurant.org/articles/news/customer-demand-for-outdoor-dining-rose)
- NRA tracking (year not shown in the snippet, probably 2020-21): outdoor dining offered by 67% of operators in September, 49% in November and 40% in February. This shows strong seasonality — [NRA](https://www.restaurant.org/articles/news/customer-demand-for-outdoor-dining-rose) (snippet; date unverified)
- NRA: 84% of adults favor letting restaurants keep sidewalk and street tables — [NRA](https://restaurant.org/articles/news/consumers-want-to-keep-streeteries-in-place)

**Baseball**
- 50 years of MLB data: attendance falls about 16% above 95°F, with effects from both heat and cold — [Kuruc, LoPalo & O'Connor](https://nbviewer.org/github/kevinkuruc/Papers_for_Download/blob/main/Baseball.pdf)
- Ge, Humphreys & Zhou (J. Sports Economics 21(3), 2020): past rain shocks raise future attendance by about 200 fans per game, so some demand is shifted rather than lost — [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:sae:jospec:v:21:y:2020:i:3:p:304-322)
- Minnesota Twins 2013: a cold, snowy and rainy stretch cost revenue "deep into seven figures" across walk-up sales, no-shows and per-capita concessions — [Sports Business Journal 2013](https://www.sportsbusinessdaily.com/Journal/Issues/2013/04/29/Franchises/Twins.aspx)
- About $4M average revenue per MLB game (tickets, food, merchandise). This was a lockout-loss estimate, not a rainout figure. Unverified for rain use — [The Hustle 2022](https://thehustle.co/03032022-canceled-baseball-games)
- Minor league (Portland Sea Dogs): pre-sold tickets "cushion" but do not replace rainout losses. Concessions and walk-up sales are lost — [Mainebiz](https://mainebiz.biz/article/early-season-rainouts-dampen-ballpark-business-for-portland-sea-dogs/)

**Theme and amusement parks**
- Cedar Fair Q2 2023: attendance −6% to 7.4M. California parks −17%, attributed to rain, while Midwest parks were +7%. The company estimated heat, heavy rain and wildfire smoke cost about 300,000 visits in the quarter. These figures are company-attributed — [The Columbian, Aug 2023](https://columbian.com/news/2023/aug/19/knotts-berry-farm-reports-attendance-drop-at-parks); [Newsio](https://newsio.com/2023/09/01/bad-weather-dampens-theme-park-traffic/?amp=1)
- Cedar Fair June 2003: −7% attendance, blamed on early-season weather — [PointBuzz 2003](https://pointbuzz.com/News/Story/101); [UPI 2004, "Rain has amusement parks lamenting"](https://www.upi.com/Business_News/2004/08/17/Rain-has-amusement-parks-lamenting/96321092777366/)
- Industry observers say park operators often use weather to excuse bad seasons, so treat company attributions skeptically — [InterTheme Park](https://interthemepark.com/observation-62926-weather-prediction.html)

**Golf**
- National Golf Foundation (NGF) and Golf Datatech, using weathertrends360 analysis: each additional inch of rain year over year is associated with about 2.2% fewer rounds played — [NGF May 2025 Rounds Played Report](https://www.ngf.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/May-2025-National-Rounds-Played-Report.pdf); [NGF July 2026 report](https://www.ngf.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/July-2026-National-Rounds-Played-Report.pdf)
- NGF: weather causes an average national swing of 2-3% in rounds per year. In 2018, weather drove a 4.8% national decline, with about 55 more rain or weather-impacted days than 2017 — [NGF 2018 summary](https://ngf.org/?p=26529); [NGF "Weather to Play"](https://www.ngf.org/?p=26494)
- Course-level example: a Massachusetts course lost 20 full days to rain in a season, double its average — [Golf Digest, "Bad weather blues"](https://www.golfdigest.com/story/bad-weather-blues)

**Festivals and concerts**
- Broker estimate: clients "typically stand to lose around 20 percent of their projected revenues" from weather-related departures. This is exposure on a bad day, not average loss — [TSE Entertainment](https://tseentertainment.com/outdoor-music-festival-concert-insurance)

### Inferences
- For a single-site restaurant or patio bar, the hedgeable loss is roughly the patio share of sales times the loss on a rainy hour or evening. With NRA's figure that a third of patio operators get more than 40% of daily sales outdoors, a rained-out Friday or Saturday evening could cost a patio-heavy venue 20-40% of that day's revenue. Monthly totals barely move, since a 1-1.5 point difference in monthly comps is small next to the premium load. This is an inference, not a measured number.
- Because Roth Tran finds bad-weather losses are not recovered later, a wet-side hedge is protecting real lost revenue, not just a timing shift. For baseball, the Ge et al. finding implies partial recovery.
- Sensitivity is largest where rain is unusual (Roth Tran adaptation result). A drought-prone farm region where rain is rare might therefore have the most rain-sensitive venues, which supports the farmer-venue natural-counterparty story. Not tested.

### Gaps
- No Toast, Square, Womply or OpenTable rain-versus-sales study was found in this pass. One may exist, but it was not surfaced.
- No peer-reviewed per-rainy-day percentage for restaurants or bars specifically. The Ohio State finding of no rain effect conflicts with the APT pattern.
- No reliable per-rainout cost for MLB. A "$500K per postponed game, 2018 study" figure circulates but comes from an unreliable site and is excluded.
- No festival attendance-versus-rain elasticity was found.
- Starr-McCluer's numeric coefficients were not retrieved (full PDF not read).

## 2. Timescale mismatch: daily and event-day contracts versus monthly totals; what Kalshi offers

### Takeaway
Venue losses happen on specific days and hours, but Kalshi does now list daily rain contracts, not only monthly ones. They include a daily NYC (Central Park) yes/no rain market and a multi-city "Where will it rain today?" series, alongside monthly totals (KXRAINNYCM) and daily high and low temperature markets. The daily contracts are binary on more than 0 inches at one station for a full calendar day. That is still a poor match for a Friday 6-10pm patio window and a site miles from the station.

### Cited Findings
- Kalshi lists a daily "Rain in NYC" market (ticker family KXRAINNYC, e.g. KXRAINNYC-26JUN18, KXRAINNYC-26JUL15) and a "daily-rain-nyc" series (KXRAINDNYC-260919) — [Kalshi KXRAINNYC](https://kalshi.com/markets/kxrainnyc/nyc-rain/kxrainnyc-26jun18); [Kalshi KXRAINDNYC](https://kalshi.com/markets/kxraindnyc/daily-rain-nyc/kxraindnyc-260919)
- Kalshi has a multi-city daily series, "Where will it rain today?" (KXRAIN, e.g. KXRAIN-26NOV13). The page could not be fetched (HTTP 429), so the city list and thresholds are unverified — [Kalshi KXRAIN](https://kalshi.com/markets/kxrain/where-will-it-rain-daily/kxrain-26nov13)
- Monthly NYC rain: the RAINNYCM underlying is monthly precipitation inches from NWS Daily Climate Reports (CLI) for Central Park, summing the latest daily report for each day. CFTC rule filings for RAINNYCM revisions are dated Aug 21 and Sept 2, 2026 (PDF metadata). Text extraction failed, so details beyond the title and CLI link are unverified — [Kalshi RAINNYCM terms](https://kalshi-public-docs.s3.amazonaws.com/contract_terms/RAINNYCM.pdf); [CFTC filing 08/21/26](https://www.cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rules08212619089.pdf); [CFTC filing 09/02/26](https://www.cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rules09022622984.pdf)
- Daily NYC rain resolves Yes if the Central Park total for the date is strictly greater than 0. Per third-party guides, trace ("T") settles as 0.00. These are secondary sources; confirm in Kalshi's rulebook — [Solflare mirror of KXRAINNYC-26JUN08](https://www.solflare.com/prediction/climate-and-weather/event/KXRAINNYC-26JUN08/); [precipitation-ai guide](https://precipitation-ai.pages.dev/learn/nws-settlement-data)
- Kalshi also lists daily highest-temperature contracts (NHIGH terms), lowest-temperature in NYC (KXLOWTNYC), and an "hourly temperature" category. A heat or cold trigger is therefore available for venues where temperature drives demand (MLB attendance −16% above 95°F; golf; restaurants per the Ohio State study) — [Kalshi NHIGH terms](https://kalshi-public-docs.s3.amazonaws.com/contract_terms/NHIGH.pdf); [Kalshi KXLOWTNYC](https://kalshi.com/markets/kxlowtnyc/lowest-temperature-in-nyc/kxlowtnyc-26oct29); [Kalshi hourly temperature](https://kalshi.com/category/climate/hourly-temperature)
- Robinhood resells Kalshi's monthly "Rain in NYC" events — [Robinhood](https://robinhood.com/us/en/prediction-markets/climate/events/rain-in-nyc-in-jan-2026-nov-13-2025)
- Commercial event-weather products use hour-window triggers, not monthly ones. Sensible Weather pays by "the number of hours the experience is negatively impacted by weather" — [Sensible help center](https://sensiblehelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/40877082840211-How-do-Weather-Guarantees-work). Beazley's UK cover pays a share of projected gross revenue if a preset rainfall level falls in a preset time window — [Artemis](https://www.artemis.bm/news/beazley-adds-parametric-rainfall-cover-to-event-cancellation-product/); [Insurance Journal 2016](https://amp.insurancejournal.com/news/international/2016/08/17/423542.htm). Descartes uses temporary on-site weather stations at festivals — [Descartes Barcelona case study](https://descartesunderwriting.com/case-studies/how-parametric-rainfall-insurance-could-protect-major-music-festival-barcelona)
- US parametric event triggers are simple. Spectrum Weather Insurance's examples are "an inch of rain", 100°F, or hurricane landfall within 25 miles, with a $20,000 example payout. Small festivals, county fairs and charity golf tournaments are typical buyers — [Insurance Business, "Was it hot enough?"](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/wholesale/was-it-hot-enough--the-rise-and-risk-of-parametric-insurance-for-weatherexposed-events-535882.aspx)

### Inferences
- Design implication: a venue contract needs (a) a daily or hourly window (for example, rain of 0.10 inch or more between 4pm and 10pm on Fri or Sat), (b) a meaningful intensity threshold rather than "more than 0" (a trace or 0.01 inch shower rarely empties a patio), and (c) a station close to the venue. Kalshi's NWS daily CLI reports give only daily totals at airports or Central Park. Hourly data would need ASOS or METAR hourly precipitation, a different settlement source.
- The farmer-venue "natural counterparty" claim is weak on timing. Farmers' PRF exposure is two-month interval totals, while venues lose on specific evenings. A monthly-total contract hedges venues badly, so a market maker would hold mismatched books unless it builds a daily-to-monthly aggregation (for example, a strip of daily contracts whose sum approximates a monthly-total exposure). Also, the companion months (Nov-Feb) are low patio season (NRA: 40% of operators kept outdoor dining in February versus 67% in September).
- The geographic overlap is also poor. Sample ranches are rural Texas, Oklahoma and Plains grids, while daily Kalshi rain is listed mainly for big-city stations (NYC confirmed, others unverified).

### Gaps
- Full list of KXRAIN daily cities, contract thresholds, volume and open interest: page rate-limited (429), not verified.
- Whether Kalshi offers any intraday or hourly rain contract: none found.
- Official daily-rain contract-terms PDF not retrieved; trace handling is from secondary sources only.

## 3. Existing products, providers, pricing and US legal status (insurance versus swap or event contract)

### Takeaway
Wet-side protection exists in three forms. (1) Traditional event-cancellation or "rain" insurance (Lloyd's market, Beazley, Tokio Marine HCC, Spectrum), priced from historical date-and-location probabilities. (2) Parametric covers (Descartes, Arbol via Lloyd's coverholder with Beazley, Beazley UK), large and broker-sold. (3) Embedded consumer "weather guarantees" (Sensible Weather), costing about 8-12% of the booking. Nothing found targets single-site restaurants or bars. Legally, a contract paying on an index without proof of loss is likely a swap or event contract under CFTC jurisdiction rather than state-regulated insurance. That helps a Kalshi-based design but rules out calling it insurance.

### Cited Findings
**Sensible Weather (consumer and embedded)**
- Raised a $12M Series A led by Infinity Ventures, more than $20M total; another aggregator lists $22.2M — [Built In](https://builtin.com/articles/sensible-weather-raises-12m-series-a-hiring); [CB Insights / coverager](https://coverager.com/company/sensible-weather/)
- Price: "We often wind up with a Weather Guarantee that costs 8-12% of the trip cost", cheaper in dry places such as Arizona. Partners included PGA of America and the Renwick hotel (Manhattan). Article dated Apr 20, 2023 — [Entrepreneur 2023](https://www.entrepreneur.com/living/one-company-will-pay-you-to-enjoy-bad-weather-on-vacation/449743)
- Uptake (vendor and partner claims, unverified): more than 300,000 Weather Guarantees bought on Campspot since 2021. 89% of 2023 survey respondents (survey run by Sensible) said it was "well worth the price". Distribution through Campspot, KOA, Tentrr, Hipcamp (June 2024) and Huttopia (150+ sites) — [Campspot](https://software.campspot.com/blog/what-is-campspot-accelerator/); [Hipcamp launch, BusinessWire 2024](https://business.mammothtimes.com/mammothtimes/article/bizwire-2024-6-18-hipcamp-launches-a-weather-guarantee-powered-by-sensible-weather-to-protect-campers-across-the-usa-from-rain)
- Payout is automatic, based on measured hours of weather impact. Hour thresholds vary by place and season — [Sensible help](https://sensiblehelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/40877082840211-How-do-Weather-Guarantees-work); [Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/living/one-company-will-pay-you-to-enjoy-bad-weather-on-vacation/449743)
- Status: marketed as a "weather-protection product", not insurance. KOA's FAQ says Weather Guarantees "are not insurance products". Commentators (Bloomberg, a Substack analysis) call Sensible an insurance company. Licensing and underwriting are not confirmed — [Travel Weekly](https://www.travelweekly.com/Travel-News/Hotel-News/Company-insures-against-rainy-day); [KOA FAQ](https://koa.com/contact-us/faqs/24/2411); [Bloomberg 2023](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-03-31/travel-insurance-for-rain-sensible-weather-gives-money-back-for-ruined-vacation); [Substack](https://garrettawolfe.substack.com/p/sensible-weather-big-data-for-a-rainy)
- July 2026: TCP Insurance Agency (Great American Insurance Group) began offering Sensible Weather Guarantees to protect film shoots, a move into commercial buyers — [FinTech Global, Jul 13 2026](https://fintech.global/2026/07/13/sensible-weather-and-tcp-insurance-protect-film-shoots/)

**Parametric and commercial**
- Descartes excess-rain product: up to $80M capacity per contract ($100M in Australia), objective precipitation triggers. Has event-cancellation and event-postponement products and a hospitality-group typhoon case — [Descartes excess precipitation](https://descartesunderwriting.com/solutions/excess-precipitation); [Descartes event postponement whitepaper](https://descartesunderwriting.com/whitepaper/insurance-for-event-postponement-due-to-bad-weather); [Descartes hospitality typhoon](https://descartesunderwriting.com/case-studies/parametric-insurance-against-typhoon-hospitality-group)
- Arbol: Lloyd's coverholder for a Beazley parametric product. Rainfall protection for "outdoor activities, events, and tourism", sold only through agents and brokers, priced by an AI underwriter — [PR Newswire, Beazley/Arbol](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/beazley-launches-parametric-climate-risk-management-product-with-new-lloyds-coverholder-arbol-301834728.html); [Arbol travel](https://www.arbol.io/solutions/travel); [Arbol Parametric 101](https://arbol.io/help/agents-brokers/the-basics)
- Beazley (2016, UK): wet-weather revenue cover for events paying a share of projected gross revenue on a preset rainfall level — [Insurance Journal 2016](https://amp.insurancejournal.com/news/international/2016/08/17/423542.htm); [Carrier Management](https://www.carriermanagement.com/news/2016/08/17/157871.htm)
- Tokio Marine HCC markets weather insurance for fall events beyond cancellation (2026) — [TMHCC, Sept 1 2026](https://www.tmhcc.com/en-us/news-and-articles/thought-leadership/beyond-cancellation-protecting-fall-events-with-weather-insurance)
- Premiums reflect the historical probability of adverse weather at the location, date and time, and the policy amount. Buyers insure a chosen share of revenue or expenses up to 100% — [TSE Entertainment](https://tseentertainment.com/outdoor-music-festival-concert-insurance)
- Price trend: festival premiums are reportedly rising and capacity shrinking, with a mid-size festival's former "modest fee" becoming prohibitive. Vendor blog, unverified — [Ticket Fairy](https://www.ticketfairy.com/blog/surviving-the-festival-insurance-crunch-managing-rising-premiums-and-limited-coverage)
- Consumer sports example: Chicago Cubs group rainout insurance requires 15+ tickets bought at least 21 days ahead. It refunds ticket cost less the premium and excludes delays and official games — [MLB.com Cubs](https://mlb.com/cubs/tickets/group-tickets/rainout-insurance)
- Event insurance market size: an Insurance Business headline refers to a "$1 billion event insurance market" (all perils, not just weather; article not read) — [Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/breaking-news/world-cup-final-puts-1-billion-event-insurance-market-in-focus-582784.aspx)

**US legal classification**
- 2012 CFTC/SEC rule: the insurance safe harbor from the "swap" definition requires an insurable interest held throughout the contract, a loss that occurs and is proved, payment capped at that interest, and a provider regulated by a state insurance commissioner — [National Law Review, ASKramer](https://natlawreview.com/node/271309/printable/print)
- A contract paying on a trigger without proof of loss "is likely to be characterized as a derivative". Tax treatment differs by classification — [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/node/271309/printable/print)
- Prof. Daniel Schwarcz (Insurance Business, Nov 9 2026): an area-wide trigger (for example, a storm that shuts 20% of local businesses) "sounds like a swap", and federal preemption then removes state insurance authority. Small single-location businesses face much larger basis risk than multi-site buyers. "Very few" prediction-market users use them to hedge, though one insurer hedged a $3M coaching bonus on Kalshi — [Insurance Business, Nov 9 2026](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/catastrophe/is-a-kalshistyle-weather-contract-insurance-look-to-insurable-interest-596536.aspx)
- A November 2026 industry article says weather prediction markets are raising questions about overlap with insurance — [ClaimsPages, Nov 9 2026](https://www.claimspages.com/news/weather-prediction-markets-raise-questions-about-insurance-coverage-and-risk-20261109/)
- Policy conflict: a CFTC comment argues parametric contracts "are undoubtedly swaps" and that the CFTC should police or expressly cede them. A New York bill would authorize retail parametric insurance that would not meet the CFTC safe harbor — [CFTC comment](https://comments2.cftc.gov/Handlers/PdfHandler.ashx?id=33637); [Better Insurance Substack, NY bill](https://betterins.substack.com/p/ny-parametric-insurance-bill)
- Kalshi sports-contract litigation (context for the legal stability of event contracts): the Third Circuit held on Apr 6 (2-1; year not stated in the article, presumably 2026) that sports contracts are swaps; the Ninth Circuit sided with Nevada; the Sixth Circuit ruled against Kalshi on Sept 25; a Supreme Court petition is pending — [Insurance Business, Nov 9 2026](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/catastrophe/is-a-kalshistyle-weather-contract-insurance-look-to-insurable-interest-596536.aspx)

### Inferences
- A restaurant can legally buy a Kalshi daily-rain contract today as a CFTC-regulated event contract. But AgriHedge cannot market it as "insurance" or say it pays losses. Selling indemnity-style cover would need a licensed insurer or a Lloyd's coverholder such as Arbol.
- Sensible Weather's 8-12% of booking value is a useful price benchmark for retail embedded weather protection. It implies a large load over the expected payout, which is not disclosed.
- The commercial parametric market (Descartes, $80M capacity) serves large events. The single-restaurant segment has no dedicated product found, which is either an opportunity or a sign the economics do not work.

### Gaps
- No published premium rates, rates on line or minimum premiums for US event rain insurance. Brokers quote case by case.
- Sensible Weather's partner commission (one secondary source said 2%, unverified), attach rates and risk carrier were not found.
- WeatherBill (later The Climate Corporation, acquired by Monsanto in 2013) and its small-business weather products were not researched in this pass. It is historically relevant as a failed SMB weather-hedge attempt; history from training knowledge only, unverified here.
- No US regulator ruling specific to a small business buying a weather event contract as a hedge.

## 4. Market size: restaurants with outdoor seating, events, festivals; addressable SMB weather-risk market

### Takeaway
About two-thirds of US restaurant operators offered outdoor dining in 2021 (NRA). The NRA counts roughly a million US restaurant locations, but that total was not verified this pass. Current outdoor-seating counts, festival counts and any SMB weather-hedge market estimate were not found from reliable sources.

### Cited Findings

- No estimate of an addressable SMB weather-risk market was found.

## 5. Behavioral and demand barriers: would small restaurants buy?

### Takeaway
Demand evidence is thin and comes from vendors. Consumers buy embedded weather guarantees at checkout when they are cheap and automatic (Sensible: 300k+ on one platform). There is no evidence found of small restaurants buying standalone weather hedges. Experts flag basis risk for single-site businesses as the core obstacle, alongside price loads and low hedging use of prediction markets.

### Cited Findings
- Embedded checkout model works for consumers: 300k+ Weather Guarantees on Campspot, and 89% of a Sensible-run survey said it was worth it (vendor, unverified) — [Campspot](https://software.campspot.com/blog/what-is-campspot-accelerator/)
- Single-site businesses face much larger basis risk than multi-site buyers, and "very few" prediction-market users hedge — [Insurance Business, Nov 9 2026](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/catastrophe/is-a-kalshistyle-weather-contract-insurance-look-to-insurable-interest-596536.aspx)
- Threshold "cliff" frustration: parametric buyers can lose badly with no payout (98°F versus a 100°F trigger; landfall at 32 miles versus 25). The insurer stresses that buyers must understand triggers and coverage periods — [Insurance Business, "Was it hot enough?"](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/wholesale/was-it-hot-enough--the-rise-and-risk-of-parametric-insurance-for-weatherexposed-events-535882.aspx)
- Venues adapt operationally (indoor shift, rescheduling, pre-sales), which reduces the residual to hedge. Roth Tran finds large adaptation where rain is common; Portland Sea Dogs notes pre-sold tickets cushion losses — [FRBSF](https://www.frbsf.org/research-and-insights/publications/economic-letter/2022/08/impact-of-weather-on-retail-sales/); [Mainebiz](https://mainebiz.biz/article/early-season-rainouts-dampen-ballpark-business-for-portland-sea-dogs/)
- Rain did not move sales in one Ohio State restaurant study, though it hurt customer mood, so some operators may not perceive the risk — [Restaurant Dive](https://restaurantdive.com/news/fair-weather-diners-how-temperature-influences-consumer-cravings/549897)

### Inferences
- The likeliest wet-side buyers are large or concentrated exposures (festivals, minor-league and MLB teams, theme parks, golf course groups, multi-unit patio chains), not single restaurants. They have larger losses per event, finance staff, and can average out basis risk across sites, as Schwarcz notes. Single restaurants are better reached via an embedded product (point-of-sale or reservation-platform add-on, like Sensible's checkout model) than via a trading account.
- For the farmer-venue pairing, the wet side is fragmented, short-dated and urban. A market maker would mostly be warehousing daily, city-station risk against monthly, rural-grid farmer risk, so the natural offset is weaker than it looks.

### Gaps
- No survey of restaurant or bar owners' willingness to pay for weather protection was found.
- No public attach rates for Sensible Weather (percentage of bookings that buy it).
- No data on whether MLB teams or theme parks buy weather derivatives or insurance (likely private).
