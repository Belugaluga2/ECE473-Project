# Other weather-exposed business segments for a weather-risk market maker

Scope: segments other than ranchers/farmers (except the irrigated vs. dryland contrast), restaurants/outdoor venues, and ski/snow resorts, which other researchers cover. US focus. Research date: 2026-11-19. All figures are from web sources found in this pass. Anything not traced to a primary source is flagged.

## Q1. What quantified evidence exists for each segment's weather sensitivity?

### Takeaway
Hard, citable sensitivity numbers exist for golf (about 2.2% fewer rounds per extra inch of monthly rain), road salt (a mild winter quarter cut salt sales 15%), hydro (California drought cost ratepayers about $2.0-2.5B), water utilities (Sacramento-area districts lost 12% of revenue under 2015 restrictions), gas demand (a colder year raised residential use 11%) and retail categories (about 3.4% of retail sales directly weather-affected). For beer, ice cream, umbrellas, water parks, trucking and rice-vs-dryland, I found only qualitative or non-US evidence.

### Cited Findings

**Economy-wide**
- Lazo et al. (2011, BAMS) found all 11 nongovernmental US sectors statistically weather-sensitive. Weather variability shifts US output by up to about $485B/yr, about 3.4% of 2008 GDP. The paper used 24 years of economic data and 70 years of weather — [LBL abstract](https://eta-publications.lbl.gov/publications/us-economic-sensitivity-weather) (2011)
- Sector sensitivities: mining 14%, agriculture 12%, manufacturing 8%, "finance, insurance and retail" 8%, utilities 7%, services 3%, wholesale 2%, retail trade 2%. New York is most sensitive (13.5% of gross state product), Tennessee least (2.5%) — [UCAR news release](https://news.ucar.edu/4810/economic-cost-weather-may-total-485-billion-us) (2011). Note: the release lists "retail" in both the 8% and 2% groups. The 8% group is probably finance/insurance/real estate; treat it as unverified until the paper is checked. The release gives no construction or transportation figure.

**Energy and utilities**
- 2025 US residential gas consumption averaged 13.3 Bcf/d, 11% above 2024, which EIA attributed to colder winter conditions. A typical January has 831 HDD (10-year average); January 2025 had 927. Natural gas is the primary heating fuel in 47% of homes — [EIA Today in Energy](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=67365) (2025-26)
- In 2023-24, mild December and February weather made the Midwest winter 12% warmer than EIA forecast, so actual gas consumption came in below the October forecast — [EIA Today in Energy](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=61784) (2024)

**Hydro (rain is good)**
- Reduced hydro cost California ratepayers about $2.0B over the four water years ending Sept 2015. Extra fossil generation raised power-plant CO2 about 10%. Replacement power cost averaged about $35/MWh — [Pacific Institute 2015 update](https://pacinst.org/?p=14883) (2016)
- An extended analysis through Sept 2016 put the cost at almost $2.5B. Hydro normally supplies about 18% of California power and fell to about 7% in 2015 — [POWER Magazine](https://www.powermag.com/drought-has-big-impact-on-california-power-market/) (2017)
- Idaho Power reports below-average hydrological conditions in 9 of the last 10 years, offset by purchased power. Its cloud-seeding program raised Payette Basin snowpack about 12% on average (an operational hedge) — [Idaho Power cloud seeding](https://www.idahopower.com/energy/delivering-power/power-plants/cloud-seeding/); [Idaho Power hydro](https://idahopower.com/about-us/energy/energy-sources/hydroelectric/)

**Construction**
- A 30-year Asheville, NC analysis found about 51 rain-delay days per 365 (range 30-77), using daily rainfall thresholds — reported via [SmartPM blog](https://smartpm.com/blog/weather-delays-in-construction) (secondary; primary study not retrieved, unverified)
- The figure of about $40B/yr in US weather delay costs is attributed to Xi et al. (2005) — secondary blogs only, **unverified**
- Contract "weather days" usually cover extreme events, so ordinary heavy rain can still trigger liquidated damages. Parametric covers attach after included weather days are used up — [IRMI](https://www.irmi.com/articles/expert-commentary/construction-risk-management-innovation-harnessing-parametric-weather-insurance-for-a-volatile-climate)

**Retail / beverage / HVAC**
- About 3.4% of retail sales are directly weather-affected (citing AMS research), about $1T globally. Over 90% of weather-driven sales volatility comes from day-to-day temperature and precipitation changes — [NRF / Planalytics, Climate-Proofing Retail](https://cdn.nrf.com/sites/default/files/2024-06/ClimateProofingRetail_2024.pdf) (2024)
- Weather-driven share of annual sales: lawn mowers 17%, heaters 32%, sunscreen 15%, snow removal products 49% — [NRF / Planalytics](https://cdn.nrf.com/sites/default/files/2024-06/ClimateProofingRetail_2024.pdf) (2024; vendor metric)
- US alcohol retail sales (2006-2023) rise with temperature up to just over 32°C, after which the effect weakens. The effect is smaller in already-warm regions — [The Drinks Business](https://www.thedrinksbusiness.com/2026/08/how-heatwaves-affect-what-we-drink/) (2026, summarizing a UC / ETH Zurich / NC State study)
- In a June 2026 French heatwave, on-trade beer was flat (-0.3%), wine fell 25% and spirits fell 14% — [The Drinks Business](https://www.thedrinksbusiness.com/2026/08/how-heatwaves-affect-what-we-drink/) (non-US)
- A Romanian heatwave raised water, beer and ice cream sales 15-30% — [Ziarul Financiar](https://m2.zf.ro/print/4699989) (non-US)

**Lawn / irrigation / snow equipment**
- Toro FY2025 10-K: "Unusually rainy weather or severe drought conditions that result in watering bans" have hurt irrigation sales. Lower snowfall cuts landscape contractors' winter revenue and leads them to postpone spring mower purchases — [Toro 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/737758/000073775825000115/ttc-20251031.htm)
- Lower snowfall has hurt residential snow thrower sales — [Toro 10-K FY2019](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/737758/000073775819000028/ttc-10312019x10k.htm)

**Golf**
- Rule of thumb (Weather Trends International, cited in Golf Datatech/NGF monthly reports): 1 inch more rain YoY means 2.2% fewer rounds played, and 1°F warmer YoY means 0.76% more rounds — [NGF July 2026 Rounds Played Report](https://www.ngf.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/July-2026-National-Rounds-Played-Report.pdf) (2026)
- The rule is noisy. In July 2018 New England rain was up about 20% but rounds were up about 3%, and 2018 national rounds did not track the rain-implied decline — [Golf Digest](https://www.golfdigest.com/story/why-golfs-latest-participation-numbers-are-actually-better-than-they-look-no-really)

**Municipal water utilities**
- Sacramento-area districts lost $25M to mandated conservation from January to September 2015, a 12% revenue drop — [Water Online](https://www.wateronline.com/doc/conservation-posed-million-threat-to-cali-water-districts-0001) (2016)
- Some California cities were selling almost half the water they normally do — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2015/09/28/444236951/california-cities-face-hidden-downside-of-water-conservation-lost-revenue) (2015)
- Investor-owned utilities recover lost revenue through CPUC decoupling and memo accounts; public agencies face political limits — [Teodoro working paper](https://ostromworkshop.indiana.edu/pdf/seriespapers/2017fall-res/teodoro-paper.pdf) (2017); [WaterWorld](https://www.waterworld.com/water-utility-management/article/16191297/lessons-learned-from-californias-investorowned-water-utilities)

**Road salt / snow removal**
- Highway deicing is "nearly half of our annual sales" — [Compass Minerals 10-K FY2012](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001227654/000114036113008641/form10k.htm)
- FY2015 salt segment sales fell 15% to $849.0M, mainly because of mild Q4 winter weather — [Compass Minerals 10-K FY2015](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001227654/000122765416000082/cmp-20151231x10k.htm)
- Compass tracks "snow events" (days with >1 inch of snow) in 11 cities: 29 events in fiscal Q1 2022 vs a 41.9 ten-year average. Q4 holds about 28% of a season's events. The company warns that counts do not map directly to its results — [Compass Minerals 8-K 2022](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1227654/000122765422000009/cmp-2022snowdataxex991.htm)

**Airlines**
- Weather caused 74.26% of NAS system-impacting delays over 15 minutes (June 2017-May 2023, OPSNET) — [FAA Weather Delay FAQ](https://www.faa.gov/nextgen/programs/weather/faq)
- Weather's share of all delay minutes was 26.8% in 2023 and 38.7% in 2019 — [The Weather Company infographic](https://weathercompany.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/The-Weather-Company.The-impact-of-weather-on-U.S.-aviation-Data-in-action.INFOGRAPHIC_US-Downloadable_Revised-4-2.pdf) (2025)

**Wineries**
- Parametric spring-frost cover uses a daily minimum-temperature index. Descartes' example is a September 2024 -4°C frost at budburst in South Australia — [Descartes case study](https://descartesunderwriting.com/case-studies/how-australian-vineyard-strengthened-its-frost-risk-resilience-parametric-insurance)
- Niagara studies design harvest-rain and cold-winter contracts. They note weather contracts "have not seen wide adoption in agriculture in developed countries" and that unrepresentative stations create basis risk — [Journal of Wine Economics 2023](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:cup:jwecon:v:18:y:2023:i:2:p:97-121_1); [Cambridge PDF](https://core-prod.cambridgecore.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/6A20683A286BB0DD383CC1CBDC8B6B09/S1931436123000184a.pdf/potential-use-of-weather-derivatives-in-hedging-aggregate-viticulture-yields-an-analysis-of-the-niagara-region-of-canada.pdf)

### Inferences
- Golf gives the cleanest monthly-rain elasticity of any small-business segment. A course with $2M of annual green fees and about $250k in a peak month loses about $5.5k per extra inch of rain that month (2.2% x $250k). That is small, so golf is a long-tail retail customer, not an anchor.
- Salt and snow-removal exposure is large, concentrated, and already defined on a station-measurable index (snow days >1 in). That makes it the most "contract-ready" non-energy segment.
- Beverage and retail exposure is real but diffuse: a national brand's weather risk averages over hundreds of stations, which favors national HDD/CDD baskets over single-station monthly contracts.
- Hydro and wind risk is real and large, but it settles on basin streamflow or turbine output over seasons or years, not one airport's monthly rain.

### Gaps
- No US per-degree elasticity found for beer, soft drinks, ice cream or HVAC unit sales. The 32°C study's coefficients were not retrieved.
- No data found for umbrella or rain-gear sales, water park attendance, trucking weather costs, or rice vs. dryland farmer correlation.
- The primary source for the construction figures (the Asheville 51-day study, Xi et al. $40B) was not retrieved.
- No statewide total for California water utility drought revenue loss was found.
- No US dollar sensitivity for wind or solar output variability was found in this pass.

## Q2. Which segments already hedge, and what share of the weather-derivative market do they represent?

### Takeaway
Energy is the founding and dominant user of weather derivatives (CME HDD/CDD and OTC swaps), but no current sector-share breakdown is publicly available. Outside energy, adoption runs through bespoke parametric insurance: construction (Zurich, Descartes), wind (proxy revenue swaps), hydro (Swiss Re), and vineyard frost. Agriculture is reported at about 10% of end users (unverified). Rain is a small slice of the market (8.6% of contracts in 2002/03).

### Cited Findings
- The US energy sector pioneered weather derivatives in 1997-98 (Koch-Enron deal), and energy firms remain the largest users — [Energy Risk / SlideShare](https://es.slideshare.net/Akasaka/energy-risk-jul-08-weather-derivatives-ilija-murisic) (2008)
- WRMA 2010-11 survey: total market $11.8B notional, OTC $2.4B, with growth across more industries (agriculture, construction, transportation). The 2005-06 peak was about $45B — [Artemis](https://www.artemis.bm/news/weather-derivatives-market-grew-by-20-in-2010-2011-says-wrma/) (2011)
- WRMA 2002/03: rain contracts were 8.6% of the market, snow 2.1%, wind 1.6%; temperature was the rest. Temperature was still dominant in 2009 — reported in search summaries of [Risk.net](https://www.risk.net/commodities/energy/1501515/weather-derivatives-trading-increases-35-year-year) (exact attribution not confirmed; treat as approximate)
- Energy "remains the dominant participant," with interest rising from agriculture, construction, transportation and hospitality — [Business Insurance](https://www.businessinsurance.com/article/20110904/NEWS06/309049992/Weather-derivatives-evolve-as-risk-mitigation-device) (2011)
- Agriculture is about 10% of end users. A snippet gives 46% energy (falling), 23% construction and 12% agriculture, with year and market unstated — **unverified**, search snippets only
- A 2008 CME/Storm Exchange study of 205 risk managers covered energy, agriculture, retail, construction and outdoor entertainment. It found over 50% of US companies unprepared for weather risk — [Artemis](https://www.artemis.bm/news/cme-group-and-storm-exchange-weather-risk-study-released/); [Risk.net](https://www.risk.net/commodities/energy/1511231/more-50-us-companies-unprepared-weather-risk-report) (2008)
- CME weather futures settle on degree days vs 65°F across 13 US, 4 European and 1 Asian city. 2023 average daily volume was about 2K (+261% YoY) and average open interest 135.6K (+277%). September 2024 average daily volume was 1.7K and open interest 101K — [CME 2024 article](https://www.cmegroup.com/news/2024/weather-derivatives-grow-as-risks-intensify.html); [CME Q1 2024 energy report](https://www.cmegroup.com/news/2024/q1-2024-energy-update.html) (figures came via search summary; the page timed out on fetch)
- Wind: the 10-year proxy revenue swap was built by Allianz Risk Transfer, Nephila, REsurety and Altenex. The first deal was Capital Power's 178 MW Bloom Wind (Kansas, 2016), with Microsoft as buyer. Old Settler Wind was the first used to secure third-party financing. Four US deals preceded the first Australian one (2018) — [Allianz](https://commercial.allianz.com/news-and-insights/news/allianz-risk-transfer-swap-wind-farms.html); [Allianz / Apex](https://commercial.allianz.com/news-and-insights/news/allianz-risk-transfer-and-apex-clean-energy-swap.html); [Artemis](https://www.artemis.bm/news/nephila-allianz-complete-first-wind-farm-revenue-swap-in-australia/); [Artemis / Enel 295 MW](https://www.artemis.bm/blog/2019/01/08/nephila-allianz-back-wind-farm-revenue-swap-for-enel/)
- Hydro: a $450M parametric drought plus oil-price cover for Uruguay's UTE (Swiss Re, World Bank, 2014); a Guangdong Meiyan precipitation index cover up to RMB 80M (2012); a Brazil river-flow-index derivative — [Swiss Re Uruguay](https://www.swissre.com/our-business/public-sector-solutions/case-studies/uruguay-hydropower-insurance.html); [Artemis](https://www.artemis.bm/news/innovative-chinese-transaction-wins-weather-risk-management-deal-of-the-year-award); [Swiss Re drought hedge](https://corporatesolutions.swissre.com/insights/knowledge/drought_hedge_for_power_trading_company.html)
- Construction: Zurich North America's Construction Weather Parametric pays on rain, wind, snow or temperature triggers with no physical loss, covering liquidated damages, expediting costs and lost earnings — [Zurich NA](https://www.zurichna.com/industries/construction/weather-parametric); [Business Insurance](https://www.businessinsurance.com/Zurich-North-America-Construction-weather-parametric-policy-launched/)
- An $18M parametric construction-delay structure uses a day rate (including liquidated damages), a 42-day deductible, and rain, wind, heat and humidity triggers — [Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/au/news/breaking-news/parametric-construction-delay-cover-inside-an-18-million-structure-597584.aspx) (Nov 2026; Australian outlet)
- Water: California investor-owned utilities use revenue adjustment mechanisms, conservation memo accounts and sales reconciliation instead of derivatives — [WaterWorld](https://www.waterworld.com/water-utility-management/article/16191297/lessons-learned-from-californias-investorowned-water-utilities)

### Inferences
- The existing market is two-tier: liquid exchange temperature contracts used by energy firms, and bespoke parametric insurance for everyone else. Non-energy demand is proven by Zurich and Descartes construction products and the wind and hydro deals. A small platform's niche is small-ticket, standardized monthly rain and snow contracts, which neither tier serves.
- Energy firms are better treated as liquidity providers or offsetting hedgers (they can lay off winter-warm or summer-cool risk) than as customers, because they already have CME access.
- Rain is historically under 10% of weather contracts, which suggests thin natural two-sided rain flow. That is consistent with the project's own finding of thin Kalshi rain books (docs 12 and 22).

### Gaps
- No current (post-2011) WRMA sector share breakdown is publicly available. WRMA surveys appear to have stopped or gone private.
- 2025 CME full-year weather volume was not retrieved.
- No data found on whether US municipal snow budgets or salt suppliers use snow derivatives today.

## Q3. Which segments form truly negatively correlated payouts in the same location and month?

### Takeaway
The strongest same-station, same-month pairs are (1) snow-removal and salt suppliers (hurt by low snow) vs. municipalities, airlines, trucking, construction and retail (hurt by heavy snow), (2) dryland rancher (hurt by dry) vs. construction contractor or golf course (hurt by wet) on monthly rain, and (3) winter: gas utility (hurt by warm) vs. construction and golf (hurt by cold). Many intuitive pairs fail. Hydro vs. venue mismatches in space and time. Ice cream vs. gas utility peak in different seasons, so their same-month losses do not offset. Water utilities sit on both sides depending on drought rules. Irrigated vs. dryland farmers are not opposite locally.

### Cited Findings
- The low-snow side is concrete: Compass salt sales fell 15% in a mild winter, and Toro notes landscapers lose winter revenue when snow is light — [Compass 10-K FY2015](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001227654/000122765416000082/cmp-20151231x10k.htm); [Toro 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/737758/000073775825000115/ttc-20251031.htm)
- The heavy-snow side is concrete: weather drives 74% of NAS delays, and construction parametric covers include snow-accumulation triggers — [FAA](https://www.faa.gov/nextgen/programs/weather/faq); [Zurich NA](https://www.zurichna.com/industries/construction/weather-parametric)
- The wet-hurt side on monthly rain: golf loses 2.2% of rounds per extra inch; construction loses rain days — [NGF](https://www.ngf.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/July-2026-National-Rounds-Played-Report.pdf); [IRMI](https://www.irmi.com/articles/expert-commentary/construction-risk-management-innovation-harnessing-parametric-weather-insurance-for-a-volatile-climate)
- Hydro is hurt by dry conditions at basin and water-year scale (California 2011-2016) — [Pacific Institute](https://pacinst.org/?p=14883)
- Irrigation equipment is hurt by both wet weather and drought watering bans, a two-sided exposure — [Toro 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/737758/000073775825000115/ttc-20251031.htm)
- Water utilities lose revenue under drought mandates — [Water Online](https://www.wateronline.com/doc/conservation-posed-million-threat-to-cali-water-districts-0001)

### Inferences
Pair quality (my assessment, not sourced):

| Pair | Same station? | Same month? | Same index? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Salt / snow-plow contractor (low snow) vs. city DPW, airline hub, trucking, construction (high snow) | Yes | Yes (Dec-Mar) | Monthly snowfall or snow-day count | **Strong.** This is a textbook pair. Both sides are measurable at one NWS station |
| Dryland rancher (dry) vs. construction contractor (wet) | Yes, if the farm is near the station (basis risk per docs 08-09) | Yes | Monthly rain total vs. rain-day count | **Good but index mismatch.** Construction loss scales with days over 0.1 in., the rancher's with the monthly total. Correlated but not identical; a two-index book carries residual risk |
| Dryland rancher vs. golf course | Yes | Yes (Apr-Oct) | Monthly rain total | **Good.** Same index and season; golf exposure is small |
| Gas utility (warm winter) vs. construction, golf, landscaping (cold/snowy winter) | Yes | Yes (Nov-Mar) | Monthly HDD | **Good on direction, weak on demand.** The small businesses rarely hedge cold |
| Electric utility / beverage / water park (cool summer) vs. ? (hot summer) | - | Jun-Aug | Monthly CDD | **Weak.** Few small businesses are hurt by heat on net: outdoor labor productivity, dairy heat stress, and retail electricity providers on price spikes (not sourced). Ice cream vs. gas utility do not offset because they peak in different seasons |
| Hydro generator vs. outdoor venue | No (basin vs. city) | No (water year vs. day) | Different | **Poor** for a station-monthly platform |
| Wind generator vs. anyone | No NWS wind index in monthly contracts | - | - | **Poor** |
| Umbrella seller (dry) vs. venue (wet) | Yes | Yes | Rain days | Directionally perfect, economically trivial |
| Water utility vs. ? | Yes | Yes | Rain/temp | **Ambiguous.** In normal years a wet, cool month cuts sales (same side as construction, opposite to ranchers). Under a drought mandate the utility is hurt by dry, the same side as ranchers |
| Irrigated vs. dryland farmer | Partly | Yes | Rain | **Not opposite.** The irrigated farmer is insulated, not helped, by a dry month; any gain comes through crop prices at regional/national scale |

- A market maker matching opposite sides should net exposure on a shared index per station-month: "rain above X" for the wet-hurt side and "rain below Y" for the dry-hurt side. The residual (the middle band and index-shape mismatch) is the maker's risk. Snow is the cleanest case because both sides naturally reference the same snow measure.

### Gaps
- No empirical study was found that measures the correlation of payouts between, for example, construction delay costs and rancher forage losses at one station. The pairing is reasoned from loss mechanisms, not measured. The project's own station data could estimate it (rain-day counts vs. monthly totals at the same airport).
- No source was found on summer heat-hurt small businesses with quantified sensitivity.

## Q4. How do the segments rank for a small platform?

### Takeaway
On exposure size, NWS-station measurability, willingness to hedge and fit with monthly contracts, the best non-farm targets are (1) snow-removal/salt vs. municipal and logistics snow exposure, (2) construction contractors (wet side, the natural rancher counterparty), (3) golf courses and similar outdoor recreation, and (4) energy firms as the liquidity/offset layer. Hydro, wind, airlines, wineries and umbrella sellers rank low for structural reasons (wrong index, wrong timescale, or tiny exposure).

### Cited Findings
- Willingness to hedge is shown by commercial products: Zurich and Descartes construction parametric covers; Allianz/Nephila wind swaps; Swiss Re hydro deals; Descartes and Meteo Protect frost covers — [Zurich](https://www.zurichna.com/industries/construction/weather-parametric); [Allianz](https://commercial.allianz.com/news-and-insights/news/allianz-risk-transfer-swap-wind-farms.html); [Swiss Re](https://www.swissre.com/our-business/public-sector-solutions/case-studies/uruguay-hydropower-insurance.html); [Lloyd's parametric case studies](https://assets.lloyds.com/assets/pdf-lloydsparametric-case-studies-v3-part3/1/pdf-lloydsparametric-case-studies-v3-Part3.pdf)
- Station-defined indices already exist in industry: Compass Minerals' snow-event count across 11 cities; Golf Datatech's rain/temperature rule — [Compass 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1227654/000122765422000009/cmp-2022snowdataxex991.htm); [NGF](https://www.ngf.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/July-2026-National-Rounds-Played-Report.pdf)
- Over 50% of US companies were unprepared for weather risk (2008) — [Risk.net](https://www.risk.net/commodities/energy/1511231/more-50-us-companies-unprepared-weather-risk-report)

### Inferences
Ranking (1 = most attractive; my scoring from the cited evidence):

| Rank | Segment | Exposure size | Station measurability | Willingness to hedge | Monthly fit | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Snow removal / salt vs. municipal, trucking, airline snow | High (salt about half of a $0.8B segment) | High (snow days at named cities) | Medium (no derivative use found) | High (Dec-Mar months) | Two-sided flow at one station; best "matching" story |
| 2 | Construction contractors | High per project (liquidated damages) | High (daily rain at nearest station) | High (Zurich/Descartes demand) | Medium (rain-day count, project windows) | Natural wet-side counterparty to ranchers; needs a rain-day contract, not only monthly totals |
| 3 | Golf courses / outdoor recreation | Low per course, many courses | High | Low-medium (none found) | High (2.2%/inch monthly rule) | Good same-index pair with ranchers; small tickets |
| 4 | Energy utilities / marketers | Very high | High (HDD/CDD at CME cities) | Already hedged | High | Act as offset/liquidity providers, not core customers |
| 5 | Seasonal retail, beverage, HVAC | High in aggregate | Low per station (national footprint) | Low (analytics, not hedges) | Medium | Better served by multi-city baskets |
| 6 | Lawn / landscaping / irrigation | Medium | High | Low | Medium | Two-sided exposure (wet and drought) complicates the product |
| 7 | Municipal water utilities | Medium (12% revenue hit) | Medium (restrictions are policy, not weather) | Low (public procurement; IOUs decoupled) | Low (multi-year droughts) | The trigger is a mandate, not a station reading |
| 8 | Wineries | Medium per estate | Low (site frost microclimate) | Medium (frost products sold) | Low (single nights) | Daily-minimum-temperature contracts, not monthly |
| 9 | Hydro generators | High | Low (basin streamflow) | Medium (bespoke deals) | Low (water year) | Wrong index and timescale |
| 10 | Airlines / trucking | High | Medium (hub stations) | Low (operational mitigation) | Low (hourly storms) | Losses are convective/daily |
| 11 | Wind / solar | High | Low (no NWS wind/irradiance monthly contract) | Medium (10-year proxy revenue swaps) | Low | Needs specialist indices (REsurety-type) |
| 12 | Umbrella / rain gear | Very low | High | Very low | Medium | Not worth a product |

- Practical implication for AgriHedge: the most direct extension of the existing rancher book is a wet-side construction/golf product on the same airport stations. That lets the platform net dry-side rancher demand instead of relying on thin Kalshi books (doc 22). A separate winter snow book (salt/plow vs. city/logistics) is the cleanest stand-alone two-sided market.

### Gaps
- No data found on actual hedging appetite or premium budgets of small US construction firms, golf courses, or snow contractors. Survey or interview evidence would be needed.
- The revenue scale of the US snow-removal contractor industry and municipal snow budgets was not retrieved.
- Regulatory feasibility (offering these as event contracts vs. insurance) is covered by another researcher.
