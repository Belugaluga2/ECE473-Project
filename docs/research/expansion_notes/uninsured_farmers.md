# Uninsured and underinsured farmers, and whether exchange-traded weather contracts could be bridge insurance

Research date: 2026-11-19. Most sources were found by web search, and most search excerpts were not opened in full. Each number carries its source and the year of its data. Figures from secondary or aggregator sites are flagged.

## Q1. US gaps: who lacks federal crop insurance or uses little of it, NAP's limits, and ad hoc aid as de facto insurance

### Takeaway
Federal crop insurance is concentrated in row crops. About 62% of row-crop farms bought it in 2022, against 9% of specialty-crop farms, and only about 13% of all US farms took part. The fallback, NAP, is thin: it pays losses above 50% of yield at 55% of price, capped at $125k. Congress has filled the gap with nearly $40B of ad hoc aid for FY2018-FY2025. CRS notes that this aid may discourage people from buying insurance.

### Cited Findings
**Participation by farm type (ERS, data year 2022)**
- About 13% of US farms took part in federal crop insurance in 2022. Small family farms were most participants but harvested only 26% of cropland acres. — [USDA ERS Chart of Note](https://ers.usda.gov/data-products/charts-of-note/104817)
- In 2022, 62% of row-crop farms bought federal crop insurance, versus 9% of specialty-crop farms (fruits, vegetables, nursery). — [USDA ERS Chart of Note](https://ers.usda.gov/data-products/charts-of-note/104817)
- Kiwifruit and strawberries had under 15% of acres covered by either FCIP or NAP; hazelnuts had under 1%. One reason given is that many growers don't grow enough of any one crop to justify the paperwork. Some crops have no policy at all, so their growers either use NAP or go uninsured. — [USDA ERS Chart of Note](https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/charts-of-note/105971); see also [CRS IF10765, Federal Crop Insurance: Fruits, Vegetables and Specialty Crops](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IF10765.html)
- ERS found crop insurance use rising among very large farms and falling among smaller operations. The data year was not confirmed from the excerpt. — [ERS via search summary](https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/charts-of-note/109049)
- **Beginning farmers (2017 Census):** 60% participation versus 64% for all producers. The Census asks about insurance at the farm level. — [NSAC analysis of Census data](https://sustainableagriculture.net/blog/crop-insurance-access-data/)
- **Organic:** 75% of organic acres had no federal crop insurance as of 2014. This is an advocacy-source figure. — [NSAC](https://sustainableagriculture.net/?p=43001)

**Tribal producers, Hawaii and the territories**
- RMA's report to Congress says all groups, including American Indian / Native American producers, took part at more than 50% of the all-producer rate. A table in the same report shows 43% for that group, but the excerpt does not make clear what the column measures. — [RMA Report to Congress](https://old.rma.usda.gov/-/media/RMA/Publications/Report-to-Congress-8744560-RMA-final.ashx?la=en)
- A 2024 study says these producer groups used crop insurance chronically less before the 2018 Farm Bill. — [Journal of Agricultural and Applied Economics, 2024, Cambridge](https://cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/16C16F1881BEB92808F22EDB555EB183/S1074070824000294a.pdf/mitigating-structural-inequities-in-us-agricultural-risk-management.pdf)
- Almost all Native-farmed land is grazing land in RMA's Oklahoma City (Southern Plains) and Billings (Northern Plains) regions. This is an old ERS piece, about 1997. That geography overlaps PRF territory. — [ERS Agricultural Outlook, May 1997](https://webharvest.gov/peth04/20041105150816/http://www.ers.usda.gov/publications/agoutlook/may1997/ao240g.pdf)
- RMA visited Hawaii's Big Island to study whether a ranch-specific product is feasible. That implies Hawaiian ranch cover was considered inadequate. — [Civil Beat](https://www.civilbeat.org/?p=1524018); [RMA Hawaii State Profile 2024](https://www.rma.usda.gov/sites/default/files/2025-03/Hawaii-State-Profile-2024.pdf)

**NAP (Noninsured Crop Disaster Assistance Program)**
- Congress created NAP in 1994 for crops the Federal Crop Insurance Program does not insure. FSA runs it. — [USDA FSA NAP guidance](https://www.usda.gov/sites/default/files/guidance-documents/FSA.Noninsured%20Crop%20Disaster%20Assistance%20Program.pdf)
- **Basic coverage:** pays losses beyond 50% of approved yield, at 55% of the average market price. There is no premium, only a service fee. Prevented planting is covered when more than 35% of intended acres can't be planted. — [CRS R48245, Oct 2024](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R48245.html)
- **Payment caps:** $125,000 per crop year per person or entity for basic coverage, and $300,000 for buy-up, which also carries a premium. Payment eligibility requires adjusted gross income of no more than $900,000. — [CRS R48245](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R48245.html); [FSA NAP fact sheet 2023](https://dts.fsa.usda.gov/Assets/USDA-FSA-Public/usdafiles/FactSheets/2023/fsa_nap_noninsuredcropdisasterassistance_factsheet_2023.pdf)
- **Scale, 2011-2023:** approved applications ranged from 53,672 (2015) to 90,435 (2023). Inflation-adjusted indemnities ranged from about $122M (2023) to about $454M (2012). The most common covered commodities are grazed forage and specialty crops. — [CRS R48245](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R48245.html)
- **Comparison with FCIP:** FCIP's catastrophic (CAT) coverage has an actuarially fair premium that USDA subsidizes fully. — [CRS R48245](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R48245.html)

**Ad hoc disaster aid**
- Ad hoc assistance for FY2018 to FY2025 totaled almost $40 billion. CRS raises the concern that it may discourage future insurance participation, because it covers losses of farmers who chose not to insure. USDA has to write rules after each appropriation, which delays payments. — [CRS IF12101, Farm Bill Primer: Disaster Assistance, updated March 2026](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IF12101.html)
- The American Relief Act of 2025 (P.L. 118-158) provided about $30.78B in farm relief. Of that, about $20.78B was disaster aid, including $16.09B for the Supplemental Disaster Relief Program (SDRP) for crop, tree and vine losses. The other $10B was economic aid. Sources differ slightly on these totals. — [Iowa State CALT](https://www.calt.iastate.edu/post/american-relief-act-2025-provides-ad-hoc-relief-farmers-while-extending-farm-bill); [AgWeb](https://www.agweb.com/news/policy/ag-economy/breaking-down-2025-american-relief-act-what-it-means-you)
- **SDRP delivery:** USDA had paid $6.7B in SDRP for 2023-2024 losses, with nearly 60% of the authorized funds still unpaid at the time of the article. The article date was not confirmed. — [Arkansas Farm Bureau](https://arkag.arfb.com/sdrp-payments-expand-for-2023-2024-losses-while-new-disasters-emerge/); [FSA SDRP page](https://www.fsa.usda.gov/resources/disaster-recovery/20232024-supplemental-disaster-assistance)
- **Uninsured disaster losses:** AFBF estimates more than $14.1B for 2023-2024 before hurricane losses, and about $5.7B for 2025, net of crop insurance indemnities. These are AFBF estimates, not CRS figures. — [AFBF Market Intel](https://www.fb.org/market-intel/farmers-head-into-2025-with-another-farm-bill-extension-aid) (search summary)

### Inferences
- The US segments where a weather contract could be a first layer are specialty-crop and small diversified growers on NAP, organic growers, Hawaii ranchers, and tribal grazing land in the Plains. NAP's 50/55 basic coverage leaves the first roughly half of a loss uninsured, so a contract that pays on moderate shortfalls would complement it rather than duplicate it.
- Ad hoc aid works as free, delayed, uncertain insurance. A paid contract has to compete with the expectation of future bailouts, which is the same demand problem CRS describes for FCIP.
- The pasture segment overlaps the project's PRF work. Native grazing land in the Plains and Hawaiian ranches are a natural extension of the PRF Companion idea.

### Gaps
- I did not find a current count of US uninsured acres or farms by crop. ERS data runs to 2022, and the beginning-farmer figure is from the 2017 Census.
- The NAP service fee amounts and waiver rules are in the FSA fact sheet but were not verified in this session. My recollection is $325 per crop, waived for beginning, limited-resource, socially disadvantaged and veteran farmers; treat that as unverified.
- I found nothing on crop insurance availability in Puerto Rico, Guam or other territories, or on Alaska.
- I did not research PRF participation rates (insured acres as a share of pasture and rangeland). The project's own docs may already have them.
- I found no GAO report specific to NAP participation.

## Q2. International: how many smallholders are insured, and how index insurance programs performed

### Takeaway
Smallholder insurance coverage in Sub-Saharan Africa is roughly 1-3.5%, and under 20% of smallholders worldwide have any agricultural insurance (ISF, 2018). Large schemes exist only where governments subsidize heavily (India's PMFBY, Mexico's CADENA, ARC sovereign cover). Without subsidies, take-up has been "disappointingly low". Documented basis-risk failures (ARC Malawi 2016) and loads well above 50% are the recurring problems.

### Cited Findings
**How many smallholders are insured**
- About 1.3% of Africa's 51 million smallholders were insured (Hess and Hazell 2016), later updated to about 3.5%. Both are far below Asia and Latin America. — [Environment and Development Economics review, 2021, Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/6FB330C15BEE881A0358E1159DFF3199/core-reader)
- Under 20% of smallholders globally have any agricultural insurance (ISF Advisors, 2018, as cited by GSMA). Sub-Saharan Africa is about 3% (a Tetra Tech/COP26 briefing). — [GSMA, Agricultural Insurance for Smallholder Farmers, 2020](https://www.gsma.com/mobilefordevelopment/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Agricultural_Insurance_for_Smallholder_Farmers_Digital_Innovations_for_Scale.pdf); [The Insurer](https://www.theinsurer.com/?p=10153)
- An Africa Re director said fewer than 17% of African farmers are insured. That uses a broader definition and is not smallholder-specific. — [Capital FM, Nov 2025](https://capitalfm.africa/low-insurance-uptake-puts-kenyan-african-farmers-at-risk/)
- **Landscape of Microinsurance 2024:** 344M people are covered by microinsurance across 37 countries. Climate and natural perils are covered by 112 products reaching more than 42M people, mostly agriculture and property. 58% of agricultural products are subsidized, covering more than 54.5M people. — [Microinsurance Network, Landscape 2024](https://landscape.microinsurancenetwork.org/taxonomy/term/1143)
- One Acre Fund and Global Parametrics built a Water Balance Index cover for 70,000 farmers in Tanzania. — [Landscape 2024](https://landscape.microinsurancenetwork.org/taxonomy/term/1143)

**ACRE Africa / Kilimo Salama (Kenya, Rwanda, Tanzania)**
- In 2013 it insured 187,467 farmers: 67,607 in Kenya, 115,550 in Rwanda and 4,310 in Tanzania. An IFC release gave rounder and conflicting numbers (60k Kenya, 40k Rwanda). — [ACRE partner profile](https://www.indexinsuranceforum.org/sites/default/files/1505755-PartnerProfiles_ACRE.pdf); [IFC press release](https://pressroom.ifc.org/all/pages/PressDetail.aspx?ID=16935)
- More than 1.7M farmers had been insured in total across Tanzania, Rwanda and Kenya as of September 2020. — [Better Finance Better Food case study 62](https://www.blendedfinance.earth/s/Better-Finance-Better-Food-Case-study-catalogue-62.pdf)
- **Pricing and delivery:** drought cover for one acre of maize cost about $37, or 10% of harvest value. Policies and receipts arrive by SMS and payouts go by M-PESA. Premiums can start at $0.50 with top-ups from $0.10. — [ACRE partner profile](https://www.indexinsuranceforum.org/sites/default/files/1505755-PartnerProfiles_ACRE.pdf); [CGIAR CSA guide](https://csaguide.cgiar.org/csa/the-agriculture-and-climate-risk-enterprise-acre-linking-insurance-to-credit-schemes)

**IBLI (index-based livestock insurance: Kenya, Ethiopia, Zambia)**
- IBLI launched in Marsabit, Kenya, in January 2010 and expanded to Isiolo and Wajir (2013) and Garissa and Mandera (2015). — [Jensen, Barrett and Mude (Cornell)](https://barrett.dyson.cornell.edu/files/papers/150824_JensenBarrettMude_FavorableImpactsKenya.pdf)
- More than 560,000 households had been individually insured across Ethiopia, Kenya and Zambia as of December 2022. — [Long-term effects of IBLI, working paper Oct 2024](https://barrett.dyson.cornell.edu/files/papers/Long_term_effects_of_IBLI%20submitted%2026%20Oct%202024.pdf)
- More than 40% of sampled households bought IBLI at least once. — [BASIS Brief 1](https://basis.ucdavis.edu/sites/g/files/dgvnsk466/files/2017-02/BASIS-BRIEF1.pdf)
- **Basis risk:** substantial. Most of it comes from differences in livestock losses between households, not from index design error. Basis risk and spatial adverse selection depress demand. Demand also responds to price and liquidity. — [BASIS Brief 1](https://basis.ucdavis.edu/sites/g/files/dgvnsk466/files/2017-02/BASIS-BRIEF1.pdf); [Jensen, Mude and Barrett, Basis Risk and Demand for IBLI](https://basis.ucdavis.edu/sites/g/files/dgvnsk466/files/2017-04/Jensen%20Basis%20Risk%20and%20Demand%20for%20IBLI_V2.pdf)

**India: PMFBY and RWBCIS**
- PMFBY farmer enrolment rose 32%, from 3.17 crore (31.7M) in 2022-23 to 4.19 crore (41.9M) in 2024-25, the highest since launch. Cumulatively, 78.41 crore applications have been insured and about ₹1.83 lakh crore paid in claims since 2016 (mid-2025). — [PIB document, Aug 2025](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/aug/doc2025811604501.pdf); [IANS](https://ianslive.in/7841-crore-applications-insured-rs-183-lakh-crore-claims-paid-under-pmfby--20250812112404)
- RWBCIS is the weather-index arm, which settles claims against preset weather thresholds. Its state-wise data for 2020-21 to 2024-25 is in Lok Sabha answers, but I could not extract the tables. — [Lok Sabha AU2558](https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/2994304/1/AU2558_bKddi7.pdf)

**Mexico: CADENA / Agroasemex**
- CADENA began in 2003 under SAGARPA as a weather-index product for small maize farmers, using ground stations. It was underwritten by Agroasemex and private insurers with reinsurance behind them. — [World Bank brief](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/124521468287160777/pdf/881000BRI0P1300urance04Pager0Cadena.pdf); [MPRA 55455](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/55455/)
- A successor IDF/UNDP/Swiss Re "tripartite" parametric pilot enrolled 9,727 producers on 24,240 ha. Full operation in 2023 was planned, not confirmed. — [NAIC slides](https://content.naic.org:443/sites/default/files/inline-files/MXTripartiteSlides.pdf); [Swiss Re](https://www.swissre.com/our-business/public-sector-solutions/strategic-partnerships/idf-launch-tripartite-project-climate-vulnerable-farmers-mexico.html)

**African Risk Capacity (sovereign parametric cover)**
- **Malawi 2015/16:** the cover was modeled on long-cycle maize, which most farmers did not plant. The long-cycle maize survived the drought; the short-cycle maize most farmers grew did not. The model did not trigger, and member states later agreed an $8.1M payout, the amount the short-cycle model would have paid. ARC's head said national input data needed closer validation. — [PreventionWeb](https://www.preventionweb.net/quick/27705); [KfW](https://www.kfw.de/stories/umwelt/klimawandel/duerre-versicherung-globale-erwaermung/); [VOA](https://www.voanews.com/a/insurance-vital-but-no-magic-bullet-fight-drought-africa/3752576.html)
- **Senegal 2014:** both the ARC macro cover and micro cover paid out. In R4's 2016 cycle, the early window paid in all villages except one in Tambacounda. R4 (WFP and Oxfam) kept a separate basis-risk fund to compensate missed farmers. — [WFP R4 / PreventionWeb](https://www.preventionweb.net/news/wfp-insurance-initiative-triggers-payouts-farmers-affected-el-nino); [WFP R4 report](https://documents.wfp.org/stellent/groups/public/documents/newsroom/wfp281956.pdf)

**Global Index Insurance Facility (GIIF, World Bank/IFC)**
- 2016-2022: more than 4M contracts, about $2.5B in sums insured and about 70M beneficiaries. GIIF is now called the Global Inclusive Insurance Facility. — [WBG GIIF Program Assessment 2016-2022](https://government.nl/binaries/government/documenten/reports/2024/05/31/wbg-giif-program-assessment-2016-2022/WBG+GIIF+Program+Assessment+2016-2022.pdf); [GIIF Jan 2024 newsletter](https://www.indexinsuranceforum.org/sites/default/files/GIIF%20January%20Newsletter%202024%20.pdf)
- **Ticket size, Côte d'Ivoire 2023 (WFP rice drought and excess-rain index):** 5,111 farmers, each with a $160 sum insured and a $16 premium, for $71,000 in total premium. — [GIIF/WFP](https://docs.wfp.org/api/documents/WFP-0000154976/download)
- **Ticket size, Haiti 2023-24 (WFP):** 4,990 farmers, with an average sum insured of $900 and an average premium of $90. One table figure looks like a typo. — [WFP](https://docs.wfp.org/api/documents/WFP-0000146779/download)

**Loads**
- Pilots typically find mark-ups "well above 50 percent", because sparse data leads insurers to add uncertainty loadings. — [UC Davis i4 policy brief](https://i4.ucdavis.edu/node/2481)

### Inferences
- Per-farmer tickets are very small: a $16-$90 premium on a $160-$900 sum insured. Kenyan ACRE premiums start at $0.50.
- Successful programs all have three things an exchange does not supply on its own: subsidies, bundling with credit or seed, and mobile-money delivery.
- The Malawi case is the international counterpart of the team's airport-gauge finding. Index misspecification (wrong crop or wrong station) can zero out a payout in exactly the year that matters, and only an ad hoc political fix rescued it.
- A 50%+ load in microinsurance is in the same range as the team's finding that Kalshi costs about 1.8x subsidized PRF. But microinsurance loads are usually measured against actuarially fair cost, not against a subsidized benchmark.

### Gaps
- I did not find a current (2025-26) ISF or Microinsurance Network figure for smallholder agricultural index insurance alone.
- I found no 2024-25 PMFBY premium subsidy split or claim-ratio figures, and no RWBCIS enrolment numbers.
- I could not verify whether CADENA was cancelled or restructured around 2019. CADENA's hectare coverage by year was not found.
- I found nothing on ARC in Zimbabwe, or on more recent ARC payout and non-payout cases after 2016.
- I found no per-country IBLI uptake rates for Ethiopia.

## Q3. Does any program use exchange-traded or prediction-market contracts, and who can access Kalshi, CME and Polymarket weather contracts?

### Takeaway
I found no smallholder insurance program that uses exchange-traded or prediction-market weather contracts. The nearest analogues are blockchain parametric products like ACRE and Etherisc, which use oracles, not exchanges. Kalshi weather markets settle at US stations, and Kalshi restricts residents of Kenya, Ethiopia, India and 15 other African countries. CME lists 13 US cities and 4 European cities. Polymarket's daily temperature markets cover some non-US cities (London, Seoul), not farm regions or rainfall.

### Cited Findings
**Blockchain parametric cover (the nearest analogue)**
- ACRE Africa and Etherisc built a decentralized parametric crop insurance protocol on Ethereum smart contracts triggered by local weather data, with a Chainlink grant. They target up to 250,000 Kenyan smallholders, and covered 17,000-22,000 farmers by about 2022. The premium is about $0.50 at seed purchase, or paid by mobile money. — [Chainlink blog](https://blog.chain.link/chainlink-awards-grant-to-support-the-joint-venture-between-acre-africa-and-etherisc/); [Techpoint Africa, Apr 2022](https://techpoint.africa/2022/04/21/decentralised-insurance-kenya-etherisc/); [Etherisc](https://etherisc.com/)

**Kalshi access**
- Kalshi says it is available from many countries under its Member Agreement. Signup requires identity verification and a declared country of residence. — [Kalshi Help Center](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/14026044-can-i-trade-on-kalshi-from-outside-the-united-states)
- International funding is by Visa or Mastercard debit, wire (**$1,000 minimum**) or crypto. ACH, PayPal and Venmo are US-only, and some phone country codes aren't supported. — [Kalshi Help Center](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/14026044-can-i-trade-on-kalshi-from-outside-the-united-states)
- The Member Agreement, as read on Nov 15, 2026, lists 55 restricted jurisdictions; this list comes from a secondary source and was not checked against the PDF:
  - **Africa (18):** Kenya, Ethiopia, Mozambique, Zimbabwe, Mali, Burkina Faso, Niger, Côte d'Ivoire, Cameroon, DRC, Namibia, Angola, Algeria, Libya, Somalia, Sudan, South Sudan and CAR.
  - **Asia-Pacific:** India, Afghanistan, Myanmar, Laos, Thailand, China, Australia, New Zealand, Singapore and Taiwan.
  - **Americas:** Bolivia, Haiti, Nicaragua, Cuba, Venezuela and Canada.

  Mexico, Brazil, Argentina, South Africa, Pakistan and Bangladesh are not listed. Brazil, Spain and the Czech Republic ordered ISP blocks in 2026 anyway. India was added to the list recently. — [Datawallet](https://www.datawallet.com/crypto/kalshi-restricted-countries); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/kalshi-adds-india-restricted-jurisdictions?rand=6792); official list in the [Kalshi Member Agreement PDF](https://kalshi.com/docs/kalshi-member-agreement.pdf)

**CME weather futures**
- US HDD and CDD contracts pay $20 per index point. European contracts pay £20 (London) or €20 (EU cities). Tokyo contracts were ¥2,500 historically. — [CME, Overview of Weather Markets](https://www.cmegroup.com/education/lessons/overview-of-weather-markets); [CME European seasonal strip page](https://cmegroup.com/markets/weather/temperature/european-seasonal-strip-weather-heating.html)
- Listed cities: 13 US cities plus 4 European cities (Essen, London, Amsterdam and Paris). The current list should be checked against CME's Weather Product Calendar. — [CME Managing Climate Risk article](https://www.cmegroup.com/education/articles-and-reports/managing-climate-risk-with-cme-group-weather-futures-and-options); [CME 2023 weather suite expansion](https://www.cmegroup.com/news/2023/cme-group-weather-suite-expanded.html)

**Polymarket**
- Polymarket runs daily "Highest temperature in [city]" bracket markets. London resolves at the London City Airport station, and Seoul at the Korea Meteorological Administration's official reading. Single markets have traded tens of thousands of dollars, with one London market at $182.6k. These figures are from aggregator snapshots, Feb-Jul 2026. — [Struct explorer](https://explorer.struct.to/markets/highest-temperature-in-london-on-may-1-2026-25c); [Lines](https://www.lines.com/prediction-markets/science/highest-temperature-in-seoul-on-july-9-2026)
- Polymarket's own geoblock lists about 7 sanctioned territories fully blocked, plus about 33-37 countries in close-only mode, depending on the source. Several governments block it separately; India did so in May 2026 under its online gaming law, which is reported but not officially confirmed. — [Datawallet](https://www.datawallet.com/crypto/polymarket-restricted-countries); [Coinperps](https://www.coinperps.com/learn/polymarket-restricted-countries)

### Inferences
- **No geographic match.** No exchange or prediction venue found lists rain or temperature contracts on stations in Sub-Saharan African, South Asian or Latin American farm regions. Even where a farmer could legally open an account, there is no local contract to buy; the basis risk would be continental rather than the team's 30-55 miles.
- **Legal access fails in the flagship markets.** IBLI's home countries (Kenya, Ethiopia) and India (PMFBY/RWBCIS) are all Kalshi-restricted. That rules out Kalshi as retail bridge insurance in the best-studied index-insurance markets.
- **Ticket size mismatch.** The $1,000 wire minimum is about 60x the Côte d'Ivoire per-farmer premium ($16). Retail use would need an aggregator (a co-op, MFI or insurer) buying for many farmers. That points to exchange contracts as a hedge or reinsurance layer for an intermediary, not a direct farmer product.
- **Funding rails.** Card, wire and crypto rails do not reach M-PESA-style mobile money. Crypto could in principle (the Etherisc route), but that adds regulatory and volatility risk.
- **Remaining fit.** Exchange-traded contracts fit best in countries not on the restricted list with a nearby listed station: the US gap segments in Q1, and possibly European growers near the four CME cities. For the US, NAP specialty and pasture growers near Kalshi airport stations are the testable segment.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the 55-country list against the Kalshi Member Agreement PDF itself, and did not open CME's live Weather Product Calendar, so current European and Asian listings are unconfirmed.
- I did not establish whether Polymarket lists any rainfall markets; none were found.
- I found no evidence on whether other venues (Kalshi-like exchanges in Europe or Asia, crypto weather derivatives such as Arbol) list contracts for Africa or Asia. That needs a separate search.
- I did not research CFTC eligibility rules for non-US persons trading CME weather futures through a futures commission merchant, or contract margin and minimum sizes in dollars.

## Q4. Main barriers: weather station density, satellite rainfall alternatives, literacy and trust, upfront cash, mobile delivery

### Takeaway
Ground stations are the binding constraint in Africa. WMO-standard station density is about one-eighth of the recommended level, and Germany has more reporting stations than all of Africa. Satellite and blended products (Africa RiskView, CHIRPS-type) and dense networks like TAHMO are the workaround. Trust, literacy and liquidity frictions are well documented, and mobile money addresses premium collection and payout.

### Cited Findings
- Africa's WMO-standard station density is about one-eighth of the recommended level, and Germany has more reporting surface stations than the whole continent. Uganda went from about 500 stations in the 1960s to under 100 by 2001. — [Eos, Building Better Weather Networks](https://eos.org/features/building-better-weather-networks); [PBS NewsHour](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/africas-upcoming-climate-summit-highlights-urgent-need-for-better-weather-data)
- **WMO GBON:** the target spacing for surface land stations is 200 km (100 km for high density). African GBON-designated stations rose from 589 to 1,045 during 2023. SOFF has projects in 24 African countries. — [WMO, Africa Increases Designation of GBON Stations](https://public.wmo.int/node/22554)
- **TAHMO:** more than 600 stations in 20 African countries, aiming for about one station per 30 km. It says it cannot replace national weather services. — [meteoblue/TAHMO](https://business.meteoblue.com/fr/articles/how-to-build-a-network-of-weather-stations-in-africa---tahmo)
- **Effect on claims:** poor rainfall accounting means some farmers struggle to get claims paid, and they stop buying. ARC's Africa RiskView triggers from satellite data. — [WEF, Jul 2024](https://weforum.org/agenda/2024/07/early-warning-systems-parametric-insurance-climate-resilience-africa)
- **Non-price frictions (India):** lack of trust, low financial literacy, liquidity constraints and salience are major barriers. Giving households enough cash for one policy raised take-up by 140%. — [Cole et al. 2013, AEJ: Applied (Harvard DASH)](https://dash.harvard.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/7312037d-6130-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b/content)
- **Mobile delivery:** ACRE's SMS policies and M-PESA payouts, with premiums from $0.50, are the working model. — [CGIAR CSA guide](https://csaguide.cgiar.org/csa/the-agriculture-and-climate-risk-enterprise-acre-linking-insurance-to-credit-schemes)
- A review of agricultural insurance take-up in Sub-Saharan Africa covers these determinants systematically. — [IDOS / Environment and Development Economics review](https://idos-research.de/en/others-publications/article/determinants-of-and-strategies-to-improve-take-up-of-agriculture-insurance-in-sub-saharan-africa-a-review)

### Inferences
- In Africa a station-settled exchange contract would face far worse basis risk than the team's US result, because the nearest station could be 100-200 km away even under GBON targets. Satellite-settled contracts (CHIRPS or TAMSAT gridded rainfall) would be needed. No exchange I found settles on gridded satellite rainfall, but USDA PRF already uses a gridded index (NOAA CPC), which is a useful precedent.

### Gaps
- I found no source on CHIRPS or TAMSAT accuracy against gauges for index-insurance use. A targeted search is needed (for example the Funk et al. 2015 CHIRPS paper and IRI/Columbia index-insurance work).
- I found no quantitative study of financial-literacy interventions alone, beyond Cole et al.

## Q5. Willingness to pay: demand at actuarially fair prices

### Takeaway
Demand for index insurance is price-sensitive and low without subsidies. Elasticity is about -0.66 to -0.88 in India, and take-up is near zero outside the treatment group. Carter et al. (2017) conclude take-up is "disappointingly low" without large, sustained subsidies, and recommend bundling insurance with credit or inputs.

### Cited Findings
- **Cole, Giné, Tobacman, Topalova, Townsend and Vickery (2013), AEJ: Applied, India rainfall insurance:**
  - Price elasticity is -0.66 to -0.88.
  - About one-quarter of treated households bought; take-up was near zero among untreated households in the same villages.
  - Liquidity, trust and salience matter.
  - [Harvard DASH copy](https://dash.harvard.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/7312037d-6130-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b/content); [IFMR LEAD](https://ifmrlead.org/files/OWC/Barriers.pdf)
- **Karlan, Osei, Osei-Akoto and Udry (2014), QJE 129(2):597-652, northern Ghana:** randomized cash grants, rainfall-index insurance, or both, among maize farmers. — [J-PAL policy brief, 2016](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/sites/default/files/Protecting%20farmers%20from%20weather%20based%20risk_2016.pdf)
  - The commonly cited result is that insured farmers invested more, and demand was strong near fair prices but fell with price. **Unverified**: the excerpts I retrieved did not include the paper's demand figures. Check the J-PAL brief or the paper itself.
- **Carter, de Janvry, Sadoulet and Sarris (2017), "Index Insurance for Developing Country Agriculture: A Reassessment", Annual Review of Resource Economics 9:421-438:**
  - Take-up is disappointingly low without large and sustained subsidies.
  - Index insurance will likely stay expensive, so it should be paired with stress-tolerant seeds and with risk-contingent credit and savings.
  - [Annual Reviews](https://annualreviews.org/doi/abs/10.1146/annurev-resource-100516-053352); [FERDI](https://ferdi.fr/en/publications/index-insurance-for-developing-country-agriculture-a-reassessment)
- An earlier Carter et al. review (FERDI P111, 2014) found uptake generally low and unsustainable without support. — [FERDI P111](https://ferdi.fr/dl/df-kLB5hYBaUiR2Vysm2cH5DfCP/ferdi-p111-index-based-weather-insurance-for-developing-countries-a-review.pdf)
- **IBLI:** demand responds to price, liquidity, basis risk and expected herd losses. — [Jensen, Mude and Barrett](https://basis.ucdavis.edu/sites/g/files/dgvnsk466/files/2017-04/Jensen%20Basis%20Risk%20and%20Demand%20for%20IBLI_V2.pdf)
- A World Bank impact-evaluation blog discusses the "promising-ish" evidence on rainfall insurance. — [World Bank DIME blog](https://blogs.worldbank.org/impactevaluations/some-promising-ish-news-on-rainfall-insurance)

### Inferences
- If farmers mostly don't buy at actuarially fair prices, an exchange contract carrying a Kalshi-level load (the team found about 1.8x subsidized PRF) would sell even less. The realistic role for exchange contracts is a wholesale hedge or bridge for a subsidized or bundled program, for example an MFI or input supplier hedging the insured loans it bundles. That would sit in the hedge layer of the design Carter et al. favour, not be sold retail to farmers.
- A "preliminary or bridge" layer before formal insurance arrives has a precedent in ad hoc and donor-funded schemes, such as R4's basis-risk fund and ARC's political fix. Those suggest any bridge product needs a discretionary backstop for index misses.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the Karlan et al. (2014) take-up and price figures.
- I found no study measuring farmer willingness to pay for exchange-traded or prediction-market weather contracts specifically.
